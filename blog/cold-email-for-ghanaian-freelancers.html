Why Cold Email Works Better Than Upwork for Nigerian Freelancers

Let's be honest about the numbers:

  • Upwork: You bid on jobs, take 20% platform cut, earn ₦10,000–₦30,000 per project after fees. Month-to-month feast or famine. No control over pricing.
  • Cold email: You pitch directly to businesses, negotiate freely, keep 100% of earnings. One client at ₦150,000/month beats 10 small Upwork jobs.

The psychological difference matters too. When a business owner receives a thoughtful email from you (not a generic proposal), they feel valued. They're more likely to agree to longer contracts, retainer arrangements, and refer you to other business owners.

A Nigerian web developer using cold email can land 2–3 clients in 60 days earning $300–$600 USD each monthly ($180,000–$360,000 NGN at ₦600/$1 rate). That's one full-time income from part-time effort.

Step 1: Define Your Niche and Target Market

Cold email fails when you're generic. It wins when you're specific.

Instead of "I do web design," you say: "I build Shopify stores for e-commerce brands in the USA making $10K–$100K/month in revenue."

That specificity signals you understand their pain. You've done this before. You're worth the investment.

How to Pick Your Niche as a Nigerian Freelancer

  1. List what you actually do well: Graphic design, copywriting, video editing, bookkeeping, customer support, WordPress development, etc.
  2. Pick one niche: E-commerce brands, SaaS startups, digital marketing agencies, coaching businesses, etc. (not "all businesses").
  3. Choose a geography: USA, UK, Canada, or Australia first. These markets have more startups, better exchange rates, and pay faster.
  4. Identify the pain point: What specific problem do you solve? E.g., "I reduce customer support response time from 24h to 2h" or "I create product photography for drop shippers."

Example niches that work from Nigeria:

  • Bookkeeping/accounting for US e-commerce brands (they need someone 24h ahead of EST)
  • Content writing for SaaS companies (you're cheaper, reliable, English-speaking)
  • Customer support for UK digital agencies (handle their overflow, save them £300–£500/week)
  • Video editing for YouTube creators (you can turn around 10-minute edits in 48h)
  • Shopify store setup for aspiring entrepreneurs

Step 3: Write a Cold Email That Actually Converts

A bad cold email gets deleted in 2 seconds. A good one gets replied to in 5 minutes.

Here's the structure that works:

The Cold Email Template

  1. Subject line (4–7 words): "Quick question about your Shopify store" or "One thing slowing your support team" — curiosity-based, not salesy.
  2. First line (personalized): "Hi [Name], I noticed you recently launched a Shopify store for eco-friendly products..." — shows you researched them.
  3. The problem (2 sentences): State the pain point you know they have. "Most stores like yours see cart abandonment rates of 70%+, which kills revenue potential."
  4. Your solution (1–2 sentences): What you do. "I specialize in reducing cart abandonment through optimized checkout flows and post-purchase sequences."
  5. Proof (1 sentence): A specific result. "I've helped 12 brands increase conversion by 18–25% in 90 days."
  6. The ask (1 sentence): Not "hire me"—ask for a low-friction next step. "Would a 15-minute call next Tuesday work to explore if this applies to your store?"
  7. Sign-off: Name, phone number (WhatsApp), and one link—your portfolio or a relevant case study.

Real Example (Nigerian Copywriter to US SaaS Company)

Subject: Quick feedback on your landing page copy

Hi Sarah,

I was checking out your Project Management Tool and noticed your homepage focuses on features ("real-time collaboration") rather than the outcome users actually want (shipping projects faster without miscommunication).

Most SaaS companies I've worked with see 23–35% higher conversion rates when they rewrite copy around customer outcomes instead of product specs.

I help SaaS teams audit and rewrite landing pages, email sequences, and onboarding copy. Would a 15-minute call next Tuesday or Thursday work to see if this would help your conversion rate?

Best,
Chisom Okafor
+234 912 345 6789 (WhatsApp)
Portfolio: [link]

That's it. 6 sentences. Personalized. Problem-focused. Not desperate.

Key Rules for Cold Email from Nigeria

  • Keep it short: Business owners get 100+ emails daily. Yours must fit on a phone screen.
  • Use their first name: "Hi Sarah" beats "Hi Sarah Smith" or "Hi there"—feels human, not robotic.
  • Avoid "I'm a freelancer": Say "I work with SaaS companies" or "I've helped 20+ e-commerce brands." Framing matters.
  • No attachments: Don't attach your CV, portfolio PDF, or proposal. Attachments = spam folder.
  • No links except at the end: One link to portfolio or case study. Multiple links trigger spam filters on Nigerian IP addresses sending to USA inboxes.
  • Use plain text or simple HTML: Fancy templates with images get flagged as spam by Gmail/Outlook.

Step 5: Handle Objections and Close Deals

When someone replies, they're interested. Now it's about addressing their concerns.

Common objections and how to answer them:

Objection 1: "We already have someone doing this"

Response: "That's great—often having two sets of eyes helps catch things the first person misses. What's one challenge you've seen with [their current solution]? I might have a quick idea."

Objection 2: "How much does it cost?"

Response: "Pricing depends on scope. Most clients like yours invest $300–$800/month. Let's hop on a quick call so I understand your exact needs and can give you an accurate quote."

Never quote via email. Phone calls close deals. Emails don't.

Objection 3: "You're in Nigeria?"

Response: "Yes—which is actually an advantage for you. I work 6–8 hours ahead of US time, so your projects get done overnight while you sleep. Plus, your cost is lower. I've worked with [Company Name] and [Company Name] with zero issues."

Own your location. Don't apologize for it. Timezone difference = advantage.

Step 6: Move to Payment (Wise, Payoneer, Crypto)

Once a client agrees, clarify payment before starting work. As a Nigerian freelancer, you have three reliable options:

  • Wise (formerly TransferWise): Low fees (0.5–1.5%), fastest to Nigerian bank account, clients can pay direct.
  • Payoneer: More familiar to global companies, ₦5,000–₦10,000 withdrawal fee to Nigerian bank.
  • Crypto (USDT/USDC): Zero fees, instant, but requires crypto wallet (Binance, Kraken). Not all clients comfortable with this.

Push for Wise or direct bank transfer if possible. Set up your Wise account immediately if you don't have one—clients will expect to pay you within days of agreement.

Pro tip: Invoice upfront for the first month, then switch to retainer billing (1st day of month). This prevents scope creep and ensures you get paid on schedule. Nigerian banks don't wait—neither should clients.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Will cold emailing from Nigeria get my emails flagged as spam?

A: Not if you do it right. The issue isn't your Nigerian IP—it's sending 1,000 emails in one hour from a free Gmail account with no domain history. Zaram Web Mailer handles email deliverability properly: warm-up sequences, domain authentication (SPF/DKIM), and spaced-out sends. Additionally, business owners expect cold emails—they're not suspicious like spam. The key is personalization and relevance. A generic email about "I offer services" gets flagged. A specific email about their business gets replied to.

Q: How long does it take to actually earn money from cold email?

A: Realistically, 60–90 days to your first client. Week 1–4: set up, send emails, get replies. Week 5–8: convert replies to calls, close first client. Week 9–12: client onboards, you start delivering, receive first payment. Don't expect income in week 2. This is a 3-month play. But once one client lands, others follow faster because you've refined your pitch, proven the approach, and built confidence.

Q: What if I don't have a portfolio or previous clients?

A: Create a "done-for-you" portfolio immediately. Spend 20 hours building 3–5 sample projects (redesign an existing company's website, write a landing page for a fictitious brand, edit sample video, etc.). Post these on Notion, GitHub, Dribbble, or a simple Wix site. When you cold email, link to these samples. You can also say, "I'm new to direct client work but have completed [X] projects independently." Honesty beats zero credibility. And one client from cold email gives you a real case study for the next 100 pitches.

Q: Can I cold email from my personal email or do I need a business domain?

A: A business domain (e.g., hello@yourname.com) gets 30–40% better deliverability and looks more professional. But personal email (yourname@gmail.com) still works if personalization is strong. If budget is tight, start with personal email for your first 100 emails to test the message. Once you get replies and interest, set up a domain (₦3,000–₦5,000 for a year) and re-contact warm leads from your custom domain. This "follow-up from better email" actually increases reply rate.
