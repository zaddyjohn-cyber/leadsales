If you're a freelancer in Nigeria, you already know the problem: Upwork and Fiverr are oversaturated. Why Pitching Beats Bidding (and Why Nigerians Aren't Doing It)

Most Nigerian freelancers are stuck in bidding mode. You write proposals on Upwork, get undercut by someone in Bangladesh offering the same service for 30% less, and repeat. The conversion rate? Usually under 5%. The pay? Measured in dollars that barely cover your NEPA bill and data subscription.

Pitching is different. You're reaching out directly to business owners, decision-makers, and hiring managers who:

  • Have budgets already allocated (they're not price-hunting)
  • Don't know every freelancer on Earth (you're not competing with 200 others)
  • Often need work done yesterday (they're desperate, in a good way)
  • Pay in USD because that's how they operate internationally

A single cold email pitch can land you a ₦500,000 project. A bidding war on Upwork rarely breaks ₦50,000.

The bottleneck? Finding the right businesses to pitch in the first place.

2. LinkedIn Prospecting (Manual but Targeted)

LinkedIn is where Nigerian business decision-makers actually spend time. If you have a LinkedIn Premium account (it's ₦50,000/month in naira or $60 USD annually), use the search filters to find:

  • Company size: 10–500 employees (big enough to have budgets, small enough to move fast)

  • Industry: Relevant to your service (e.g., "Marketing Agencies," "E-commerce," "Tech Startups")

Then send personalized connection requests with a pitch in the message. Example:

LinkedIn conversion is slower than email (expect 2–4 week timelines) but carries more weight because it's a "warm" channel. People feel less defensive about DMs than cold emails.

Time investment: 30–45 minutes per day for 5–10 quality pitches. Results: 1–2 leads per week.

Refining Your Pitch List: Who Actually Needs Your Service

Having 8,500 leads is useless if you're pitching the wrong ones. Before you hit send, filter your list by relevance.

For example, if you're a video editor:

  • Target: E-commerce brands, digital marketing agencies, YouTube channels, event companies
  • Skip: Law firms, accounting practices, one-person consultants

If you're a copywriter:

  • Target: Marketing agencies, SaaS companies, course creators, e-commerce brands
  • Skip: Retail shops, hospitals, construction firms

A filtered list of 2,000 warm leads converts better than a bloated list of 8,500 cold ones.

Crafting a Pitch That Actually Gets Responses

Your lead list is only as good as your pitch. Here's the structure that works:

Subject Line (First Line of Defense)

Make it specific and curiosity-driven. Avoid "I want to work with you" — that's spam territory.

Examples that work:

  • "Quick question about [company name]'s video content"
  • "Helped 3 similar agencies cut editing time by 60%"
  • "Are you paying too much for copywriting? (Real data from 12 Lagos businesses)"

Body (3 Paragraphs Maximum)

Paragraph 1: Why you're reaching out (specific to them).

"I noticed [Company] just launched a new product line and your YouTube channel doesn't have product walkthroughs yet. Most brands in your space see a 40% lift in conversions when they add video content."

Paragraph 2: Proof of your work (number, case study, or credential).

"I've edited videos for 23 Nigerian e-commerce brands, cutting their production time from 3 weeks to 5 days. One client (clothing brand, 40K followers) went from 2 videos/month to 12."

Paragraph 3: The ask (simple, low-friction).

"I've got availability next month. Want me to send over a 2-minute sample edit of your recent product footage? No cost — just want to show you what's possible."

Call-to-Action

Never ask "Are you interested?" People say no out of habit. Instead, ask for something small:

  • "Reply with your phone number and best time to chat briefly?"
  • "Should I send that sample edit to this email or to someone else on your team?"
  • "What's your budget range for this project? I'll tell you upfront if I'm the right fit."

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Sending Generic Pitches

"Hi, I'm a freelancer and I do [service]." — This gets deleted. Always reference something specific about the company or a problem they likely have.

2. Not Following Up

60% of cold email deals close on the follow-up, not the first email. Send 3–4 follow-ups over 2–3 weeks. Use Zaram's automation to handle this without manual effort.

3. Pitching Too Early

If your pitch reaches out and immediately asks "Can we jump on a call?" — you're asking too much too fast. Qualify them first. Share a quick win. Then ask for the meeting.

4. Overlooking Small Businesses

Restaurants, boutique shops, construction firms, and local service businesses are actually easier to land than big corporations. They have real problems (no time, no in-house team) and real budgets. Don't sleep on them.

5. Not Tracking Results

If you send 500 emails and don't track which subject lines, which industries, or which follow-up sequences worked — you're flying blind. Keep a simple spreadsheet: Industry → Response Rate → Close Rate. Double down on what works.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is cold emailing legal in Nigeria? Will I get in trouble?

A: Cold emailing is legal in Nigeria. There's no Nigerian equivalent of GDPR or the CAN-SPAM Act (yet). However, best practice is to include an unsubscribe option in your emails and respect opt-outs immediately. Most importantly, use legitimate lead sources (like Freelance LeadsHub) instead of scraping, and send from a professional email address (not a Gmail account that looks fishy). Businesses expect sales outreach — it's normal, not illegal.

Q: How many leads should I buy to start seeing results?

A: The Starter plan (8,500 leads for ₦20,000) is enough to see results within 30 days if your pitch is solid. You don't need 30,000 leads to test the waters. Start small, filter ruthlessly, send personal pitches, and measure response rates. Once you know your conversion math, scale up.

Q: What's the difference between cold email and cold calling?

A: Cold email is asynchronous (they reply when they're ready), scalable (you can send 1,000 emails in one day), and less intrusive. Cold calling is synchronous, personal, and has higher immediate rejection. For Nigerian freelancers with limited time, cold email + Zaram automation wins. Cold calling works better if you're comfortable with rejection and have a warm introduction first.

Q: How do I know if my pitch worked or if I'm just getting lucky?

A: Track these metrics: Open Rate (% who opened your email), Reply Rate (% who replied), Meeting Rate (% of replies that became calls), Close Rate (% of meetings that became deals). If your open rate is 25%+, reply rate is 5%+, and close rate is 20%+, your pitch is working. If not, test new subject lines or body copy and measure again. Zaram and most email tools give you these numbers automatically.
