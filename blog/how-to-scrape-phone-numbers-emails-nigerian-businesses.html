Method 2: Scraping Nigerian Business Data Yourself (The Manual Way)

If you want to scrape Nigerian business contacts manually, here are the most practical methods:

Google Maps and Search

This is the slowest method, but it works.

  1. Go to Google Maps and search for businesses in your niche (e.g., "web designers in Lagos")
  2. Visit each business's profile or website
  3. Copy the phone number and look for email addresses on their website or social media
  4. Paste everything into a spreadsheet

Time cost: 30–40 hours to build a list of 500 contacts

Data quality: 40–60% of phone numbers and emails are outdated or incorrect

LinkedIn Sales Navigator

LinkedIn is a goldmine for B2B contacts in Nigeria.

  1. Use Sales Navigator (₦15,000/month) to filter by job title, company size, and location
  2. Use a tool like Dripify or LinkedHelper to extract emails and phone numbers from LinkedIn profiles
  3. Cross-reference the emails with Zaram Web Mailer to verify they're accurate before sending

Time cost: 20–30 hours for 500 contacts

Data quality: 70–80% accurate

Tool cost: LinkedIn Sales Navigator (₦15,000/month) + extraction tool (₦5,000–10,000/month)

Business Directories and Google Sheets Automation

Nigerian business directories like Yellow Pages Nigeria, Nigeria Business Directory, and Nairaland classifieds list thousands of businesses with contact info.

  1. Visit these directories and filter by category
  2. Use a browser extension like Instant Data Scraper or Web Scraper to extract names, emails, and phone numbers
  3. Export to CSV
  4. Clean the data (remove duplicates, invalid emails, etc.) using Google Sheets

Time cost: 10–15 hours for 500–1,000 contacts

Data quality: 50–70% accurate (directories are often outdated)

Tool cost: Free browser extensions

Twitter and Instagram Scraping

Many Nigerian businesses promote their services on social media. You can scrape contact info from their bios and posts.

  1. Search for relevant hashtags (#NigerianBusinesses, #LagosBusiness, #WebDesignerLagos, etc.)
  2. Visit profiles and copy their website URL, email, or phone from their bio
  3. Use tools like Octoparse or Apify to automate this at scale

Time cost: 15–25 hours for 500 contacts

Data quality: 60–75% accurate

Tool cost: Octoparse/Apify (₦10,000–15,000/month)

How to Use Scraped Data: Cold Email Strategy

Once you have your list of Nigerian business contacts (whether you scraped it or bought it), here's how to turn it into actual revenue:

Step 1: Segment Your List

Don't send the same email to everyone. Segment by:

  • Business type (e-commerce, services, B2B, etc.)
  • Company size (solo, 2–10 people, 10+ people)
  • Industry (real estate, education, health, fintech, etc.)

A personalized email gets 2–3x higher response rates than a generic one.

Step 2: Write a Cold Email That Actually Works

Here's a template that gets 8–12% response rates from Nigerian businesses:

Subject: Quick question about [Company Name]'s website

Hi [Name],

I noticed you're a [business type] in [location]. I worked with [similar company] to [specific result: increase their leads by 40%, redesign their site, etc.].

I'm not here to sell you anything—just thought you might find this useful: [link to your portfolio or case study].

If you ever need help with [your service], let me know.

Best,
[Your name]

Why this works:

  • It's short (Nigerian business owners are busy)
  • It shows credibility with proof
  • It doesn't ask for anything immediately
  • It positions you as helpful, not desperate

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Buying cheap, outdated lead lists

There are lots of ₦5,000–10,000 lead lists floating around on Facebook groups. 80% of those contacts are fake, outdated, or duplicates. You'll waste time and damage your sender reputation. Buy from reputable sources like Freelance LeadsHub.

2. Sending to everyone the same email

Generic emails get 1–2% response rates. Personalized emails get 8–12%. Spend 10 minutes segmenting and customizing your subject lines.

3. Giving up after one email

Most conversions happen on the 3rd or 4th touchpoint. Send follow-ups. Zaram Web Mailer automates this, so you don't have to.

4. Not tracking results

You need to know which angles work, which industries convert best, and which subject lines get opened. Use UTM parameters and open tracking to measure everything.

5. Not qualifying leads before reaching out

Scraping 10,000 random businesses is useless. Focus on a specific niche with a specific problem your service solves. A list of 500 real estate agents in Lagos is worth more than 5,000 random businesses.

Pro Tip: Before you scrape or buy any leads, write down exactly who your ideal client is. Age? Industry? Revenue? Location? Company size? The more specific, the higher your conversion rate. Legal and Ethical Considerations

Before you start scraping or buying contact data, know the rules:

In Nigeria: There's no strict GDPR equivalent, but the NITDA Privacy Regulations require consent before sending marketing emails. However, most Nigerian businesses are lenient about unsolicited B2B emails if they're relevant and professional.

Best practice: Only scrape or buy contact data that's publicly available (websites, business directories, LinkedIn, etc.). Don't buy hacked or stolen databases.

For cold email: Always include an unsubscribe option in your emails. Use a proper email authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) to avoid spam filters. If you're using Zaram Web Mailer, this is handled for you.
