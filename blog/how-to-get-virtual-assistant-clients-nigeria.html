Why Marketplace Platforms Aren't Working for Nigerian Virtual Assistants

Let's be straight about this: Upwork and Fiverr were never designed to help you earn enough to live on in Nigeria.

On Upwork, you're bidding against 50+ other VAs for a single job posting. The client has no reason to pick you—they'll just pick the cheapest option. After Upwork's 20% fee and currency conversion losses (USD to Naira via Wise costs 1-2%), you're losing money before you even start.

Fiverr is worse. A ₦5,000 gig ($3.40 USD) sounds reasonable until you realize:

  • Fiverr takes 20% upfront
  • Currency conversion eats another 1–2%
  • You need 40+ gigs per month just to hit ₦200,000 ($135 USD)
  • Clients expect unlimited revisions and instant responses
  • Your time is worth nothing at scale

The math doesn't work. Even if you work 8 hours a day on Fiverr, you're making ₦8,000–₦12,000 per day—less than minimum wage.

The alternative? Stop competing on price. Start selling directly to businesses that actually have a budget.

The Direct Outreach Method: Step-by-Step

Here's how to actually get these clients. This is the method that works, and it takes 2–4 weeks to land your first paid client if you execute it properly.

Step 1: Define Your Ideal Client Profile

You can't reach out to "everyone." You'll waste your time and sound desperate.

Instead, pick a specific type of business you want to work with. Examples:

  • E-commerce store owners (handling order management, customer emails, inventory)
  • Digital marketing agencies (managing client schedules, proposal follow-ups, reporting)
  • Consulting firms (scheduling calls, managing contracts, client communication)
  • Real estate agents (managing property inquiries, scheduling viewings, client follow-up)
  • SaaS founders (support email triage, customer onboarding, billing follow-ups)

Why? Because when you specialize, you:

  • Understand their specific pain points
  • Can speak their language in your pitch
  • Can charge more (they perceive specialized VAs as higher value)
  • Can stand out from general-purpose VAs

Pick one. Commit to it for the next 30 days.

Step 3: Write a Cold Email That Actually Gets Responses

A bad cold email sounds like this:

"Hello Sir/Madam, I am a virtual assistant with 5 years of experience. I can help your business with admin tasks, scheduling, email management, and more. Please let me know if you're interested. Thanks."

No one responds to this. Here's why: It's about YOU, not about THEM.

A good cold email sounds like this:

"Hi [Name], I noticed [specific thing about their business]. A lot of [business type] owners I work with struggle with [specific pain point]. I help them by [specific solution]. Would a quick call make sense? [Your name]"

The structure:

  1. Personalized observation: Show you actually looked at their business (mention a recent post, their product, a project they launched)
  2. Named pain point: "E-commerce owners tell me their inbox gets chaotic when orders spike" (not generic)
  3. Your specific solution: "I manage their customer emails, track responses, and flag urgent issues"
  4. Call to action: Ask for 15 minutes, not a sales conversation
  5. Keep it short: 4–5 sentences. They're busy.

Real example for e-commerce clients:

"Hi Chioma, I just saw you launched your new product line on Instagram—looks great. Most store owners I talk to say managing customer DMs + emails + Whatsapp orders gets overwhelming when sales pick up, and they miss messages. I help e-commerce owners like you handle all customer communication so nothing falls through the cracks. Worth 15 mins to chat? Cheers, [Your name]"

This works because it shows you understand their specific world.

Step 5: Follow Up With Phone Calls (This Is Key)

Email alone gets you maybe a 3–5% response rate if your copy is good. Phone calls get you 10–15%.

Why? Because a real human voice breaks through the noise. Businesses get 50+ emails a day. They get 2–3 actual business calls.

After you send your email, wait 2 days. Then call. Keep it short:

"Hi [Name], this is [Your name]. I sent you an email earlier about helping manage your customer communications. Did that land in your inbox? Have you got 15 minutes this week for a quick chat?"

Most will say yes or "Let me check my calendar." A few will say no. That's fine. Move to the next person.

Pro tip: Use a Nigerian phone number. If you're calling from abroad, use a service like Skype or Whatsapp to call. It's cheaper than international rates and more professional.

What to Say on the Discovery Call

You got them on the phone. Now what?

Your job isn't to sell. Your job is to understand their problem so clearly that the solution becomes obvious.

Ask these questions:

  1. "What's your biggest challenge right now with managing day-to-day tasks?" (Listen. Don't pitch yet.)
  2. "How much time does that take away from what actually grows your business?"
  3. "What have you tried to solve it so far?"
  4. "If you could hand that off to someone you trusted, what would that free you up to do?"

Then—and only then—describe what you do:

"Based on what you just told me, I think I can help. Here's how: [specific solution]. We'd start with a trial week so you can see if it's a fit. Does that sound reasonable?"

If they say yes, propose a small trial project. Not a long-term contract yet. Something like:

  • 1 week of email management at ₦15,000
  • 10 hours of customer support at ₦12,000
  • Calendar management + scheduling for 2 weeks at ₦10,000

Once they see the value, they'll renew for a full month. Once they see a full month works, they'll commit to 3 months.

That's how you go from ₦0 to ₦200,000+ per month.

How to Scale Beyond Your First Few Clients

Once you have 2–3 paying clients, you have options:

Option 1: Raise Your Rates

Don't add more clients at the same rate. Raise your rates instead.

If you were charging ₦150,000/month, increase to ₦200,000 for new clients. Your existing clients stay at the original rate (no point disrupting a working relationship). But each new client you add comes on at the higher rate.

Over 6 months, your average client rate climbs to ₦180,000–₦220,000 without working longer hours.

Option 2: Specialize and Charge Premium Rates

After your first 5 clients, you'll see patterns. Maybe all your best-paying clients are e-commerce owners. Maybe they're all willing to pay extra for Shopify management.

Become "the VA for Shopify e-commerce owners in Nigeria." Suddenly your value proposition is tighter, you can charge 30–50% more, and leads come to you through referrals.

When you're specialized, ₦400,000/month for 20 hours of work becomes realistic.

Option 3: Hire Other VAs and Become a VA Manager

Once you have 5+ clients, you can't handle them all alone. Hire 1–2 other VAs at ₦80,000–₦120,000/month, manage them, and keep the margin yourself.

You're now running a VA service business, not doing VA work. That's a different income level entirely.

The bottom line on scaling: Don't focus on getting 10 clients at ₦50,000. Focus on getting 2–3 clients at ₦200,000+. Your business scales on client value, not volume.

Common Mistakes Nigerian VAs Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Mistake 1: Not Researching Before You Email

If your email says "I noticed you have a great business" without any specific detail, they know you're mass-emailing. Delete.

Spend 90 seconds on each prospect's website or social media. Find one specific thing to mention. It transforms your response rate from 2% to 5–8%.

Mistake 2: Pitching Too Early

Your first email should NOT include your full service list, rates, or portfolio. It should ask a question and request a conversation.

The pitch happens in the call, after you understand their problem.

Mistake 3: Charging Too Little

If you charge ₦50,000/month, the client doesn't value you. If you charge ₦200,000/month and deliver results, they'll renew for years.

Price your work based on value to the client, not on how many hours you work.

Mistake 4: Not Following Up

Most people say no because they didn't see your message, not because they're not interested. Follow up 2–3 times before you give up on a lead.

This is why a tool like Zaram Web Mailer is crucial—it automates follow-ups so you don't have to.

Mistake 5: Trying to Get Clients Through Upwork/Fiverr While Running Direct Outreach

You can't do both. Pick one.

Direct outreach requires focus and consistency. If you're splitting your attention between Fiverr gigs and cold email, you'll fail at both.

My recommendation: Go all-in on direct outreach for 30 days. If it doesn't work, go back to Upwork. But I guarantee it will work if you execute properly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How many cold emails should I send per week to get results?

A: Start with 100–150 emails per week (20–30 per day). This is a manageable pace that doesn't burn you out and gives you room to follow up with phone calls. Once you see your response rate, you can scale. Most Nigerian VAs land their first client within 200–300 emails, so you're looking at 2–3 weeks of consistent outreach. Use Zaram Web Mailer to space these out automatically so you don't trigger spam filters.

Q: What if a business doesn't have a phone number in the lead list?

A: Email is fine. Phone is just a follow-up tool, not a requirement. If they don't respond to email after 2–3 attempts over 10 days, move on. There are plenty of other leads. Don't spend time trying to track down phone numbers manually—it wastes time better spent emailing other prospects.

Q: Can I get clients from referrals instead of cold email?

A: Absolutely. Referrals are easier and have higher close rates than cold email. But referrals come AFTER you have clients. You need cold email to get your first 2–3 clients. Once you do great work for them, they'll refer you to others. For more on this, read our article on how to get referrals as a Nigerian freelancer.

Q: Should I include my portfolio or CV in my cold email?

A: No. Your cold email should be short and curiosity-driven. The discovery call is where you build credibility. If they ask for a portfolio on the call, then send it. But leading with "here's my CV" signals that you're desperate, and most will just delete it.
