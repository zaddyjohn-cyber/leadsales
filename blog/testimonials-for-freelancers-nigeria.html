How to Get Testimonials as a New Freelancer in Nigeria: A Practical Guide

4 August 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

You're new to freelancing in Nigeria. You've got skills—maybe web design, copywriting, social media management, or virtual assistance. But you're stuck on the same problem every newcomer faces: nobody wants to hire you because you have no testimonials, and you can't get testimonials without clients.

This is the chicken-and-egg problem, and it's real. But here's what most new Nigerian freelancers don't understand: getting your first testimonials doesn't require waiting months on Upwork or Fiverr where you'll compete against 50 other people for a ₦5,000 job.

In this guide, I'll walk you through exactly how to get testimonials as a new freelancer in Nigeria—without the platform fees, without the low rates, and without the wait. You'll learn strategies that work in the Nigerian market, with realistic timelines and actual numbers.

Why Testimonials Matter More Than You Think

Let's be honest: testimonials are social proof. They tell a potential client that real people have paid you real money and been happy about it. In Nigeria, where trust is everything, a single strong testimonial can be worth ₦200,000+ in additional income.

Here's what research shows about testimonials:

  • 92% of people trust recommendations from other humans more than ads. In Nigeria, word-of-mouth still dominates business decisions.
  • Testimonials increase conversion rates by 30–50%. If you're trying to land clients through cold email or LinkedIn, testimonials can double your close rate.
  • A single 5-star testimonial + case study can command 2–3x your typical rate. A freelancer with a strong testimonial can charge ₦50,000–₦150,000 for work that would cost ₦15,000–₦30,000 without proof.

But here's the catch: you need to be strategic about which clients you target for testimonials. Not every early project is worth pursuing just for the social proof.

The Problem With Getting Testimonials as a New Freelancer

Before we solve it, let's name the real obstacles:

1. You Don't Have Established Clients Yet

Most new freelancers start on platforms like Upwork or Fiverr because they think that's the only way to find work. But these platforms are flooded. You'll spend weeks applying to jobs, undercutting your rates, and still not landing projects. Even if you do, the clients are often price-conscious and won't write detailed testimonials.

2. You're Competing on Price, Not Value

On Upwork, a Nigerian freelancer with no portfolio gets buried. A client sees 20 proposals at ₦3,000–₦10,000 and picks the cheapest. There's no room to build a relationship, and cheap clients rarely leave testimonials.

3. Most Early Clients Are Too Small to Matter

A testimonial from someone's side hustle or hobby project doesn't carry weight. When you land your first few clients through traditional freelance platforms, they're often personal projects, not businesses. A business owner's testimonial is worth 10x more because it shows you can deliver results that impact revenue.

4. You Don't Know How to Ask Without Looking Desperate

Asking for a testimonial feels awkward when you're new. Most freelancers either don't ask at all or ask in a way that makes clients uncomfortable.

The solution? Stop relying on platforms and start targeting real businesses directly. This is where Freelance LeadsHub changes everything.

Key insight: The fastest path to testimonials isn't Upwork or Fiverr—it's finding small-to-medium businesses in Nigeria that actually need your services right now, at prices they can afford, and with clear outcomes you can measure and showcase.

Step 2: Choose Projects That Are Perfect for Testimonials

Once you start getting inquiries, be selective. Not every client is worth your time as a new freelancer trying to build testimonials. Focus on projects where:

The Client Will See Clear Results

Good projects for testimonials:

  • Website redesign (faster load time, more leads, better conversion)
  • Social media management (more followers, better engagement, more inquiries)
  • Google Ads campaigns (measurable ROI, trackable leads)
  • Logo & branding (client feels proud, shows off the new look)
  • Content writing (SEO traffic, blog visibility)

Avoid these for now:

  • Vague projects with no clear success metrics
  • One-off small tasks (₦3,000–₦5,000)
  • Projects with extremely difficult clients (not worth the headache)
  • Long-term retainers with no upfront payment (cash flow killer)

The Client Can Actually Pay

Here's a hard truth: broke clients don't pay on time, and they definitely don't leave testimonials. Target businesses that:

  • Are making money already (have revenue, not just ideas)
  • Have been in business 2+ years
  • Can pay ₦50,000–₦200,000 upfront or in 2–3 installments

A single testimonial from a successful business owner is worth more than 5 from people running side hustles.

Step 3: Price Your Early Testimonial Projects Strategically

Here's where most freelancers mess up: they either price too low (₦10,000–₦20,000) or too high (₦300,000+) for their first clients.

The sweet spot for landing testimonial projects: ₦40,000–₦100,000

Here's why:

  • It's high enough that clients take you seriously and expect real results
  • It's low enough that small business owners can approve it without a committee meeting
  • It's high enough that you actually care about delivering perfect work
  • It's high enough that clients will write detailed testimonials (they invested real money)

Real example: A freelancer doing social media management at ₦15,000/month gets low-value clients. A freelancer charging ₦60,000 for a 30-day social media overhaul attracts business owners who care about results. That client sees 200+ new followers and 50+ qualified inquiries in 30 days, and they happily give a testimonial. Next time, you charge ₦120,000 because you have proof.

Step 4: Deliver Exceptional Work (The Foundation of Testimonials)

You can't ask for a testimonial if you didn't blow the client away. Here's what I mean:

Overdeliver on the First Project

Spend 120% of the hours you promised. Find extra value. If you're redesigning a website, also optimize the contact form. If you're doing copywriting, also create a simple email sequence. Clients remember you for going above and beyond, and they tell others about it.

Communicate Constantly

Send progress updates without being asked. Weekly check-ins. Photos, screenshots, video walkthroughs. This sounds like extra work, but Nigerian clients especially appreciate constant communication—it shows respect and professionalism.

Show Clear Before/After Results

Don't just deliver the work. Deliver a short report showing the impact:

  • "Your website now loads 3x faster" (before: 8 seconds, after: 2.5 seconds)
  • "Instagram engagement increased from 2% to 8% in 30 days" (with screenshots)
  • "You received 15 qualified inquiries from Google Ads in week 1"

Measurable results = powerful testimonials later.

Be Professional, Always

Deliver early, communicate in clean English or Pidgin (depending on the client), meet every deadline, and handle complaints gracefully. Nigerian business culture values reliability and respect. Being the freelancer who actually does what they say is rare enough to be memorable.

Step 5: Ask for the Testimonial the Right Way

Timing is everything. Ask when the client is happiest—usually 3–7 days after you've delivered and they've had time to see the results or use the product.

How to Ask (Template)

Via email or WhatsApp:

"Hi [Client Name], I'm really proud of the work we did on [project name]. Your feedback has been invaluable. As I continue helping more businesses in [industry], I'd love to include a brief testimonial from you—just a sentence or two about your experience and the results you've seen so far. Feel free to share via email, voice note, or even a quick 30-second video if you're comfortable. No pressure at all. Thanks!"

Why this works:

  • It's specific (mentions the project)
  • It's easy (says "sentence or two," not a novel)
  • It gives options (email, voice note, video—Nigerian clients like voice notes)
  • It's polite but clear ("No pressure" removes guilt)

Best Format for Testimonials

Video testimonials are 5x more powerful than written ones. A 30-second video of a client saying "This freelancer helped me get 50 new customers in 60 days, and I'm hiring them again" beats any written testimonial. But if they're not comfortable, written works fine.

What to ask them to include:

  1. Their name and business
  2. The specific problem they had before
  3. What you did
  4. The result (numbers preferred: "increased sales by 40%", "saved 10 hours per week")
  5. A recommendation (short: "I'd recommend [your name] to any business needing [service]")

Example strong testimonial:

"Before working with Chioma, my e-commerce store was getting 20 visitors per day with 0 sales. She redesigned my product pages and set up Google Shopping ads. Now I'm getting 300+ visitors daily and averaging 5–8 sales per day. She's professional, communicative, and delivers results. If you need e-commerce help, hire her."—Tunde O., Owner of TechHub Nigeria

Compare that to: "Good work, would recommend." One is usable, the other is not.

Step 6: Build Your Testimonial Portfolio (Where to Display Them)

Testimonials don't matter if nobody sees them. Here's where to put them:

1. Your Website or Portfolio

If you don't have a website yet, build one immediately. Testimonials should be prominent—on the homepage, in a dedicated "Results" section, and on service pages. A simple portfolio website for Nigerian freelancers doesn't need to be fancy. Use Wix, Webflow, or even WordPress. Cost: ₦0–₦30,000/year.

2. LinkedIn

Ask clients to write recommendations on your LinkedIn profile. This is huge for credibility. Every time someone views your profile, they see 3–5 testimonials from real businesses.

3. Your Cold Email Signature

When you're reaching out to new clients, include a small testimonial in your email signature: "Trusted by 15+ Nigerian businesses to manage their online presence."

4. WhatsApp & Email

Save 2–3 strong testimonials. When a prospect asks "Can you prove you can do the work?", reply with the testimonials immediately. Having them saved and ready cuts your response time and shows professionalism.

Pro tip: Once you have 3–5 solid testimonials, you can start raising your rates by 50–100%. Clients with proof of past results will pay ₦100,000+ for services they'd previously pay ₦40,000 for.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Should I do free work to get my first testimonials?

A: No. Absolutely not. Free work teaches clients that you're cheap and desperate. It also means they won't value the results, and they won't give you strong testimonials because they didn't invest in you. Instead, charge ₦30,000–₦50,000 for your first testimonial project. That's low enough to be approachable but high enough to be taken seriously. If a client won't pay ₦30,000 for work that will help their business, they're not worth your time.

Q: Can I use one strong testimonial over and over again?

A: Yes, for a while. One powerful testimonial is better than no testimonials. Use it on your website, in emails, and in cold pitches. But aim to get 3–5 within your first 3 months so you can show variety (different industries, different results). This shows you're not a one-trick pony.

Q: What if the client is happy but doesn't want to give a testimonial?

A: Don't push. Instead, ask why. Sometimes they're just shy or don't know what to say. Offer to write a draft testimonial and let them edit it. Other times, they're hesitant because they're worried about getting too many inquiries themselves. Respect that. Move on to the next client. One in three happy clients will give a testimonial if asked properly—that's normal.

Q: Is it better to get testimonials from big companies or small businesses?

A: Small business owners' testimonials are often MORE powerful in Nigeria because they're relatable. A testimonial from a fellow entrepreneur saying "This freelancer helped me scale to ₦500,000/month" resonates more than a corporate testimonial. However, if you can get both, even better. Quality matters more than company size.
