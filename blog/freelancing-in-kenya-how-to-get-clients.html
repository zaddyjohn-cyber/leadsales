Why Kenya? Why Not Just Target Nigeria?

Two reasons:

  1. Less competition. Most Nigerian freelancers only pitch to other Nigerians or Indian clients. Kenya is wide open. Fewer than 15% of Nigerian freelancers actively pursue Kenyan clients.

They also use payment methods like M-Pesa integration, which makes it easier to receive money directly (though Wise and Payoneer remain your best bet).

Plus, this exact strategy works for South Africa, Ghana, and Uganda too. Once you understand the system, you can scale it across 5+ African countries without much extra effort.

Part 2: Write and Send Cold Emails That Get Responses

Now you have 500 Kenyan business owners' emails. The temptation is to copy-paste one generic email and blast it to all 500. Don't.

Here's what works:

Step 1: Personalise the opening line.

Instead of "Hi [Business Name]," write something that shows you've actually looked at their website:

"Hi Sarah, I was just on KenyaBiz.co.ke and noticed you've published 3 blog posts this month. Smart move for SEO—but I'm guessing you're doing that manually, which takes time you probably don't have."

This takes 30 seconds per email (you scan their website or LinkedIn for 10 seconds, then type). It increases response rate from ~1–2% to 5–8%.

Step 2: Lead with value, not your service.

Don't open with "Hi, I'm a copywriter." Open with a problem you solve:

"Most e-commerce stores in Kenya lose 40% of visitors at checkout. I've helped 8 Kenyan stores reduce cart abandonment by rewriting their checkout flow. Would you be open to a quick audit?"

Step 3: Include a specific CTA (call-to-action).

Don't say "Let me know if you're interested." Say: "Can I send you a 5-minute case study on how I did this for [similar company]? Or would you prefer a 15-minute call Thursday or Friday?"

Specific = higher response rate.

Part 3: Follow-Up and Close the Deal

Most Nigerian freelancers send one email and give up. That's why their cold email conversion rate is 0.5%.

Smart freelancers follow up. Here's the sequence:

  1. Day 1: Send your first email (personalised, problem-focused).
  2. Day 4: Send a follow-up: "Hi Sarah, didn't hear back—just wanted to follow up. Are you still managing blog content in-house, or have you outsourced it?"
  3. Day 9: Send a second follow-up with a case study or testimonial: "One more thing—attached is a before/after from a Nairobi e-commerce store I helped. Thought you might find it useful."
  4. Day 14: Final follow-up (then remove from list): "Last check-in—if you're not interested, I totally understand. If you ever need help, my email is [your email]."

Expected result: Out of 500 emails sent with follow-ups, you'll get 15–25 replies (3–5% response rate). Out of those replies, 5–8 will turn into discovery calls. Out of those calls, 2–3 will turn into paying clients.

That's 2–3 new clients from one ₦20,000 lead list.

Reality Check: This isn't overnight money. From the day you send your first email to your first client payment, expect 4–6 weeks. Payment & Logistics: Getting Your Money Safe

Kenyan clients will ask: "How do I pay you?" You need a clear answer.

Best payment methods for Nigerian freelancers receiving from Kenya:

  • Wise (TransferWise): Fastest and cheapest for international transfers. Kenyans can send KES directly, you receive in NGN or USD. Fee: ~1–2%. Setup time: 5 minutes. Recommended.
  • Payoneer: Still reliable. Takes 2–3 days. Fee: 1.5–2%. Good backup option.
  • Bank transfer: Slow (5–7 days) but some clients prefer it. Only if you trust the client.
  • Stripe or 2Checkout: If you're selling digital products or courses, not retainer services.

Pro move: Tell Kenyan clients "I invoice monthly via Wise. Just send the payment to [your Wise email], and you'll get an official invoice." This removes friction and looks professional.

Cold Email vs. LinkedIn: Which Is Better?

Both work. Here's when to use each:

Use cold email when:

  • You have a specific list of 500+ businesses and you want to reach them all systematically.
  • Your service solves a clear, urgent problem (e.g., "Your checkout is losing you sales").
  • You don't mind a 3–7 day sales cycle (they read, think, reply).

Use LinkedIn when:

  • You're targeting specific people (e.g., "Digital marketing directors at e-commerce companies").
  • You want to build a personal brand while prospecting.
  • The sales cycle is longer (2–3 weeks) and you want to build rapport first.

Honestly? Do both. Start with cold email (faster results, easier to scale). While those emails are in motion, use LinkedIn to directly message a few high-value prospects. For more on this strategy, check out our comparison of cold DM vs. cold email for freelancers in Nigeria.

Common Mistakes That Kill Your Cold Email Campaign

Mistake #1: Sending generic emails.

If your opening line could apply to any business in that industry, it won't work. Spend 10 seconds looking at their website and reference something specific.

Mistake #2: Trying to close the sale in the first email.

Don't ask for a job. Ask for a conversation. Email #1 is about getting the reply. Email #3 is about the sale.

Mistake #3: Not following up.

50% of your responses come from follow-ups #2 and #3. If you only send one email, you're leaving money on the table.

Mistake #4: Using a generic email signature or no signature.

Add: Your name, phone number, a testimonial (if you have it), and a link to your portfolio or a sample of your work. This builds trust.

Mistake #5: Blasting emails manually instead of using a tool.

If you're copy-pasting emails to 500 people, you'll end up in the spam folder and waste 6 hours of your time. Use Zaram Web Mailer or a similar tool. It's worth the cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How long before I get my first reply from a cold email?

A: Most replies come between day 2–7 after your first email. However, some people take 2–3 weeks (they're busy, emails get buried). This is why follow-ups matter. If you send 500 emails, expect your first reply within 24–48 hours. If you get zero replies after 5 days, it's likely a problem with your email subject line or opener—test a different angle before sending your next batch.

Q: Do I need a website to land Kenyan clients?

A: No, but it helps. At minimum, you need a portfolio link or a one-page Google Site showing 2–3 examples of your work and a testimonial. If you're a copywriter, put a PDF of your best work. If you're a designer, show screenshots. Kenyan clients want proof you're not a beginner. A website ($0–₦5,000/year on Wix or Hostinger) beats zero online presence.

Q: What's the difference between cold email and LinkedIn outreach for Kenyan clients?

A: Cold email is faster (people reply within days) and scales better (you can send 500+ emails). LinkedIn is slower (2–3 week sales cycle) but builds personal rapport and feels less "salesy." Most successful freelancers use both: cold email for volume, LinkedIn for high-value targets. If you're just starting, cold email gets results faster.

Q: What if a Kenyan client can't pay via Wise?

A: Ask if they can send via bank transfer or Payoneer. If they insist on M-Pesa, you'll need a Kenyan bank account or to use a service like Remitly (more fees). In 2026, most Kenyan businesses can access Wise or bank transfers, so this is rare. If they absolutely can't pay via your preferred method, move on—it's a sign of a difficult client relationship.

Q: Should I target Kenya only or also South Africa, Ghana, and Uganda?

A: Start with Kenya only (1 niche, 1 country) to keep variables simple. Once you've landed 3 Kenyan clients and perfected your pitch, expand to South Africa or Ghana with the same cold email system. This is how you scale to ₦2M+/month without burning out.
