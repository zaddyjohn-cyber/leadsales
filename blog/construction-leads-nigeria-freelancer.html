The Challenge: How Do You Find Construction Leads in Nigeria?

You can't just Google "construction companies in Lagos" and start pitching. Here's why:

  • Most construction firms have no email listed online. Their website might be 5 years old with only a phone number and WhatsApp contact.

  • Generic pitches don't convert. Sending the same message to 100 companies gets you a 0.5–2% response rate at best.

How to Structure Your Pitch for Maximum Conversions

Before You Send: Research the Company (5 Minutes Per Email)

Your leads CSV from Freelance LeadsHub includes their website. Spend 5 minutes on each prospect's site before personalizing your email:

  • What projects are they currently promoting?
  • Is their website outdated or poorly optimized for mobile?
  • Do they have a blog or social media presence?
  • What's their team size (helps you gauge budget and decision-making process)?

This intel makes your pitch feel custom, not automated.

The Email Structure That Works

1. The Hook (First Sentence): Reference something specific about them or their industry challenge.

"Hi [Name], I noticed [Company] is handling multiple sites across Lagos and Abuja—managing that much coordination must be complex."

2. The Problem: State a challenge construction firms face (that they likely face).

"Most construction companies I've worked with struggle with one thing: getting quality leads from their website, so they rely heavily on referrals and agent relationships."

3. The Proof: Mention a result or case study (no company names needed for confidentiality).

"I helped a contractor in Lekki rebuild their site for lead generation. In 90 days, they had a 35% increase in qualified inquiries."

4. The CTA: Ask for something small (not a ₦500K commitment yet).

"Would a 15-minute call to show you what we'd do for [Company] be worth your time?"

Follow-Up Is Where Conversions Happen

Most freelancers send one email and expect a response. That's not how B2B sales works.

Follow-up sequence (automated via Zaram Web Mailer):

  • Email 1 (Day 1): Initial pitch
  • Email 2 (Day 5): "Just wanted to follow up on my previous message—did you get a chance to look at it?"
  • Email 3 (Day 12): Share a specific case study or testimonial relevant to their type of construction work
  • Email 4 (Day 20): "I'm reaching out one last time—if now's not the right time, no worries. Feel free to reach out if things change."

Data shows follow-ups convert 30–40% of your total responses. Most freelancers never send them.

Common Mistakes Nigerian Freelancers Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Mistake 1: Using a Generic Gmail Address for Cold Email

Sending from "freelancer123@gmail.com" tanks your credibility. Construction company owners expect professional communication. Use a custom domain (even a simple one like firstname@yourname.com costs ₦3,000–₦8,000/year).

If you can't afford a domain yet, use a Zoho Mail free account (zoho.com). It looks professional and is free.

Mistake 2: Not Following Up

80% of responses come after the third email. If you're only sending one email per prospect, you're leaving money on the table.

Mistake 3: Asking for Too Much Too Soon

Don't ask for ₦500K in your first email. Ask for a 15-minute call. Let the conversation naturally lead to the proposal and payment.

Mistake 4: Not Filtering Your Leads by Geography or Company Size

A micro-contractor doing small residential jobs has a ₦50K budget. A mid-size firm doing commercial projects has a ₦500K budget. Make sure your pitch matches their likely spend capacity.

When downloading from Freelance LeadsHub, filter by company size or location to ensure you're targeting the right tier.

Mistake 5: Ignoring Payment Methods

If you're getting paid in naira but you want to save in dollars (most smart freelancers do), set up your account on platforms that convert naira to dollars like Wise, Payoneer, or Grey. Lock in the exchange rate quickly—₦900/$ moves fast.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What if I don't have a portfolio of construction projects yet?

A: You don't need one. In your cold email, reference relevant experience (even if it's for other industries). For example: "I've helped 3 companies implement project management systems that reduced delays by 25%." That's specific enough without naming construction firms. As you close your first 1–2 construction clients, you can reference them in future pitches. Most freelancers starting out build their first 2–3 cases for low rates (₦100K–₦200K), then scale quickly once they have social proof.

Q: Won't construction company owners just ignore cold emails from freelancers?

A: Some will. That's expected. A 3–7% response rate is normal and profitable. The key is volume and follow-up. If you send 1,000 emails and get 30 responses, that's 30 qualified conversations. Even if only 3 convert, that's ₦600K–₦2.4M in revenue. Construction owners ignore poorly written, generic emails. They respond to specific, value-focused pitches that address real problems. Your edge is doing the research and personalizing your message—99% of freelancers don't.

Q: How long should I expect to wait between getting leads and actually getting paid?

A: Realistic timeline: 6–8 weeks to your first payment (4 weeks of emailing + follow-ups, 1–2 weeks of calls/proposals, 1–2 weeks of contract negotiation and deposit). However, once your pipeline is full (after 3–4 months), you're getting paid monthly by 2–5 active clients while continuously prospecting. By month 6–7, you'll likely have ₦500K–₦1M in monthly retainer revenue plus project fees.

Q: What if I get payment delays from construction clients?

A: Construction companies often have cash flow challenges. Protect yourself by: (1) Getting a 50% deposit upfront before starting work, (2) Delivering in phases and getting paid per phase, (3) Setting a net 7 or net 14 payment term (not net 30), (4) Using contracts that allow you to pause work if payment is 10+ days late.
