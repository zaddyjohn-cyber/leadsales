How to Get Voice Over Clients in Nigeria as a Freelancer: The Direct Outreach Method

August 9, 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

If you're a voice over artist in Nigeria, you already know the frustration: Upwork is flooded with competitors undercutting each other to ₦5,000 per gig. Fiverr takes 20% commission. Local job boards? They barely exist.

The reality is this: most Nigerian businesses that need voice overs don't post on freelance platforms. They're running ads, making YouTube videos, creating training content, and producing commercials—but they're searching for voice talent the hard way: asking friends or paying overpriced production agencies.

This guide shows you how to get voice over clients in Nigeria as a freelancer by going direct. You'll learn to identify businesses that actually need your service, pitch them cold, and land gigs paying ₦50,000–₦300,000+ per project—instead of competing for crumbs on platforms.

Why Voice Over Freelancers in Nigeria Fail to Get Clients

The Platform Trap

Upwork and Fiverr make it easy to start, but they've become traps for Nigerian voice artists. Here's why:

  • Race to the bottom: A five-minute voice over gig pays ₦3,000–₦8,000 on Upwork. That's ₦36,000–₦96,000 per 8-hour day if you land 8–10 gigs. Platforms take 20% commission anyway.
  • Invisible to algorithms: You're competing with 50,000+ voice artists globally. Without portfolio pieces and 5-star reviews, you won't rank in searches.
  • No direct relationship: Clients book a gig, get the work, leave. No repeat business. No referrals. No relationship.
  • Naira conversion headaches: Most Upwork clients pay in USD. That ₦8,000 gig becomes ₦3,500 after currency conversion losses on Payoneer or Grey.

The businesses paying real money (₦100,000+) for voice work rarely use platforms. They go direct.

The Solution: Direct Outreach

Successful voice over freelancers in Nigeria earn 3–5× more by pitching directly to businesses that need voice work. You're no longer competing on price—you're solving a real problem.

Key insight: A single corporate video voice over for a Lagos fintech company or an e-learning platform can pay ₦100,000–₦250,000. One client like this replaces 20 Upwork gigs. Direct outreach makes this possible.

Who Actually Needs Voice Over Work in Nigeria?

Before you start pitching, you need to know which businesses hire voice artists. Here are the real segments:

1. E-Learning & Training Platforms

Nigerian edtech companies, corporate training departments, and online course creators constantly need voice overs. Think:

  • Udemy course creators
  • Corporate training departments (banks, telcos, oil companies)
  • Online schools and tutorial platforms
  • Skill-building apps (coding, design, business)

Budget: ₦80,000–₦250,000 per course (5–20 minutes of voice work)

2. YouTube Channels & Content Creators

Money-making YouTube channels (finance education, lifestyle, motivational, tech reviews) need consistent voice talent. Many Nigerian YouTubers with 100K+ subscribers outsource voiceovers.

Budget: ₦20,000–₦100,000 per month (retainer) for regular uploads

3. Advertising & Marketing Agencies

Lagos ad agencies, production houses, and marketing firms produce commercials, radio ads, and brand videos. They need voice talent but don't want the overhead of hiring in-house.

Budget: ₦50,000–₦300,000 per project

4. Real Estate & Property Developers

Property companies in Lagos, Abuja, and Port Harcourt produce promotional videos, walkthroughs, and virtual tours. Voice overs add polish and professionalism.

Budget: ₦30,000–₦150,000 per project

5. Fintech & Tech Companies

Nigerian fintechs, SaaS startups, and software companies produce demo videos, app onboarding walkthroughs, and educational content. Voice work is essential for user experience.

Budget: ₦60,000–₦200,000 per project

The Step-by-Step Process to Land Voice Over Clients

Step 1: Create a Professional Portfolio (1–2 weeks)

You need samples before pitching. If you're starting from zero, create them:

  1. Record 5–7 short samples: A corporate explainer (45 seconds), an e-learning intro (30 seconds), a YouTube video intro (15 seconds), a commercial/ad read (20 seconds), a training module intro (1 minute). Aim for professional quality—clear audio, proper pacing, good mic.
  2. Host them on a simple website or portfolio link: Use Linktree, Anchor.fm, or a simple Google Drive folder. You don't need a fancy website yet.
  3. Create a one-line pitch: "Professional voice overs for eLearning, YouTube, and corporate videos—Nigerian accent, native English speaker."

Timeline: Record samples this week. Have a link ready within 5 days.

For voice work specifically, focus on:

  • Real estate developers with marketing departments

Cost-benefit: ₦20,000 once = 100+ qualified leads. If even 2–3 respond and book a ₦100,000 project, you've made back the investment 5× over in one month.

Step 3: Craft Your Cold Email

Your email must be short, specific, and show you understand their business. Here's a template:

Subject: Professional voice overs for [Product/Video Name]

Body:

Hi [Name],

I noticed you're producing [specific thing: YouTube videos, training content, commercials]. I'm a voice over artist based in Nigeria—native English speaker with experience in [your niche: eLearning, commercials, explainers].

I work with [similar company type] and can deliver professional voice work in 3–5 days at competitive rates. Here are samples: [link to portfolio].

Let me know if you'd like to discuss a project.

Best,
[Your name]
[Phone number]
[Email]

Keep it under 80 words. Be specific about what they do, not generic.

Step 5: Qualify and Quote

When someone replies, ask these questions before quoting:

  • What's the project scope? (length, format, subject matter)
  • When do they need it?
  • Who's the target audience?
  • Any specific accent, tone, or style preference?
  • Is this a one-off or ongoing?

Then quote based on complexity:

  • Simple reads (commercials, ads, YouTube intros): ₦20,000–₦50,000
  • Medium projects (explainer videos, training modules, 5–10 min): ₦50,000–₦120,000
  • Large projects (full eLearning courses, 30+ min): ₦150,000–₦400,000+
  • Ongoing retainers (monthly YouTube channel voiceovers): ₦50,000–₦150,000/month

Always quote based on the scope, not your desperation. A business with a real project will pay your rate.

Step 6: Deliver & Build Relationships

Your first project is a portfolio builder and relationship starter. Deliver on time, ask for feedback, and ask for referrals.

After the first gig, send a follow-up email in 2 weeks asking if they need ongoing voice work or if they know anyone else who does.

Reality check: 60% of voice over freelancers' repeat business comes from asking for referrals, not cold outreach.

How to Scale This From 1–2 Clients to 5+

Once you land your first two clients, scale by:

1. Ask for Testimonials & Case Studies

After delivering work, email the client: "Would you be comfortable sharing a brief testimonial about working with me? I use them when pitching to similar companies."

Add these to your portfolio or in your cold email subject line: "Voice overs trusted by [Company Name]."

2. Create Case Studies

For bigger projects, ask permission to feature the work as a case study. Example: "eLearning voice overs for TechStart Academy—delivered 45-minute course in 8 days, 98% on-time delivery."

3. Run Targeted Follow-Up Campaigns

Re-pitch to the same lead list every 6 months. Use different angles:

  • "Looking for voice talent for this season's videos?"
  • "Need consistent voice overs for your YouTube channel?"
  • "Quick question—who handles your voiceovers right now?"

4. Leverage Referrals

After every project, ask: "Do you know anyone else who could use voice work?" Most of your best clients will come from referrals, not cold email.

5. Consider Niche Depth Over Breadth

Instead of pitching to everyone, become known in one niche. Example: "The voice over person for fintech explainer videos in Nigeria." Depth = higher rates, easier sales.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Sending poor-quality audio samples: Invest in a decent USB mic (₦15,000–₦30,000). Poor audio kills pitches instantly.
  • Generic cold emails: Personalize every email. Reference their specific YouTube channel, product, or company. It takes 30 seconds and increases response rate 3×.
  • Underpricing on the first gig: You set the anchor price. Charge fair rates from day one. Never go below ₦20,000 for any project.
  • Not following up: Most sales happen on the 3rd–5th email, not the first. Use Zaram Web Mailer to automate follow-ups.
  • Staying on platforms only: Upwork and Fiverr are survival mode, not growth. Direct outreach is how you build a real business.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Do I need a studio or professional equipment to start?

A: No. A ₦25,000–₦40,000 USB condenser mic (Audio-Technica AT2020 USB equivalent), a quiet room, and free recording software like Audacity is enough to start. As you earn, reinvest in better equipment. Most businesses care about clear, professional-sounding audio—not studio-grade production.

Q: How do I handle payments from Nigerian clients? Should I use Payoneer, Grey, or bank transfer?

A: For international payments (USD), use Payoneer, Grey, or Wise—they're cheaper than bank transfers. For local Nigerian clients (NGN), ask for direct bank transfer or Flutterwave. Always get 50% upfront before starting work to protect yourself.

Q: What if businesses ask me to send samples first before paying? Should I do free work?

A: No. You have a portfolio for this reason. If they want a custom sample, it's a project—charge for it. Use your existing portfolio samples to get initial interest, then charge for custom auditions only if it's a serious client with a real budget.

Q: Can I do this part-time while working another job?

A: Yes. Cold email outreach takes 1–2 hours/week to manage. Recording and delivery fit around your schedule. Most voice over freelancers start part-time. Once you hit ₦200,000/month, you can go full-time. See our guide on freelancing vs remote jobs in Nigeria for perspective.
