Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Emailing Without Research

Don't send generic emails to every lead. Spend 30 seconds on each business's website or Google Maps listing. Mention something specific ("I noticed you recently launched your e-commerce site..."). Personalization increases reply rate by 5–10x.

Mistake 2: Poor Email Infrastructure

Sending 500+ emails from Gmail without proper setup gets you shadowbanned. Use Zaram Web Mailer to manage sends and warm up your domain properly. Test with 50 emails first before scaling to 500.

Mistake 3: Weak Call-to-Action

Don't say "Let me know if you're interested." Instead, make it specific: "I can have a free website audit to you by Friday. Here's my calendar: [link]." Friction = no replies.

Mistake 4: Giving Up Too Early

Most freelancers send 20 emails, get no replies, and quit. Reality: you need 200–500 emails minimum to see real results. Conversions compound. Stick with it for 4–6 weeks before deciding if it works.

Mistake 5: Not Having Sales Skills

Getting a meeting is half the battle. When someone replies, you need to close. Don't pitch immediately. Ask questions: "What's your biggest challenge with your current website?" Listen. Then pitch a solution. This is sales 101—get good at it or hire someone who is.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is downloading business leads from Google Maps legal in Nigeria?

A: Using Google Maps business leads for cold outreach is legal. Businesses list their info on Google Maps publicly expecting to be contacted. What's NOT legal is scraping Google's data in violation of their terms of service. The safe, legal way is using platforms like Freelance LeadsHub that source leads properly. You're buying data, not stealing it. Nigerian business law has no restrictions on cold email outreach to registered businesses—it's a standard B2B practice globally.

Q: What if leads don't have email addresses in the CSV file?

A: Most Google Maps businesses have phone numbers. If email is missing, research the business name + location on Google to find their email. Alternatively, call and ask: "Hi, I'm [Name]. I wanted to email you about something, but I can't find your email on your website. What's the best way to reach you?" This actually starts a conversation and increases your odds. You can also use free email finder tools like Hunter.io or RocketReach to find corporate emails if the lead is a registered company.

Q: How many leads should I download for my first campaign?

A: Start with the Starter Plan (8,500 leads for ₦20,000). You'll send about 500 emails from that list (don't email everyone at once—pace it over 2–3 weeks). A 500-email campaign is a good test. If you get 2–3 meetings and land 1 project, scale up. If results are weaker, adjust your email template or targeting, then try again with another 500 leads. Most freelancers should have 5+ projects lined up before scaling to 1,000+ leads/month.

Q: Will my emails land in spam if I send bulk emails to Nigeria/Africa?

A: If you send from Gmail without proper setup, yes. Nigerian ISPs and African email providers are strict about bulk sending. The solution: (1) Use Zaram Web Mailer which manages sending speed and authentication, (2) Set up SPF/DKIM records on your own email domain, (3) Warm up your domain gradually (send 20, then 50, then 100 emails over a week), (4) Avoid spam triggers (no "FREE," "$$," or suspicious links). Nigerian business owners also use email, so they'll receive and read your emails if deliverability is managed correctly.
