How to Get Accounting Software Clients in Nigeria: A Freelancer's Complete Guide

August 24, 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

If you're an accounting software specialist, bookkeeper, or business consultant in Nigeria, you already know that competing on Upwork and Fiverr is a losing game. Clients there want rock-bottom prices, and you're bidding against freelancers from India and the Philippines willing to work for ₦3,000–₦5,000 per month.

The real money—and this is not exaggeration—comes from direct outreach to Nigerian small and medium enterprises (SMEs) that need accounting software setup, training, and support. These businesses are drowning in manual spreadsheets, missing financial deadlines, and losing money to poor record-keeping. They're willing to pay ₦50,000–₦200,000+ per project or ₦15,000–₦40,000 per month for ongoing support.

This guide shows you exactly how to get accounting software clients in Nigeria by skipping the freelance platforms entirely and selling directly to the businesses that need you most.

Why Accounting Software Is a High-Ticket Niche in Nigeria

Before we dive into tactics, let's look at the numbers. According to data from the Central Bank of Nigeria and informal surveys of SME owners, approximately 78% of small businesses in Nigeria still use manual bookkeeping or basic Excel spreadsheets. That's a massive gap.

Here's what that means for your income:

  • One-time setup project: ₦80,000–₦150,000 (USD $50–$95) for software installation, data migration, and user training
  • Monthly retainer: ₦20,000–₦50,000 (USD $12–$30) per client for ongoing support, training, and troubleshooting
  • Realistic income with 5–10 recurring clients: ₦100,000–₦500,000 per month (USD $60–$300)
  • Realistic income with 15–20 recurring clients: ₦300,000–₦1,000,000+ per month (USD $180–$600+)

Most successful accounting software freelancers in Nigeria aim for a mix: 2–3 setup projects per month at ₦100,000 each, plus 10–15 small retainers at ₦25,000 each. That's ₦200,000 + ₦250,000 = ₦450,000 per month ($270 USD). Sustainable, recurring, and achievable within 6–8 months.

Key Insight: Accounting software clients are "sticky." Once you set them up and they trust you, they rarely switch. A single monthly retainer client is worth 10 quick Upwork gigs. Your goal should be to build a base of 12–15 retainer clients, not chase one-off projects.

Step 1: Choose Your Accounting Software Niche

You can't sell everything to everyone. Pick ONE software platform and become the expert:

  • Wave Accounting – Free, cloud-based, popular with micro-businesses and traders. Easiest entry point.
  • Zoho Books – ₦3,000–₦8,000 per month per business. Professional, feature-rich. Growing in Nigeria.
  • QuickBooks Online – Most expensive (₦15,000–₦30,000/month per business). Premium market. Harder to sell but higher fees for you.
  • Xero – ₦8,000–₦20,000/month per business. Strong in Lagos, Abuja. Popular with accountants and tax professionals.
  • Local Nigerian tools – Flutterwave's accounting tools, Paystack Business, MoneyBox. Emerging market. Less competition, but smaller client base.

Recommendation for most beginners: Start with Wave or Zoho. Both have excellent free trial periods, tons of YouTube tutorials, and a large Nigerian user base already asking for help.

Step 3: Set Up Your Cold Email Funnel

Once you have your lead list, the next step is sending cold emails. This is not spam—it's targeted, relevant outreach to businesses that need exactly what you offer.

Email Structure That Works

  1. Subject line: Keep it short, benefit-driven, and local. Examples:
    • "How [Business Name] Can Cut Accounting Time by 10 Hours/Week"
    • "Free 30-Minute Audit of Your Current Bookkeeping (Lagos-Based)"
    • "Wondering Why Your Monthly Reconciliation Takes 3 Days?"
  2. Body: 3–4 sentences max. Mention a specific pain point, your solution, and a soft call-to-action (CTA).
    • Example: "Hi [Name], I noticed [Business Name] is growing fast. Most businesses at your stage spend 20+ hours monthly on spreadsheets and manual bookkeeping. I help retail and logistics companies set up cloud accounting software (Wave, Zoho Books) so they save time and never miss tax deadlines. Would a quick 15-min call to discuss your current process be useful? Available Tuesday or Thursday this week."
  3. Signature: Include your phone number, WhatsApp link, and email. Nigerians prefer quick contact, so make it easy.

Step 4: Create a Simple Sales Framework

When someone replies or picks up the phone, don't go into a long pitch. Use this framework:

  1. Diagnostic call (15 minutes): Ask about their current bookkeeping process, pain points, number of transactions monthly, team size, and budget awareness. Listen more than you talk.
  2. Proposal: Send a one-page proposal within 24 hours outlining the software you recommend, the setup timeline (usually 3–5 days), training scope, and cost. Include a success metric: "After implementation, you'll spend 70% less time on monthly reconciliation."
  3. Close: Follow up within 48 hours with a simple question: "Do you want to move forward, or do you have questions?" This isn't pushy—it's clear.

Step 5: Pricing and Payment Structure in Nigeria

Price in Naira. Never quote in dollars to small Nigerian businesses—it confuses them and adds friction.

Recommended pricing structure:

  • Setup + 3 months training: ₦100,000–₦150,000 (includes software configuration, data import, team training, 30 days support)
  • Monthly retainer (ongoing): ₦20,000–₦40,000 depending on transaction volume and complexity
  • Custom training (per session): ₦10,000–₦15,000 for 1-hour sessions beyond the initial training

Always include a "money-back" guarantee in the first 7 days if they're not satisfied. This removes risk and shows confidence.

Collecting payment: Use Payoneer, Wise, or Grey (if they have a naira bank account). You can also accept bank transfers directly to a Zenith, Access, or GTBank account—many older business owners prefer this. If taking international payments, Wise is fastest (₦980–₦1,020 per USD at current rates) and safest.

Step 6: Build Social Proof and Credibility Fast

Nigerian business owners trust case studies and testimonials. After your first 2–3 clients, create simple one-pagers showing:

  • Before/after screenshots (accounting software comparison)
  • Time saved per month (quantified)
  • Client name and business type (with permission)
  • A short quote from the owner

Share these on WhatsApp, LinkedIn, and in follow-up emails. "See how [Business Name], a logistics company in Lagos, cut their accounting time from 15 hours to 3 hours per month" is far more convincing than any pitch.

Quick Win: After your first retainer client, ask them for a 30-second WhatsApp voice message saying what they liked about your service. This video testimonial is worth 10 paragraphs of text. Use it in all future pitches.

Step 7: Scaling Beyond Cold Email

Once you've landed your first 3–4 clients, you can diversify your outreach:

  • LinkedIn: Connect with business owners and CFOs, share tips on accounting efficiency. Drop one or two clients' success stories monthly.
  • Referrals: Offer ₦5,000–₦10,000 per referral to existing clients who introduce you to their business peers. This is Nigeria's best word-of-mouth strategy.
  • Google Local (GMB): Create a simple business listing as "Accounting Software Consultant, Lagos" and wait for inbound inquiries.
  • WhatsApp Business: Get a WhatsApp Business number, add a link to your service on your GMB profile, and reply within 5 minutes. This converts.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Trying to sell to everyone: "We do all accounting software" sounds weak. "I specialize in Zoho Books for retail businesses" sounds like an expert.
  • Forgetting the phone call: Nigerians buy from people, not emails. Always move a lead to a call within 2–3 email exchanges.
  • Underpricing: Charge ₦20,000–₦40,000 for retainers, not ₦5,000. Low price signals low quality to Nigerian business owners.
  • No follow-up sequences: If you only send one email, you'll get 1–2% response. With 5 spaced-out follow-ups, you'll get 8–12%.
  • Competing on Upwork still: Honestly, just stop. The money is in direct sales. Upwork takes 20%, clients are cheap, and you have zero leverage.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What if I don't have certification in accounting software?

A: You don't need formal certification, but you should be comfortable with the software's basics and have set up at least one test account. Most accounting platforms (Wave, Zoho Books, Xero) have free certifications you can complete in 1–2 weeks online. Spend 20 hours learning the software, do 2–3 test setups, then start selling. Your real learning will come from client work. Nigerian business owners care about results, not certificates.

Q: Should I target big companies or small businesses?

A: Target businesses with 3–50 employees. Companies with 1–2 people have no budget. Companies with 100+ employees already have accountants and complex ERP systems. The sweet spot is growing SMEs doing ₦10–₦100 million in annual revenue. They have cash flow problems, hate spreadsheets, and will pay for a solution that saves time.

Q: How do I handle objections like "Our accountant already does this" or "We don't have budget"?

A: Objection 1: "That's great. Most accountants handle tax filing, but they're not optimizing your daily operations. I help your team automate invoicing, expense tracking, and payroll—things that free up 15+ hours monthly. Even if your accountant helps, would extra visibility into daily cash flow be valuable?" Objection 2: "I understand. Most of my clients started saying the same thing. Here's what changed their mind: the time they save is worth ₦400–₦500 per day. So a ₦25,000 monthly retainer pays for itself in 50 hours. Can I show you how it works with your numbers?"

Q: What's the best way to follow up without seeming desperate?

A: Send follow-ups at 5 days, 10 days, 15 days, and 25 days after the initial email. Each follow-up should be short (2 sentences) and add new value: "Thought of you because of [industry trend]..." or "Quick question: Are you still managing invoices manually or have you found a solution?" Never say "Following up on my previous email." Make each email feel like a new conversation.
