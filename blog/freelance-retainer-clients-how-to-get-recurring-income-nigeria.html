How to Get Retainer Clients for Recurring Income: A Nigerian Freelancer's Guide

2026-08-11 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

If you're a freelancer in Nigeria, you already know the truth: project-based work is unstable. One month you're drowning in Upwork gigs at ₦15,000–₦30,000 per project. The next month, silence. You're always hunting for the next client, always competing on price, always stressed about next month's Internet bill and NEPA payments.

Retainer clients solve this. A retainer is a business that pays you a fixed fee (usually monthly) to handle ongoing work—social media management, content writing, bookkeeping, design, whatever your skill is. Instead of ₦50,000 one week and nothing the next, you get ₦100,000–₦300,000 reliably every month from 2–4 retainer clients. That's recurring income. That's predictable. That's what transforms freelancing from a side hustle into a real business.

This guide shows you exactly how to find, pitch, and close retainer clients as a Nigerian freelancer—with realistic numbers, timelines, and the tools that actually work.

Why Retainer Clients Change Everything for Nigerian Freelancers

Let's do the math. A typical Nigerian freelancer on Upwork or Fiverr earns:

  • ₦15,000–₦50,000 per small project (logo design, article writing, basic social media post)
  • ₦50,000–₦200,000 per medium project (website, brand identity, 30 social posts)
  • ₦200,000+ per large project (full redesign, course creation, long-term campaign)

The problem? Projects end. You celebrate finishing a ₦80,000 gig, then spend two weeks with zero income looking for the next one. You're not earning during that dead time. You're also taking 5% Upwork fee, 3–4% payment processing fee on Payoneer/Wise, and tax headaches.

With retainers, you flip the script:

  • Predictable income: ₦120,000–₦500,000 per month from just 2–4 clients, no dead periods
  • Reduced fees: Direct payment (Wise transfer at 0.8% fee, no platform middleman)
  • Higher hourly value: A ₦150,000/month retainer for 20 hours/week = ₦1,875/hour vs. ₦500/hour on Upwork
  • Time to build: Once you have 3 retainers locked in, you work 60 hours/week for ₦450,000+/month. That's ₦1.8M+ in four weeks. In USD at 1,400 NGN/USD, that's $1,285 monthly from retainers alone
  • Predictability for growth: With stable income, you can invest in tools, upskilling, hiring a VA, or scaling—instead of scrambling

Real numbers: A Nigerian social media manager with 3 retainer clients at ₦150,000 each per month earns ₦450,000 (≈$321 USD) monthly—with zero job hunting, zero Upwork competition, zero algorithm stress. Compare that to averaging ₦100,000/month across 8 platform gigs with no security.

The Five Steps to Land Your First Retainer Client

Step 1: Narrow Your Niche (This is Non-Negotiable)

You cannot pitch "I do everything." Retainer clients—especially Nigerian businesses willing to pay—want specialists. They want someone who knows their industry.

Choose one niche from this proven list for Nigeria:

  • Social media management: For e-commerce stores, real estate agencies, beauty brands, fitness centers
  • Content writing: For SaaS companies, financial institutions, EdTech platforms, blogs
  • Email marketing: For e-commerce, online courses, subscription services
  • Bookkeeping/accounting: For small businesses, online shops, startups (high-value, recurring)
  • SEO/Google Ads: For agencies, clinics, law firms, e-commerce stores
  • Graphic design: For agencies, real estate, e-commerce, event companies
  • Website maintenance: For agencies, small businesses, freelancers

If you're unsure which niche fits you, read our guide on best niches for freelancers in Nigeria—it includes market demand and earning potential by niche.

Your next move: Pick ONE niche. Commit to it for 90 days. Build your messaging, portfolio, and case studies around it. You'll close deals 5x faster than generalists.

Step 2: Build a Portfolio (Even If It's Not Perfect)

Retainer clients want proof you can do the work consistently. You don't need 20 projects; 2–3 strong ones in your niche do the job.

If you're starting from zero:

  1. Do 1–2 projects at a discount or free for past clients, friends, or small businesses you know. Make sure the results are measurable (e.g., "grew Instagram followers from 500 to 2,000 in 30 days").
  2. Document everything: before/after screenshots, metrics, testimonials.
  3. Create a simple portfolio (Google Drive shared folder, Notion page, or a basic WordPress site) with 2–3 case studies showing your process and results.

The portfolio must answer: "What did I do? What were the results? What would you expect working with me?"

Step 4: Craft a Retainer-Specific Cold Email

Your email isn't a generic "I'm available" message. It's a proposal that shows you understand their business and have a specific, valuable offer.

Structure:

  1. Hook (1–2 lines): Show you know them. "I noticed your Instagram has 3K followers but you're posting inconsistently—I help brands like [similar company] grow engagement by 40–60% with a consistent posting schedule."
  2. Problem (2–3 lines): Name their pain. "Most beauty brands in Nigeria struggle with Instagram consistency because they're juggling inventory, sales, and customer service—no time for social."
  3. Solution (2–3 lines): Your offer. "I offer a monthly retainer where I manage your Instagram: 4 posts/week, caption writing, community replies, and a monthly performance report. ₦80,000/month."
  4. Proof (1–2 lines): A case study or credential. "I grew @[brand]'s engagement from 2% to 8% in 60 days with a similar approach. Here's the case study: [link]"
  5. CTA (1 line): Clear ask. "Can we chat Thursday or Friday for 15 mins? I'll show you what a month looks like."

Example email:

Hi [Owner Name],

I noticed [Brand Name] has 3,200 followers on Instagram but you're posting once every 10 days. That's leaving money on the table—engagement drops when you go silent.

I work with beauty and wellness brands in Lagos, helping them hit 4–5 posts/week without the stress. Most of my clients see 30–50% engagement growth within 90 days.

I offer a retainer: ₦85,000/month for consistent posting, captions, and community management. Case study here: [link]

Free 15-min call this week?

[Your Name]

Step 5: Follow Up, Close, and Over-Deliver

Most cold emails get no response. That's normal. Expect a 2–5% reply rate, which means from 500 emails, 10–25 people will reply. Of those, 30–50% will book a call. Of calls, 20–30% close. So 500 emails → roughly 1–2 retainer clients.

Your follow-up sequence:

  • Email 1 (Day 1): Initial pitch (see example above)
  • Email 2 (Day 5): Short follow-up: "Hi [Name], checking if you got my last email about helping [Brand] with Instagram. Worth a chat?"
  • Email 3 (Day 10): Value-add follow-up: "One more thing—I wrote a quick guide on how beauty brands in Nigeria can grow from 2K to 10K followers without ads. Thought you might find it useful. [link]"
  • Email 4 (Day 14): Final: "Last one—open to a quick call sometime?"

When someone replies or books a call:

  • Qualify: Is their budget aligned? (Minimum ₦70,000/month for most services.) Do they need what you offer?
  • Pitch: Show your retainer package: "For ₦100,000/month, I handle [specific tasks]. Expected outcome: [metrics]. Start date: [date]. Contract: [link]"
  • Close: Get a signed agreement or email confirmation of terms, payment method, and start date.
  • Over-deliver first month: Go 20% above what you promised. Document results. This client will renew and refer.

Structuring Your Retainer Offer for Maximum Closes

Retainer clients want clarity. Vague offers don't close. Here's what works:

Package Structure

Example: Social Media Management Retainer

  • Tier 1 (₦80,000/month): 3 Instagram posts/week, captions, weekly community engagement (replies/comments), 1 monthly report
  • Tier 2 (₦150,000/month): 4 posts/week across Instagram + Facebook, caption writing, daily community engagement, weekly analytics call, monthly growth strategy
  • Tier 3 (₦250,000/month): Everything in Tier 2 + TikTok content, paid ad management (₦50K/month budget included), content calendar planning, competitor analysis

Why tiers work: Clients self-select. A small shop picks Tier 1. A growing e-commerce store picks Tier 2. An ambitious brand picks Tier 3. You close faster because options reduce friction.

Payment Terms

  • Monthly retainer: Payment on the 1st of each month (or before work starts)
  • Payment method: Wise transfer (0.8% fee, instant), bank transfer, or Payoneer (for clients paying from abroad)
  • Minimum commitment: 3 months (protects both of you; gives time to show results)
  • Billing: Send an invoice the day before payment is due. Set up auto-reminders.

Contract

Don't overcomplicate it. A one-page Google Docs agreement with these sections is enough:

  • Services included (exactly what you'll do)
  • Deliverables and timeline
  • Monthly fee and payment date
  • Minimum term (usually 3 months)
  • Cancellation notice (30 days notice to cancel)
  • Revision/scope limits (e.g., "unlimited revisions within scope; extra projects cost ₦X")

Common Obstacles and How to Beat Them

Obstacle 1: "Nobody Replies to My Cold Emails"

Real reasons: Your subject line sucks, your email is too long, you're pitching the wrong businesses, or your email is landing in spam.

Fix:

  • Subject line should reference their business: "Instagram growth for [Brand Name]" not "Let's work together"
  • Keep email to 50–80 words max (see example earlier)
  • Verify email addresses are real (use an email validation tool before sending)
  • Send from a professional domain email if possible (not Gmail, especially for B2B)
  • Use Zaram Web Mailer to avoid spam filters; test your emails first on one small batch

Obstacle 2: "Clients Want Lower Prices"

Real issue: You're pitching the wrong businesses or positioning yourself as a commodity.

Fix:

  • Target wealthier niches: e-commerce stores (not small shops), agencies (not solopreneurs), companies (not students)
  • Show ROI, not just effort: "This will add ₦500K/month to your revenue" beats "I'll post 4 times/week"
  • Start with your best-fit clients (higher confidence in your work = better pitches)
  • Don't negotiate price; negotiate scope instead: "Can't do ₦80K? How about ₦100K for 3 months, no commitment?"

Obstacle 3: "I Don't Have Time to Send Cold Emails"

Real issue: You're overwhelmed or you're still taking small platform gigs.

Fix:

  • Set a 30-min block each day just for outreach (send 20–30 emails, schedule follow-ups)
  • Automate with Zaram Web Mailer; you only need to check replies once daily
  • Cut low-paying platform work immediately; replace with retainer hunting (even if income dips for 30 days, the payoff is huge)

Obstacle 4: "How Do I Get My First Client Without Experience?"

Real issue: You're waiting for perfection before starting.

Fix:

  • Do 2 pro bono or discounted projects NOW (offer ₦30,000 instead of ₦100,000)
  • Focus on measurable results (you grew their followers, their website traffic, their email list—whatever you can prove)
  • Pitch as an "emerging specialist" not a newbie: "I specialize in [niche] and I'm offering an introductory rate of ₦100K/month (instead of my normal ₦150K) for the first 3 months—this is usually for new clients willing to be case studies"
  • This positions you as intentional, not desperate

Pro Tips From Successful Nigerian Retainer Freelancers

  • Start with one retainer. Don't aim for three at once. One successful client gives you proof, testimonial, and breathing room to land the next.
  • Pick clients you actually like. You'll talk to them weekly for months. Choose a niche or client type that doesn't drain you.
  • Automate everything. Recurring work should have templates, systems, and tools (Buffer for scheduling posts, Zapier for data entry, etc.). This frees you to sell.
  • Raise rates every 6–12 months. As you get better and prove ROI, increase your retainer by ₦15,000–₦30,000. Long-term clients usually accept (and loyal ones become brand ambassadors).
  • Invest profits in a VA or second freelancer. Once you have ₦400K/month in retainers, hire someone to handle delivery (even part-time). Now you scale without burning out.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How much should I charge for a retainer as a Nigerian freelancer?

A: Minimum ₦70,000/month for most services. The real benchmark is your niche and target client. E-commerce stores and agencies can afford ₦120,000–₦300,000/month for quality social media, content, or design retainers. Small local shops might only budget ₦50,000–₦80,000. Start by researching what similar freelancers charge in your niche, then price 10–20% higher (you're better than most). As you land clients and build testimonials, raise rates. A freelancer with 3 strong retainer clients can charge 2–3x more than someone with zero.

Q: How long does it usually take to land a retainer client using cold email?

A: Realistically, 3–6 weeks from your first email to a signed contract. You'll send 100–150 emails, get 3–8 replies, book 1–2 calls, and close 1 client. The timeline depends on your email quality, niche, and follow-up consistency. Niches like social media management and content writing close faster (2–4 weeks) than specialized services like SEO or bookkeeping (4–8 weeks). Cold email is a numbers game—more emails, more closes.

Q: What if a retainer client wants to cancel after one month?

A: Include a 30-day cancellation notice clause in your contract. If they cancel, require 30 days notice (so you can find replacement work). For early cancellation, charge a small penalty (e.g., 50% of one month's retainer). Most clients who see results stay; those who don't will leave anyway. Focus on over-delivering in month one to prove value. If a client does cancel, don't take it personally—use their feedback to improve and move to the next prospect.

Q: Can I manage multiple retainer clients without hiring help?

A: Yes, up to 4–5 clients depending on the service. A social media manager can handle 3–5 clients (20 hours/week total if using templates and scheduling tools). A content writer can handle 4–6 clients (writing 10–15 pieces/month per client). Once you hit 5+ clients or 50+ hours/week, hire a VA or junior freelancer to handle delivery. This scales your income without burning you out. A VA might cost ₦30,000–₦100,000/month depending on skills, but they free you to sell and land more clients (which pays for itself immediately).
