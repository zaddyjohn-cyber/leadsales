Why Real Estate Businesses Need Freelancers (And Pay Well)

Real estate is one of Nigeria's fastest-growing sectors. The market is estimated at over $100 billion USD, with Lagos alone accounting for tens of billions. Property developers, estate managers, real estate agents, and property tech platforms are all competing fiercely for clients.

Here's what they need:

  • Website design and development – Most estate agents still have terrible websites or none at all. A professional, mobile-responsive property listing website can close deals.
  • Content marketing and copywriting – They need product descriptions, landing pages, email sequences, and blog posts that convert property browsers into buyers.
  • Social media management – Instagram, Facebook, and TikTok showcase properties visually. Real estate businesses need daily posting, engagement, and content strategy.
  • Video production and editing – Virtual property tours, testimonial videos, and promotional reels are now expected by serious buyers.
  • Email marketing automation – Nurturing leads through email sequences until they're ready to buy or rent.
  • SEO and SEM – Getting their websites and ads in front of property buyers searching on Google.

These businesses are funded. They have budgets. A mid-size real estate agency in Lagos makes ₦50 million to ₦300 million in annual revenue. They can afford to pay you ₦200,000 to ₦500,000 per month for ongoing marketing services, or ₦2 million to ₦10 million for a complete website rebuild.

The Problem: How Do You Find These Businesses?

You can't find them on Upwork. They're not posting jobs there. They're either:

  1. Hiring agencies (paying inflated prices)
  2. Hiring friends or local vendors (paying inconsistently)
  3. Ignoring their marketing entirely (because they don't know who to hire)
  4. Managing everything in-house badly (because they're not marketing experts)

This is where cold email becomes your superpower. But cold email only works if you have real, targeted leads with actual business names, decision-maker emails, phone numbers, and websites.

Reality Check: Sending 100 generic cold emails to random addresses wastes your internet data and your time. But sending 50 highly targeted emails to real estate business owners who match your ideal client profile? That converts at 5–15%, meaning 2–7 real conversations, and 1–2 actual clients.

How to Cold Email Real Estate Businesses (The Process)

Step 1: Segment Your List by Value and Pain

Not all real estate businesses are equal. Prioritize:

  • Estate developers – Highest budgets (₦5–50 million/year for marketing)
  • Large agencies – Multiple branches, big ad spend
  • Property tech platforms – Tech-savvy, willing to invest in growth
  • Solo agents and small agencies – Lower budgets, but easier to close

Step 2: Personalize Your Subject Line and Opening

Generic subject lines get deleted. Reference something specific:

Example 1 (Generic – Don't Use):
"Web Design Services for Your Business"

Example 2 (Personalized – Use This):
"Seen your Lekki properties on Instagram — noticed website could convert more buyers"

Example 3 (If You've Done Research):
"Quick idea for XYZ Estates: Your competitors rank #1 on Google for 'estate in Ikoyi' — we can change that"

Step 3: Lead With Value, Not a Pitch

Your first email should offer insight, not ask for a call.

Template (Adapt to Your Service):

Hi [Name],

I noticed [Name of Agency] listed 50+ properties on Instagram in the last 3 months—solid output. But I also noticed your website hasn't been updated since 2022, and most property pages don't have professional photos or detailed descriptions.

Most estate agents leave 40–60% of potential sales on the table because their websites don't convert browsers into inquiries. The ones that do typically see 2–3x more leads.

I help real estate businesses rebuild their websites and sales funnels to actually close deals. I thought it might be worth a quick conversation.

Is this something you'd be open to exploring?

[Your name]

Step 5: Follow Up (Most Replies Come After Email 3–5)

Never expect a reply to your first email. Most real estate owners are busy. They see your email in a crowded inbox.

  4. Email 4 (Day 14): Last attempt. "Guessing this isn't a priority right now, but if you ever want to discuss, happy to help."

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Selling Too Early

Your first email says "hire me." They delete it. Instead, lead with education or insight. The pitch comes in email 3 or a phone call.

2. Using a Free Email Sender

Gmail, Yahoo, and Outlook flag bulk emails as spam when sent in batches. Use Zaram Web Mailer or similar tools that warm up domains and manage deliverability.

3. Not Personalizing

Sending the exact same email to 100 people gets you reported and blocked. Use merge tags ([Business Name], [Owner Name]) and vary your angles slightly.

4. Giving Up Too Early

Most freelancers send one email and assume it didn't work. Real estate owners are busy—follow up 3–5 times over 2 weeks before marking them as "no response."

5. Targeting Too Broad

If you specialize in website design, don't email accountants. Get real estate-specific leads. When you're starting, niche down to one city (Lagos) or one service (web design for estate agents).

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Will real estate businesses actually reply to cold emails from a freelancer?

A: Yes. Real estate business owners are pragmatic—if you offer something that saves them money or makes them money, they respond. The key is not sounding desperate or generic. When your email says "I noticed your website hasn't been updated in 3 years, and here's why that costs you leads," they pause and read. Expect 3–8% reply rate from quality leads, and 40–60% of those will actually have a conversation. Real estate professionals are used to sales outreach, so they're not offended by it.

Q: How much should I charge Nigerian real estate clients?

A: Start with this framework. For a website design: ₦2–5 million depending on complexity (small agent site: ₦2M; medium agency: ₦3.5M; large developer: ₦5M+). For monthly retainers (social media, content, email): ₦250,000–₦500,000/month for small-to-medium businesses; ₦500,000–₦2M/month for large developers. Most real estate clients prefer retainers because they want ongoing marketing, not a one-time project. For more on pricing, see our article on how to price web design in Nigeria.

Q: How long does it take to get a reply after sending cold emails?

A: First replies typically come within 2–7 days. But don't expect everyone to reply after email 1. Most conversions happen after email 3–4 (days 9–14). Real estate owners are busy—they're managing properties, showing clients, and closing deals. Your email is one of 50 in their inbox. Patience and follow-ups are critical. Using Zaram Web Mailer automates the follow-up sequence, so you don't have to manually resend emails.

Q: What if I send cold emails and get reported for spam?

A: This happens if you: (1) send from Gmail/Yahoo in bulk, (2) don't follow up properly, (3) copy-paste the exact same email 100 times. Solutions: Use Zaram Web Mailer, which is built for cold email and handles spam filtering. Warm up your domain first. Personalize every email with at least the business name and owner name. Keep emails under 150 words. Don't use multiple links. And always include an unsubscribe option (required by law). Following these rules, you won't get reported.
