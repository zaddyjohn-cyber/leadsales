Why Cold Email Beats Upwork for Nigerian Plumbers and Service Providers

Let's be honest: Upwork takes 20% commission. Fiverr takes 20%. You're also competing against freelancers in Pakistan, India, and the Philippines who charge ₦2,000–₦4,000 for jobs you should be charging ₦50,000 for.

Cold email changes the equation:

  • Direct negotiation: No platform middleman. A ₦200,000 plumbing job stays ₦200,000—you don't give 20% away.
  • Recurring clients: Plumbers need ongoing work—regular maintenance, emergency calls, new installations. One lead can turn into ₦500,000+ annually.
  • Higher rates: Businesses pay more for direct service providers than gig platforms. Expect 2–3x higher rates.
  • Reduced competition: Most Nigerian plumbers aren't cold emailing. You're competing against 5–10 businesses, not 5,000.

Real example: A plumber in Lagos sending cold emails to property management companies can land clients paying ₦150,000–₦300,000 per month for maintenance contracts. On Upwork, you'd need 30–40 small jobs to earn that.

How Much Can You Actually Earn? Real Numbers for Nigeria

Before diving into tactics, let's set expectations. Here's what Nigerian plumbers and service providers are realistically earning with cold email:

  • Month 1–2: ₦50,000–₦150,000 (1–3 small clients, no reply rate yet)
  • Month 3–4: ₦300,000–₦600,000 (4–6 active clients, some recurring work)
  • Month 5–6+: ₦800,000–₦1,500,000 (8–12 clients, mostly recurring maintenance contracts)

The timeline depends on:

  • How many leads you email per week (50–100 is realistic)
  • Your email response rate (2–5% for plumbing services in Nigeria)
  • Your close rate (20–40% of replies convert to clients)
  • Average job value (₦50,000–₦300,000 depending on scope)

In USD terms: This translates to $200–$3,600 monthly by month 6+, using the 1 USD = ₦1,550 NGN exchange rate (adjust for current rates). For many Nigerians with Payoneer, Wise, or Stripe accounts, this becomes genuinely livable income—especially if you reinvest ₦100,000–₦150,000/month back into lead generation and tools.

Step 2: Craft Your Cold Email (The Pitch That Works)

Your email has one job: Get a reply. Not a sale—a conversation.

Here's a template that works for plumbing services in Nigeria:

Subject: Quick question re: [Company Name]'s plumbing maintenance

Hi [First Name],

I help facilities managers in Lagos cut emergency plumbing call-outs by 40% through preventative maintenance contracts.

Most companies don't have a dedicated plumber on retainer—so when a toilet leaks or a tap fails, it becomes a ₦80k+ emergency that disrupts operations.

We fix that. Scheduled quarterly inspections, priority response times, flat monthly rates—no surprise bills.

Quick question: Is plumbing maintenance something your team currently handles in-house, or do you contract it out?

If the latter, I'd love a 15-min call to see if our setup could work for you.

Cheers,
[Your Name]
[Your Phone]
[Your Email]

Why this works:

  • It speaks to a real pain point (expensive emergency repairs)
  • It offers a solution (preventative maintenance contracts)
  • It asks a question (not pushy, invites response)
  • It uses local context (₦ amounts, facility manager roles)
  • It's short—fits on mobile (critical in Nigeria where many open emails on phones)

Tweak the pain point based on your lead type:

  • For property owners: "Cut tenant complaints about water pressure/leaks by fixing issues before they complain."
  • For contractors: "Reduce project delays from subcontractor plumbing issues by having a vetted plumber on speed-dial."
  • For hotels/restaurants: "Prevent kitchen/bathroom downtime that costs you paying customers."

Step 4: Follow Up and Close

Your first email gets a 2–5% response rate on a good day. That means out of 100 emails, 2–5 people reply. Most will be lukewarm: "Send me more info" or "Call me next week."

This is where most Nigerian freelancers fail. They send one email, get no reply, assume it doesn't work, and go back to Upwork.

Reality: Follow-ups are where deals close.

  • Email 1 (Day 1): Your main pitch
  • Email 2 (Day 5): Light re-engagement—"Just checking in. Any thoughts?"
  • Email 3 (Day 10): Add value—share a client case study, article on plumbing maintenance ROI
  • Email 4 (Day 15): Final soft close—"Last message from me, but happy to chat anytime."

Zaram Web Mailer automates this. You write all four emails once, and it sends them on schedule to everyone in your list.

For warm replies (people who actually engage), move to WhatsApp or call:

  • WhatsApp is the default in Nigeria—most businesses are on it
  • Send a WhatsApp message: "Hi [Name], Thanks for the email reply. Thought it'd be easier to chat here. When are you free for a quick call about the plumbing setup?"
  • Call within 24 hours if they give you a number. Phone sales close faster.

Step 5: Qualify and Price Your Services

Not every lead is worth your time. Qualify early:

  • Budget: Does this company have ₦50,000+/month for plumbing services? If it's a small 2-person office, maybe not.
  • Pain point match: Do they actually have plumbing challenges (maintenance, emergencies, new construction)?
  • Decision-maker: Are you talking to the person who approves spending, or an assistant who'll need to loop in their boss?

For pricing, offer a menu based on scope:

  • Monthly retainer (preventative): ₦80,000–₦150,000/month for quarterly inspections + priority response
  • Per-job (emergency/repair): ₦60,000–₦150,000 per job depending on complexity
  • Annual contract: ₦600,000–₦1,200,000/year for unlimited emergency calls + scheduled maintenance

Don't compete on price. Compete on reliability and speed. A ₦150,000 retainer sounds expensive until a burst pipe costs them ₦500,000 in downtime.

Common Mistakes Nigerian Freelancers Make With Cold Email

Mistake 1: Buying Random Leads Without a Plan

You download 8,500 leads and immediately send generic "I offer plumbing services" emails to all of them. Result: 0.5% response rate, ₦500 per response when divided by cost.

Fix: Buy leads, segment by type (facilities manager vs. property owner vs. contractor), and write a unique email for each segment. Freelance LeadsHub lets you download leads by specific criteria, so start narrow.

Mistake 2: Expecting Instant Replies

You send 50 emails and check your inbox every hour. After 3 days with no replies, you declare cold email "doesn't work in Nigeria."

Fix: Expect responses to trickle in over 2–4 weeks. First 5 days: almost nothing. Week 2: a few replies. Week 3–4: bulk of responses as people read the 3rd/4th follow-up. This is normal globally and definitely normal in Nigeria where email isn't always checked daily.

Mistake 3: Not Following Up

One email = one conversation. If they don't reply to email 1, they saw it at a bad time, deleted it, or forgot. Four follow-ups over 2 weeks = much higher close rate.

Fix: Use Zaram Web Mailer to automate follow-ups. Set it and forget it.

Mistake 4: Not Tracking What Works

You send 500 emails, get replies, but don't know which email template, subject line, or lead source worked best. Next month, you repeat the same approach blindly.

Fix: Use Zaram Web Mailer's tracking to see open rates, click rates, and reply rates by campaign. Tweak and improve each month.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Will plumbing companies in Nigeria actually respond to cold emails?

A: Yes, but with caveats. Facilities managers, property owners, and construction companies check email (though not obsessively). Response rates of 2–5% are realistic, which means 100 emails = 2–5 conversations. The key is following up 3–4 times over 2 weeks. Most response happens in week 2–3, not immediately. Also, WhatsApp follow-ups dramatically increase close rates since it's more immediate and personal than email in the Nigerian context.

Q: Should I specialize in one type of plumbing (e.g., repairs vs. installation) or offer everything?

A: Specialize in your first 6–12 months. Pick one niche—say, facility maintenance for office buildings in Lagos—and become the known expert for that. Once you have 5–10 repeating clients, expand to installation or emergency repairs. Generalists compete on price; specialists charge premium rates. A ₦150,000/month retainer from one facilities manager is better than ₦20,000 emergency calls from 10 different sources.

Q: What if I don't have a dedicated plumber yet—I'm just a freelancer interested in the business?

A: You can still cold email. Position yourself as a "plumbing solutions coordinator" who handles scheduling, invoicing, and quality control while contracting actual plumbers. This is a scalable model: You sell services, contractors do the work, you take 20–30% commission. Or, if you have plumbing experience yourself, start part-time—cold email in evenings, do jobs on weekends. Once you hit ₦200,000+/month, go full-time.

Q: How do I handle payment from clients? Should I use Payoneer, Wise, or a Nigerian bank account?

A: Use all three. Nigerian businesses prefer paying to Nigerian bank accounts via transfer (fastest, no forex fees). Get a business account with your bank (GTBank, Access Bank, Zenith, etc.). Startups and international clients use Payoneer or Wise. For very large contracts (₦500,000+), ask for 50% upfront transfer to your Nigerian account, 50% on completion. This protects you and gives clients confidence you're real.
