The Most Common Mistakes Freelancers Make

Mistake 1: Sending Generic Emails

Your email says: "Hi, I'm a freelancer offering copywriting services. Let me know if interested."

Delete rate: 95%+.

Your email should say: "Hi [Name], I noticed [Company] just launched [Product/Service]. I specialize in writing email sequences that convert browsers into buyers—I helped [Similar Company] increase email revenue by 35% in 90 days. Happy to share samples. Let me know."

Delete rate: 50–70% (normal). Reply rate: 2–5% (profitable).

Mistake 2: Not Following Up

Most freelancers send one email and wait. That's why their response rate is 0.2%.

The real money is in follow-ups. Send an initial email. Wait 5–7 days. Send a follow-up. Wait another 5 days. Send a final follow-up. This 3-email sequence generates 60–70% of your total replies, not the first email.

Mistake 3: Wrong Niche or Too Broad

Downloading an email list of "all businesses in Nigeria" and sending the same pitch to law firms, e-commerce stores, and tech startups means 0% of your emails are relevant.

Narrow down. Pick one niche. Become the expert in that niche. Your response rate will be 5–10x higher.

Mistake 4: Not Tracking Results

You send 5,000 emails and have no idea how many opened, clicked, or replied. You don't know if your subject line sucks or your offer sucks. You can't iterate.

Use a tool like Zaram Web Mailer that tracks opens, clicks, and replies. You'll see data like "Subject line A: 22% open rate. Subject line B: 8% open rate." Now you know what works.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is it legal to download and email business contacts in Nigeria?

A: Yes, it's legal. Nigeria doesn't have strict GDPR-like laws for B2B email marketing. However, you should follow basic best practices: include your name and business in the email, provide a way for people to unsubscribe, and don't send more than once per week to the same person. Most cold email is compliance-friendly as long as you're transparent about who you are. Avoid spam traps by using a legitimate email tool like Zaram Web Mailer that doesn't let you email harvested lists or old scraped data.

Q: How many emails should I send per day to avoid getting marked as spam?

A: Start with 100–300 emails per day from a single email account. Most email providers flag accounts sending 500+ emails in one day as suspicious. Use a tool like Zaram Web Mailer that spreads sends across multiple accounts and time zones—it mimics human behavior and keeps you safe. More detail in our cold email frequency guide.

Q: What if I get low response rates from my downloaded list?

A: Low response rates usually mean one of three things: (1) your subject line isn't compelling, (2) your pitch isn't personalized or relevant, or (3) you're emailing the wrong niche. Test a new subject line on 200 emails and compare open rates. Personalize by mentioning their company name or recent news. And if you're sending the same pitch to lawyers and e-commerce stores, you're guaranteed to fail—narrow your niche.

Q: Can I use the same email list twice or resell it?

The data refreshes monthly, so old lists become outdated anyway. If you need a fresh list after 3 months, download a new batch—the cost is low enough that it's worth having current, warm data.
