Method 2: LinkedIn and Social Media Outreach

Many Nigerian e-commerce owners are on LinkedIn, Instagram, and Facebook. You can find them, but it requires work.

LinkedIn Search Strategy

  1. Search: "E-commerce" OR "Online Store" OR "Fashion Brand" location: Nigeria
  2. Filter by: 1st or 2nd-degree connections, current title includes "Founder," "CEO," or "Director"
  3. Personalized message: "Hi [Name], I noticed you run [store name]. I've helped similar brands reduce product photo turnaround time by 40%. Happy to share what I've learned. Best, [You]"
  4. Follow-up after 3 days if no response

Time cost: 30 minutes/day to find 10 prospects, pitch 5, get 0–1 response. Low conversion, but zero cost.

Instagram and TikTok Shop Strategy

Nigerian fashion, beauty, and food e-commerce thrives on Instagram and TikTok Shop. Follow trending hashtags (#Nigerian Fashion, #BuyNigerian, #ShopLagos) and direct-message store owners. This works best if you're offering:

  • Content creation / Reel editing
  • Instagram shop setup
  • TikTok Shop optimization
  • Caption writing / hashtag strategy

You'll get 2–5 DM replies per 50 outreaches. It's slower than email, but some owners respond faster to Instagram.

Method 3: Local Networking and Referrals

This works better in Lagos and Abuja, but it's gold if you're in these cities.

Where to Find E-Commerce Owners

  • E-Commerce Nigeria Facebook Groups: "Nigerian Online Sellers," "E-Commerce Hustlers Nigeria," "Shopify Store Owners Nigeria"—3,000–50,000 members. Post value, not your sales pitch. Answer questions. Drop a link to a free resource. Some group members will DM you.
  • Founder meetups: Attend Lagos Digital Summit, WBEF (Women Business Empowerment Forum), or local startup events. Bring 20 business cards. Have a 30-second pitch ready.
  • Chamber of Commerce: Lagos Chamber of Commerce, Abuja Business Association. Pay ₦5,000–₦15,000 for networking events. You'll meet 40–100 business owners in a room.
  • Ask for referrals: If you've worked with one store owner, ask for 3 referrals. Pay them ₦10,000 referral fee per closed deal. This spreads word-of-mouth fast.

Method 4: Google Maps and Directory Scraping (Manual)

Search "online store Lagos," "fashion boutique Nigeria," or "beauty supply shop." Google Maps lists hundreds. You'll find websites, phone numbers, and sometimes email addresses.

Process:

  1. Open Google Maps
  2. Search your niche + city (e.g., "Electronics Store Abuja")
  3. Click each listing, visit their website
  4. Copy contact email or phone
  5. Add to spreadsheet
  6. Send cold email

Time cost: 1–2 hours to find 30 emails. Tedious, but free. Conversion: 2–4 responses.

If you're doing this at scale, use a tool like Hunter.io (₦5,000/month for 100 verified emails) to verify email addresses from websites you've found. This saves you sending to dead emails.

What to Say When You Pitch: The Cold Email Formula

You've found 20 store owners. Now what? Don't send generic "I can help grow your business" nonsense. Be specific.

Cold Email Template That Works

Subject Line: "[Store Name] + [specific pain point]"

Example: "Raven Beauty + faster product shots"

Body:

Hi [First Name],

I checked out your Shopify store—your summer collection photos are clean, but I noticed you're uploading ~3 products/day. Most stores with your turnover do 8–10.

I've helped [similar store name] build a batch product photography + editing system that cut their turnaround from 2 days to 4 hours. Result: they added 15 new products/week instead of 5.

If that's a bottleneck, I'd love to spend 15 min this week exploring whether we're a fit.

Best,
[Your name]
[Phone]

Why this works:

  • Specific (mentions their store, actual problem)
  • Social proof (you've done this before)
  • Quantified result (15 products vs 5)
  • Low-friction ask (just a 15-minute call)
  • Short (60 seconds to read)

Send this with Zaram Web Mailer, which tracks opens and auto-sends follow-ups on day 3 and day 7. Track which subject lines get the most opens, which niches convert best, and refine your pitch.

Real conversion rate: 5–12% response, 20–40% of responders become clients. So from 100 cold emails, expect 5–12 meetings, 1–5 paying clients.

Common Mistakes (And How to Avoid Them)

Mistake 1: Sending from a Gmail with no real name
Fix: Use a professional email domain (yourname.com or your-agency.com). You'll get 2–3x higher open rates and fewer spam folder hits.

Mistake 2: Pitching without research
"I can help your business grow" doesn't work. Spend 2 minutes on their Instagram or website. Find a real pain point. "I noticed your site takes 5 seconds to load—that's costing you 20% of mobile visitors" converts. Generic doesn't.

Mistake 3: Only sending once
Expect 3–5% response on first email, 8–10% on second follow-up (3 days later), 10–12% on third (7 days later). Most freelancers give up after one email. Don't.

Mistake 4: Not tracking data
Which subject lines convert best? Which niches? Which days of the week? Which follow-up timing? Track this. You'll optimize your way from 5% to 12% response rates by month 3.

Mistake 5: Using your personal phone
Some store owners will call you back. If you're managing Slack, WhatsApp, and client calls on one phone, you'll miss calls and look unprofessional. Use a VoIP service (Gabb costs ₦2,000/month) to separate business from personal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How do I know if an e-commerce store is worth pitching?

A: Look for three signals: (1) They're posting regularly on social media (Instagram stories 3+ times/week), (2) They have a functioning website or Shopify store with at least 20 products, (3) They mention revenue or have visible customers (comments, reviews). If a store hasn't posted in 6 months and has 5 products, they're not actively operating or spending money. Skip them.

Q: What's the difference between cold emailing and spam?

A: Cold email is personalized, relevant, and sends only one email per prospect per week. You mention their store by name, identify a real problem, and provide value. Spam is blast-sending "Make ₦500k/week" to 10,000 random addresses. You're not spamming if you: (a) research the prospect, (b) use a professional email domain, (c) send from a tool like Zaram Web Mailer that avoids spam filters, (d) include an unsubscribe link, (e) don't send to the same person twice in one week.

Q: Should I specialize in one niche or cast a wide net?

A: Start with one or two niches (e.g., fashion e-commerce + beauty e-commerce). You'll understand their pain points, develop case studies, and pitch with authority. A generic "I help e-commerce stores" message converts at 2%. "I've helped 5 fashion brands increase their average order value by 18% through email upsell sequences" converts at 8–10%. Once you have 3–4 case studies in one niche, expand.

Q: How many cold emails should I send per week?

A: Start with 50/week if you're a beginner, 100–150/week if you're experienced. Gmail will flag accounts sending 500+ emails/day as spam. Space them out: 20 Monday–Wednesday, 20 Thursday–Friday, 10 Monday of next week. Use Zaram Web Mailer to automate this—it staggered sends to avoid spam triggers.
