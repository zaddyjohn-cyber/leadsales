How to Get SEO Clients in Nigeria: A Practical Guide for Freelancers

August 11, 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

You're an SEO freelancer in Nigeria. You know how to rank websites, improve domain authority, and drive organic traffic. But here's the problem: you're stuck competing with thousands of other freelancers on Upwork, bidding on ₦10,000 projects, and your earnings barely cover your NEPA bill.

This article shows you exactly how to get SEO clients in Nigeria—the right way. Not by hoping someone finds you on a marketplace. But by directly reaching out to businesses that actually need your services and can pay real money (₦150,000–₦500,000+ per month for ongoing SEO contracts).

Why the Standard Path Doesn't Work for Nigerian SEO Freelancers

Upwork, Fiverr, and Freelancer are crowded. In Nigeria, you're competing against:

  • Indian freelancers quoting ₦20,000 for a full month of SEO services
  • Other Nigerians underselling at 40–50% below market rate just to land work
  • Clients with unrealistic budgets who want premium results at budget prices
  • Algorithm changes that bury your profile beneath thousands of newer listings

The math doesn't work. Even with a 5-star profile, you're fighting for scraps.

Direct outreach—cold emailing businesses that need SEO—changes the equation entirely. Instead of competing on price, you're solving a real problem for a business owner who already knows they need help.

Step 1: Build a Simple SEO Portfolio and Case Studies

Before you pitch to anyone, you need proof of work. Businesses won't hire you based on promises. They'll hire you based on results.

What You Actually Need

  • 3–5 real case studies showing before/after metrics: keyword rankings, organic traffic growth, conversion improvements
  • Screenshots from Google Search Console, Google Analytics, or Semrush showing organic traffic increases
  • A simple one-page PDF or Google Doc showing your results (no fancy design needed—focus on numbers)
  • A basic website or portfolio (even a single landing page on Wix or Carrd) showing your services and one client result

If you're starting out and don't have clients yet, work with 2–3 local Nigerian businesses for free or at a heavily discounted rate. Get them real results in 60–90 days, then use that as your portfolio. This investment pays off quickly—one ₦200,000/month retainer client covers the cost of your initial free work in weeks.

Key insight: Nigerian business owners trust results more than credentials. A single case study showing "Ranked #3 for a target keyword within 90 days" is more persuasive than 10 certifications.

Step 2: Define Your Target Market (Be Specific)

The biggest mistake freelancers make is trying to serve "all businesses that need SEO." That's too broad and makes your pitch sound generic.

Instead, pick a specific niche:

  • E-commerce businesses selling physical products (fashion, beauty, food)
  • Local service providers (real estate agents, lawyers, plumbers, electricians)
  • Online courses and coaching businesses
  • SaaS and software companies
  • B2B service businesses (digital agencies, consulting firms, logistics)
  • Travel and hospitality (hotels, tour operators, restaurants)

Why? Because when you specialize, you can:

  • Speak the language of that industry
  • Reference specific competitors they care about
  • Show results from similar businesses
  • Charge premium rates (because you're solving industry-specific problems)

For a Nigerian context, focus on niches with local demand and higher budgets: e-commerce, real estate, and professional services are your goldmines.

Step 4: Craft a Cold Email That Gets Responses

Your cold email is do-or-die. If it doesn't get opened, nothing else matters.

The Formula for a Response-Getting SEO Cold Email

Subject line: Keep it short and specific. Avoid spam-trigger words.

  • ✅ "Quick SEO opportunity for [Business Name]"
  • ✅ "[Competitor Name] is ranking ahead of you—I can fix that"
  • ❌ "FREE SEO audit" (screams spam)
  • ❌ "You Need SEO NOW" (aggressive, ignored)

Email body: Use this structure:

  1. Personalization (1 line): Show you've researched them. "I noticed you're selling [product/service] in Lagos."
  2. The hook (2 lines): Mention a specific problem or missed opportunity. "Your main competitor [name] ranks for 200+ keywords you're not targeting. They're getting the search traffic you're leaving on the table."
  3. Social proof (1 line): One quick win. "I helped [similar business] rank #1 for their top keyword in 90 days and they've since closed 12+ deals from that ranking."
  4. One clear CTA: Don't ask for the sale. Offer a low-friction next step. "Worth a 15-minute call to discuss? I can show you the specific opportunities in your market."
  5. Sign-off: Keep it casual. You're solving a problem, not begging for work.

Example email:

Hi [First Name],

I noticed you're selling beauty products across Nigeria. Quick observation: your competitors on Google are getting 200+ organic searches monthly for keywords like "[relevant keyword]"—but you're not ranking for those searches yet.

I help e-commerce businesses like yours fix that. I recently helped [similar business] rank #1 for their top keywords, and they've been getting 50+ qualified visits per month from it.

Interested in a quick 15-minute call to see what's possible for your brand? No pressure either way.

Best,
[Your name]
[Your phone number]
[Simple website or portfolio link]

This works because it's: personalized, specific, proof-backed, and low-pressure.

Step 5: Qualify Leads Before Pitching

Not every lead is worth your time. Some businesses can't afford you. Others aren't ready for SEO (they need branding first). Others are too small to justify your effort.

Before sending your email, quickly check:

  • Do they have a website? (If no, they're not ready for SEO)
  • What's their current organic traffic? Use SEMrush free trial or Ubersuggest to spy on competitors
  • Are they targeting keywords? (Look at their website content and Google Search Console data if visible)
  • What's their revenue size? Use LinkedIn or their website to estimate. Companies with ₦5M+ annual revenue can afford SEO. Below that, they likely can't.
  • How established are they? Newer businesses (< 1 year) aren't great SEO clients yet—they need more stable revenue first

This 2-minute check prevents you from wasting time on bad-fit prospects.

Quick tip: Nigerian businesses that sell products or services to other businesses (B2B) have higher budgets for SEO than B2C. Prioritize B2B leads—they close faster and pay better.

Step 6: Follow Up Without Being Annoying

Your first email gets a 15–20% open rate. Most replies don't come from the first send.

Plan a follow-up sequence:

  1. Initial email: Day 1 (the pitch)
  2. Follow-up #1: Day 5 ("Just checking in—did you see my last email?") Keep it light. Include a different value angle. Maybe a specific competitor they should know about.
  3. Follow-up #2: Day 12 ("One more thing before I move on...") Share a quick insight about their industry or a free resource. Make it useful, not salesy.
  4. Follow-up #3: Day 20 (Final touch) Let them know you're wrapping up your outreach but you're open if they change their mind in the future.

Don't send 10 emails. Most people respond between email 2 and 4. More than that feels desperate.

Step 7: Close the Call and Land the Contract

When you get a meeting, your goal is simple: understand their problem, show how SEO fixes it, and get a commitment.

On the Call, Ask These Questions

  • "What's your biggest challenge with getting new customers right now?"
  • "How much traffic are you getting from Google per month?" (Most don't know—this shows a gap you can fill)
  • "What's your timeline to see results?" (Sets expectations)
  • "What's your monthly marketing budget?" (Critical for pricing)

Then make the pitch: "Here's what I'd do for you in the first 90 days: [specific actions]. Based on similar work I've done, you'd likely see [specific result]. My fee is [price]. Let's start with a trial month so you can see results before committing longer."

Nigerian pricing for SEO retainers (realistic market rates 2026):

  • Starter (local businesses, limited keywords): ₦80,000–150,000/month
  • Mid-tier (growing e-commerce, multiple keywords): ₦200,000–400,000/month
  • Premium (established brands, competitive markets): ₦500,000–1,000,000+/month

Start with a 3-month commitment. This gives you time to deliver results while building trust.

Payment: Always ask for 50% upfront via Grey, Payoneer, or Wise. The other 50% on day 15. This protects you from non-payment (a real issue with some Nigerian clients).

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How long before I see results with this approach?

A: Your first response typically comes within 3–7 days of your initial email. From there, a meeting happens within 1–2 weeks. A signed contract can happen the same week. So realistically, 2–3 weeks from your first cold email to landing a new client. SEO results for your clients take longer (3–6 months), but you get paid while you work.

Q: Should I charge in dollars or naira? What's the current rate?

A: Charge in dollars for international clients (easier for them to pay via Payoneer/Wise). For Nigerian clients, charge in naira but base your pricing on the USD equivalent. As of 2026, 1 USD ≈ ₦1,200–1,300 depending on your conversion method. A ₦200,000/month retainer is roughly $155–170 USD. Q: What if I'm new and don't have case studies yet?

A: Do 2–3 projects with small discount (₦60,000–80,000 each instead of ₦150,000) for local businesses. Require they use you for at least 3 months so you have real numbers to show. In 90 days, you'll have legitimate case studies. This strategy costs you time but gives you the portfolio to charge premium rates afterward. It's an investment in your credibility.

Q: How many emails should I send per day to avoid spam filters?

A: If you're using Zaram Web Mailer, send 50–100 per day maximum. Spreading them out over time (not blasting all 1,000 at once) keeps you safe. Gmail and other providers flag sudden spikes as spam. Slow and steady wins the race here.
