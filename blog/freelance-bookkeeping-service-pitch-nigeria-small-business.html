Most Nigerian freelancers pitching bookkeeping services waste time on Upwork, competing with thousands of others for scraps. Or worse, they send generic cold emails to random businesses and get ignored.

Here's the reality: Nigerian small business owners desperately need bookkeeping help—they're drowning in receipts, struggling with tax compliance, and have no idea what their actual profit margin is. But they won't find you on Fiverr. They need someone to reach them directly with a solution tailored to their pain.

This guide shows you exactly how to pitch bookkeeping services to small businesses Nigeria using cold email, realistic pricing in naira and dollars, and where to find the right businesses to contact. By the end, you'll have a system that generates ₦50,000–₦150,000 per month per client.

Why Small Businesses in Nigeria Need Bookkeeping (And Will Pay for It)

Let's start with the economic truth. Nigeria's small business sector is fragmented. Most businesses operating in Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, and secondary cities run on spreadsheets—if they keep records at all. Many don't understand VAT, withholding tax, or the Companies Income Tax Act requirements.

According to the Small and Medium Enterprises Development Agency of Nigeria (SMEDAN), there are over 41 million micro, small, and medium enterprises (MSMEs) in Nigeria. Most are registered and legally required to file tax returns. That's your market.

The pain points Nigerian business owners face:

  • Tax compliance confusion: FIRS deadlines, VAT returns, withholding tax obligations—most owners ignore it until an audit letter arrives.
  • No visibility into profitability: They can't tell if they made a profit or loss this month without hiring an accountant (which costs ₦100,000+/month).
  • Wasted time on admin: Reconciling bank statements, chasing invoices, organizing receipts—time they could spend growing.
  • Expensive accountants: Traditional accountants in Nigeria charge ₦80,000–₦200,000/month and take weeks to deliver reports.
  • Bank loan rejection: Banks won't approve loans without proper financial records—many businesses have this problem.

You solve all of these. And they will pay between ₦30,000 and ₦150,000 per month for reliable bookkeeping, depending on transaction volume.

Step 2: Craft Your Cold Email Pitch

A good cold email for bookkeeping has one job: Show them you understand their problem and have solved it before. No jargon. No "synergy." No fluff.

The winning email structure:

  1. Open with specificity (1 line): Mention their business name and something specific about what they do.
  2. Problem statement (2–3 lines): Show you know a real pain point.
  3. Quick proof (1–2 lines): Mention a relevant result you've achieved.
  4. The ask (1 line): Super specific—call, demo, 15-min chat.
  5. Close (simple): Your name, phone number.

Cold email template for Nigerian bookkeeping:

Subject: Bookkeeping help for [Business Name]

Hi [First Name],

I noticed [Business Name] does [specific service/product] in [location]. 

Most businesses in your space spend 5–8 hours monthly just organizing invoices and receipts—time you could use to grow.

I've helped similar trading companies in Lagos get tax-ready records in weeks instead of months, which made bank loan applications painless.

Do you have 15 minutes next week to see how we could simplify your books?

Best,
[Your Name]
[Phone: +234XXXXXXXXXX]

Real example for a salon:

Subject: Help for Glam Beauty Lounge

Hi Chioma,

I saw Glam Beauty Lounge just opened a second location in Lekki. Congrats!

Managing records across two locations is a nightmare without proper bookkeeping—and VAT compliance gets messy fast.

I've set up books for 7 salons in Lagos, and most cut their admin time by 60% within the first month.

Worth a quick call to explore?

+234 708 345 6789
Sandra

Notice: Short, specific, one clear problem, one clear outcome, one clear call-to-action.

Step 4: Follow-Up and Qualification

Your first email gets 10–20% opens. Most people don't respond to the first email. This is normal. You need a follow-up sequence.

Follow-up sequence:

  • Email 2 (3 days later): "Wanted to make sure this landed in your inbox—here's a 2-minute video on how bookkeeping helps businesses like yours." (Link to a 90-second YouTube short or Loom video.)
  • Email 3 (5 days later): "Just spoke with a business in [their industry] about their bookkeeping headaches. Thought you'd find it useful." (Share a quick case study or testimonial.)
  • Email 4 (7 days later): "Last one—if now isn't the right time, no worries. But if you ever need a bookkeeper, I'm here."

Then stop. Move to the next batch.

When someone responds, qualify them immediately:

  1. How many transactions do you process monthly? (Tells you pricing tier.)
  2. Who's currently handling your records? (Tells you if they're serious or just browsing.)
  3. What's your biggest headache right now? (Tells you their real pain.)
  4. When would you want to start? (Tells you urgency.)

If they're vague or just "thinking about it," send them a 2-minute call link and move on. Your time is valuable.

Step 5: Close with Confidence (Pricing & Contract)

Once they agree to a call, your job is simple:

  1. Ask questions to understand their exact needs (transaction volume, number of staff, current chaos level).
  2. Propose the right tier (Starter, Professional, or Enterprise).
  3. Quote in NGN. Be specific—"₦50,000/month, starting next week, due on the 5th of each month."
  4. Send a one-page service agreement via email (Google Docs or PDF is fine).
  5. Ask for 50% upfront, 50% after the first month's work (protects you both).
  6. Request they pay via Payoneer, Wise, or direct bank transfer. If they insist on cash, meet them in person.

First client cash flow timing: You pitch on Week 1, close on Week 2, receive 50% upfront (₦15,000–₦75,000 depending on tier), start work Week 3. That money hits your Payoneer/Wise account within 3 days. Convert to USD at ₦600–₦750 per dollar and move on.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Pitching to businesses too small

Don't pitch sole traders with no employees. They won't pay. Pitch businesses with 3+ staff, or those handling high transaction volume (retail, e-commerce, restaurants).

2. Generic emails

Every email should mention their business name and something specific about them. Generic emails get a 0.3% response rate.

3. Not setting boundaries

Bookkeeping clients will ask for "quick help" outside your scope. Document what's included in your ₦XX,000/month scope. Anything extra = extra invoice.

4. Weak follow-up

Most freelancers send one email and give up. Send at least 3 follow-ups. Persistence works.

3. Using Gmail for bulk sends

Gmail will block you after 500 emails in 24 hours. Use Zaram Web Mailer instead—it's designed for bulk cold email and has Nigerian domain reputation built in.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What software should I use to keep my clients' books?

A: Use Wave (free, cloud-based), Zoho Books (₦8,000/month for basic), or Google Sheets if they're just starting. For serious clients, Wave is best—it's free, integrates with Nigerian banks via Paystack/Flutterwave, and clients can access reports anytime. Your job is data entry and reconciliation; you don't need expensive software. Keep it simple.

Q: How do I handle currency risk if a client suddenly stops paying in NGN?

A: Always quote in NGN. If a client says "I can only pay in USD," convert to NGN at the commercial rate on the day of invoice (usually ₦750–₦850/USD). Move money to Wise or Payoneer same day—don't hold NGN cash. This protects you from naira depreciation.

Q: Do I need formal accounting certification to pitch bookkeeping?

A: Not legally required in Nigeria. You're offering a freelance service, not an accounting firm. That said, basic knowledge of FIRS tax rules, VAT, and Nigerian accounting standards helps. Read the FIRS website and take a free online course (Coursera, Udemy). Most clients care about results (accurate records, tax compliance), not your certificate.

Q: How do I handle a client with disorganized records stretching back 2+ years?

A: Charge extra for cleanup (₦30,000–₦50,000 one-time fee, or add it to their first 2 months). Set expectations clearly: "First month will be data entry and organization. Starting month 2, we'll have real-time, clean books." If records are extremely messy, refer them to an accountant or audit firm—it's not a freelance job anymore.
