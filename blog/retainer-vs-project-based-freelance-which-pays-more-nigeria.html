Retainer vs Project-Based Freelance: Which Pays More in Nigeria?

August 14, 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

The question every Nigerian freelancer asks: which model makes more money — retainer or project-based work?

The honest answer: retainers almost always win. But not in the way you might think. It's not about the hourly rate or per-project fee. It's about predictability, consistency, and time leverage.

In this guide, I'll break down real numbers in Naira and dollars, show you the actual income timelines, and explain why most Nigerian freelancers stay broke even when they have skills — and how the right business model fixes that.

The Core Difference: Why Project-Based Feels Fast But Retainer Wins Long-Term

Let's start with definitions, because clarity matters here:

  • Project-based work: You complete a defined job (design a logo, build a website, write 10 blog posts), get paid once or in milestones, then hunt for the next client.
  • Retainer work: A client pays you a fixed monthly fee (e.g., ₦150,000/month) in exchange for ongoing services — 10 hours/week, specific deliverables, recurring value.

On the surface, a big project seems attractive. You quote ₦200,000 for a website build, complete it in 3 weeks, bank the money. But here's what actually happens:

  • Week 1–3: You build the site, make ₦200,000.
  • Week 4–6: You're hunting for the next client. Zero income.
  • Week 7–9: You negotiate, revise proposals, maybe land a ₦150,000 project.
  • Week 10–14: You execute. Zero downtime income.
  • Week 15–17: Hunting again.

Over 17 weeks, you've made ₦350,000. That's ₦20,588/week average. But you've worked hard, stressed, and had constant gaps.

Now compare retainer income:

  • Month 1: Land a retainer for ₦150,000/month (4 clients × ₦37,500 each, or 2 clients × ₦75,000 each — realistic for content, social media, or light design work).
  • Month 2–12: Same clients pay ₦150,000 each month. Zero hunting during work hours.
  • Months 1–12: You've made ₦1,800,000 annually.

And here's the kicker: with retainer income covering your baseline (rent, data, NEPA), you can take selective higher-margin projects on the side. That ₦200,000 website build in month 6? Now it's pure leverage, not survival money.

Real Income Numbers: Nigeria-Specific Breakdown

Let me be specific about what Nigerian freelancers actually earn, because the difference between ₦50,000 and ₦500,000 income is the difference between eating or thriving.

Project-Based Income Model

Typical freelancer (copywriting, basic design, social media management):

  • Average project value: ₦80,000–₦200,000
  • Time to complete: 1–4 weeks
  • Time to find next client: 2–4 weeks (this is unpaid)
  • Client churn rate: 40–60% (most don't repeat)
  • Average annual income: ₦600,000–₦1,200,000 (₦1,600–₦3,200/month)
  • In USD at ₦1,650/$ rate: $364–$727/month

Advanced freelancer (web development, advanced design, strategy):

  • Average project value: ₦300,000–₦800,000
  • Time to complete: 4–12 weeks
  • Time to find next client: 3–6 weeks (unpaid hunting)
  • Client churn rate: 30–40%
  • Average annual income: ₦1,800,000–₦3,200,000 (₦150,000–₦267,000/month)
  • In USD: $91–$162/month average (despite big project fees, unpaid gaps destroy the math)

Notice the problem? Even a ₦500,000 project doesn't translate to ₦500,000/month income — because you're not finding clients every month.

Retainer Income Model

Entry-level retainer freelancer (3–4 clients):

  • Average retainer fee: ₦40,000–₦75,000/client/month
  • Total monthly revenue: ₦160,000–₦250,000 (3–4 clients)
  • Annual income: ₦1,920,000–₦3,000,000
  • In USD: $116–$182/month (steady, predictable)
  • Time spent: 25–30 hours/week in billable work + minimal hunting

Mid-level retainer freelancer (5–7 clients):

  • Average retainer fee: ₦75,000–₦150,000/client/month
  • Total monthly revenue: ₦400,000–₦750,000
  • Annual income: ₦4,800,000–₦9,000,000
  • In USD: $291–$545/month (highly stable)
  • Time spent: 30–35 hours/week, mostly on execution

Advanced retainer freelancer (6–10 clients at ₦150,000+ each):

  • Monthly revenue: ₦1,000,000–₦1,500,000+
  • Annual income: ₦12,000,000–₦18,000,000+
  • In USD: $727–$1,091+/month (exceptional by Nigerian standards)
  • Time spent: 35–40 hours/week billable work (can outsource or systematize)

The difference is stark: retainer income compounds because clients stay. Project-based income is a perpetual treadmill.

The Real Math: A freelancer earning ₦200,000/month from retainers makes ₦2,400,000/year with 80% certainty. A freelancer making ₦300,000 per project but taking 4–6 weeks between projects makes roughly ₦1,500,000–₦2,000,000/year with constant stress. Retainers win on consistency, not just total income.

Why Retainers Pay More: The Hidden Factors

1. Time Certainty = Higher Effective Hourly Rate

Let's say you charge ₦200/hour for design work. On a project, you work 100 hours, earn ₦20,000, then spend 40 hours hunting (unpaid). Real hourly rate: ₦20,000 ÷ 140 hours = ₦143/hour.

On a retainer, you bill 20 hours/week at ₦200/hour = ₦4,000/week guaranteed. Your effective hourly rate includes zero hunting time. You make ₦200/hour in reality because the client funds your existence.

2. Clients Pay More for Predictability

Businesses value continuity. They'd rather pay ₦100,000/month to a reliable freelancer who shows up consistently than hire 4 different people across 4 months.

This means retainer rates are often 30–50% higher than what the same work costs project-by-project. A ₦50,000 website homepage revision as a project becomes ₦100,000/month retainer work (you handle all revisions, updates, small changes).

3. Retention is Your Profit Margin

Keeping a ₦120,000/month client for 12 months = ₦1,440,000 revenue. The first month costs you ₦40,000 in onboarding and setup. Months 2–12 are 80–90% profit (marginal cost is low). That's impossible in project work where you start hunting again.

4. You Can Raise Rates Annually

Retainer clients accept 10–20% annual increases because switching freelancers is expensive. Project clients shop around every time. You're stuck competing on price.

Retainer vs Project-Based: Decision Matrix

Not every freelancer should chase retainers immediately. Here's how to decide:

Choose Project-Based If:

  • You're just starting out and need to build a portfolio.
  • Your skill takes 2–4 weeks maximum per project (quick turnarounds).
  • You enjoy variety and don't mind hunting constantly.
  • Your service is one-time (e.g., logo design, brand identity).
  • You're working toward retainers but aren't there yet (interim strategy).

Chase Retainers If:

  • You have 1–2 years of experience and proven results.
  • Your service is ongoing (copywriting, design, content, social media, development maintenance).
  • You want predictable income and breathing room.
  • You can find clients willing to commit (usually local businesses or small companies).
  • You're willing to pitch directly instead of relying on platforms.

Real talk: the ideal model is hybrid. Start with 3–4 ₦75,000–₦150,000/month retainers (60% of your time). Use the remaining 40% time for high-margin projects or service expansion. This gives you:

  • ₦225,000–₦600,000/month baseline (retainers).
  • ₦50,000–₦150,000/month from projects (variable).
  • Total: ₦275,000–₦750,000/month with lower stress.

  3. Reach out: "I now offer monthly retainers for [service]. Would ₦X/month work for ongoing [need]?"

The Exchange Rate Reality (and Why It Matters)

As of August 2026, the Naira-to-dollar exchange rate hovers around ₦1,650–₦1,700 per dollar. This matters because:

  • A ₦100,000/month retainer = ~$61/month = not enough if you're targeting USD earnings.
  • A ₦500,000/month retainer = ~$303/month = solid local income, modest in global terms.
  • A ₦1,500,000/month retainer = ~$909/month = respectable anywhere.

If you're working with international clients (and you should be), charge in USD and receive via Wise, Payoneer, or Grey. A $300/month retainer is much better than ₦500,000 monthly retainers with Naira volatility.

Local clients pay in Naira, so your baseline retainer pricing should be ₦75,000–₦200,000 depending on skill and scope. International retainers should start at $150–$500/month depending on specialization.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I charge more for retainers than project-based work?

A: Yes, almost always. A ₦50,000 project (40 hours) works out to ₦1,250/hour. That same work as a retainer (10 hours/month at ₦25,000/month) is ₦2,500/hour effective rate because you're not hunting for clients. Clients accept higher monthly fees because they value stability. Expect retainers to be 20–50% higher than per-project rates for the same work.

Q: What happens if a retainer client leaves?

A: It stings, but it's survivable when you have 4–6 retainers. One client leaving costs you ₦150,000/month, not your entire income. With project work, one client leaving is catastrophic. This is why retainer income is safer — diversification. Your goal is enough retainers that losing one doesn't break you.

Q: How do I price a retainer if I don't know how many hours the client will need?

A: Define the scope clearly: "₦120,000/month includes 8 hours/week, up to 15 deliverables." If they need more, it's additional. Start small (4–6 hours/week) and scale up after 3 months if they ask for more. Many freelancers underprice because they assume clients will dump unlimited work on them — set boundaries upfront.

Q: Should I still take projects while building retainers?

A: Absolutely. Take high-margin projects (₦200,000+) that don't interfere with retainer work, but stop chasing low-value project work (₦20,000–₦50,000) that eats your prospecting time. Retainers are your foundation; projects are bonus income. Once you have ₦400,000+ in monthly retainers, you can be selective about projects.

The numbers are clear: retainer work pays more reliably than project-based work in Nigeria, even if individual projects seem bigger.

Here's what you actually need to do:

  1. Accept that hunting for clients is costing you money. Unpaid prospecting time is the reason most freelancers earn ₦50,000–₦100,000/month despite having valuable skills.
  2. Build a system to find retainer clients. This means cold email outreach with targeted lead lists, not hoping Upwork delivers. Use Freelance LeadsHub to get verified business contacts, then Zaram Web Mailer to send bulk personalized pitches.

  4. Raise rates annually. Existing retainer clients accept 10–20% increases. New clients always come cheaper, so keep existing relationships.
