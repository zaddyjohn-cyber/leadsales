How to Pitch Clients as a Nigerian Freelancer: The Complete 2026 Guide

August 6, 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

Most Nigerian freelancers lose money competing on Upwork and Fiverr. They underbid. They chase every job. They never build a sustainable income.

The freelancers making ₦500,000–₦2,000,000 per month do something different: they pitch clients directly.

But here's the problem nobody talks about: how to pitch clients as a Nigerian freelancer when you're competing globally, dealing with time zone differences, currency gaps (the naira dropped to 1,550+ per dollar in 2026), and the constant friction of payment delays on traditional platforms.

This guide walks you through the exact process I've watched work for hundreds of Nigerian writers, designers, developers, and virtual assistants. You'll learn how to position yourself, what to say, how much to charge, and where to find the right businesses to pitch—without wasting time on low-paying gigs.

Why Pitching Clients Directly Beats Marketplace Platforms

Let's be honest: Upwork and Fiverr take 20–30% commission. You compete on price. Your profile gets buried under 10,000 other freelancers. The client sees "Nigeria" and assumes you'll work for ₦5,000/hour.

Direct pitching flips the game:

  • 100% of what you charge stays in your account (via Payoneer, Wise, or Grey) — no platform tax eating your profit
  • You control the narrative — your pitch, your rates, your terms
  • Better long-term clients — businesses hiring directly are usually willing to pay more than marketplace browsers
  • Recurring revenue — once you land a client, they often hire you monthly for ongoing work
  • Faster scaling — 3–5 good direct clients paying $1,500–$3,000/month beats 20 Upwork micro-gigs

The tradeoff? You have to find the businesses yourself and overcome the initial "cold outreach" friction. But that's exactly what this guide solves.

Step 1: Know Your Niche and Pricing in USD Terms

Nigerian freelancers often underprice because they think in naira. A designer charges ₦50,000 ($32 USD) for a logo because that feels like real money locally. A US client sees $32 and passes.

Here's what actually works:

Realistic Pricing by Niche (USD per Project or Monthly)

  • Copywriting/Content Writing: $300–$800/project or $1,200–$3,000/month for retainer
  • Graphic Design: $200–$600/project or $1,500–$4,000/month retainer
  • Virtual Assistant (admin/email/scheduling): $600–$1,500/month part-time
  • Web Development (WordPress/Webflow): $1,200–$5,000/project
  • Social Media Management: $800–$2,000/month for small business
  • Video Editing: $400–$1,200/month for YouTube channels
  • SEO/Marketing Consulting: $1,500–$3,000/month minimum

At the current 1,550 naira per dollar exchange rate, a $1,500/month retainer = ₦2,325,000. Even at 50% margin after tax and internet, that's ₦1,162,500 — enough to sustain yourself while building a second or third client.

Money Math: Most successful Nigerian freelancers target 3–5 clients at $1,000–$2,500/month each rather than 15 clients at $100–$300. The first approach gives you ₦3.87M–₦9.675M monthly (before tax) vs. chaos and burnout.

How to Justify Your Rate to a Potential Client

When you pitch, never lead with price. Lead with results. Your pitch should sound like:

"I help SaaS companies reduce CAC by 35% through high-converting landing page copy. My last 3 clients saw average revenue lift of $12,000/month. I charge $1,500/month for ongoing copywriting, but we can start with a $2,000 test project on your homepage."

Not: "Hi, I do copywriting for $1,500."

The difference is specificity and proof. If you don't have a portfolio, build one with 3 spec projects (free work on real businesses) and use real results to anchor your rate.

Step 3: Write a Pitch Email That Actually Gets Responses

Your email is 8 seconds. Most freelancers waste it.

They write: "Hi, I'm a freelancer. I do [service]. Let me know if you need help." Delete. Spam folder. Next.

Here's the structure that works:

The 5-Part Cold Pitch Formula

  1. Subject line with specificity (not "Quick Question"):
    • ❌ "Interested in Copywriting?"
    • ✅ "Copywriting That Converts: 3 Ideas for [Company Name]"
  2. Open with their problem, not your service:
    • "Most e-commerce brands lose 70% of visitors on their product page. I noticed [Company] gets decent traffic but your product description is pretty thin."
  3. One proof point (not a resume):
    • "I helped [Type of Business] increase product page conversions by 23% with revised copy + micro-copy tweaks."
  4. Specific next step (not vague "Let's chat"):
    • "I'd like to spend 20 mins auditing your top 3 product pages and share 5 specific copy changes you can test this week. No charge for the audit."
  5. Social proof or credibility anchor:
    • "I've worked with 15+ DTC brands in the supplement space over 3 years. Portfolio: [link]"

Cold Email Template for Nigerian Freelancers

Here's a template you can customize by niche. For more templates, check out our Cold Email Templates for Nigerian Freelancers guide.

Subject: [Company Name] + [Specific Opportunity]

Hi [First Name],

I came across [Company Name]'s work with [specific thing I noticed on their site]. One thing I noticed: [specific problem or gap related to your service].

I've helped [similar companies] solve this exact problem by [your solution in plain language]. For example, [One quantified result: "increased click-through by 18%" or "reduced response time from 48 hrs to 4 hrs"].

I'm not looking for a long-term commitment yet. I'd like to offer a small, no-risk way to test this: [Specific small deliverable: "a 500-word landing page rewrite" or "a 10-post content calendar for Q3"].

Cost: [If free: "This is free—I want to show you the value first." If paid: "$[small amount] to start"]

Are you open to exploring this?

Cheers,
[Your Name]
[Your website/portfolio]

Key rules:

  • Keep it under 100 words. People read on mobile. Paragraph breaks every 2 sentences.
  • Use their name. "Hi there" gets deleted. "Hi Sarah" gets read.
  • Mention something specific about their business. Proves you didn't just mail-merge 1,000 people.
  • Lead with benefit, not credentials. They don't care you worked on 20 projects. They care if you can increase their revenue.
  • Offer something small first. Free audit, sample social posts, wireframe, etc. Lowers the barrier to "yes."

Send this email Monday–Thursday, 9 AM their time zone (use a tool that auto-adjusts). Skip Fridays and weekends—decision makers aren't in office.

Step 4: Handle Objections and Close the Sale

Most pitches die in the follow-up. The client doesn't see your email. Or they do but aren't ready to decide. Then you disappear.

Instead, use a 3-touch follow-up sequence (built into Zaram Web Mailer for automated sending):

  • Day 1: Initial pitch
  • Day 5: Gentle follow-up: "Just in case this got buried—here's the offer again + a small new piece of value (e.g., a 1-page case study)"
  • Day 10: Final touch: "Last time reaching out—link to a helpful resource or alternative if now isn't the right time"

Common objections and how to handle them:

"How much does it cost?"

Never quote cold. Say: "Depends on scope. The audit I mentioned takes me about 2 hours ($250). The full engagement would be $1,500/month. But let's talk about your specific goals first—cost scales with what you need."

"We use a freelancer already."

Ask: "Is it working well?" Then: "My offer isn't to replace them—it's to test one project with us, see if there's a fit. If not, no hard feelings. If yes, you've got options now."

"We don't have budget right now."

Reply: "I get it. When does Q3 budget planning happen? I'd rather touch base then than bother you now. Is late July a good time to follow up?"

"We need references/portfolio first."

Send a portfolio link + 2 case studies (specific numbers: "Increased email open rate from 18% to 31%") + say: "I've also worked with [Company Name] and [Company Name]—happy to have them chat if needed."

Step 5: Set Up Payment to Avoid Naira Stress

You land the client. Great. Now you need to get paid.

The standard flow for Nigerian freelancers:

  • Client pays via Stripe, PayPal, or bank transfer to your US/UK business account
  • You receive USD in Payoneer, Wise, or Grey
  • Convert to NGN at the best rate (Wise usually beats Payoneer by 2–3% on rate)
  • Withdraw to your Nigerian bank account (usually within 24 hours)

Real numbers: A client owes you $1,500. At 1,550 naira per dollar, that's ₦2,325,000. Wise charges ~1.5% fee = $22.50 loss = ₦34,875. You net ₦2,290,125.

Never ask for payment in naira. If a client insists, add 20% to your USD rate to cover their bank conversion fees.

Pro tip: Invoice in USD. Set payment terms as "NET 15" (payment within 15 days). Use a tool like Invoice Ninja or Wave to automate reminders. Most US/UK businesses expect 30-day terms but will pay faster if you don't chase.

The Lead-to-Client Conversion Timeline

Realistic expectations:

  • Day 1–2: Email sent. 15–20% open rate (depends on subject line quality)
  • Day 3–5: First responses come in. Maybe 2–3% response rate (normal)
  • Day 5–8: Follow-up sent. Another 0.5–1% response rate from people who missed first email
  • Day 8–15: Sales conversations happen. Close 5–10% of people who respond
  • Day 15–30: Project starts. Payment received (usually within 15 days of invoice)

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is cold emailing legal in Nigeria and does it violate CAN-SPAM rules?

A: Yes, it's legal when done right. CAN-SPAM (US law) requires you to: (1) include a legitimate business address in your email footer, (2) include an unsubscribe link, (3) honor unsubscribe requests within 10 days. Nigerian laws (NITDA guidelines) don't prohibit cold outreach, but email service providers like Gmail will penalize you if spam complaints rise above 0.3%. Use Zaram Web Mailer because it's built for compliance—it handles unsubscribes automatically and monitors bounce rates to protect your sender reputation.

Q: What if I don't have a portfolio yet? How do I pitch?

A: Build one immediately. Offer 3 free projects to real small businesses (not friends) in exchange for results and testimonials. For example: offer to rewrite 5 social media posts for a local Lagos juice bar, track engagement for 2 weeks, document the results. Then use that case study in your pitch to new clients. Most clients will forgive "new freelancer" if you show one solid before/after example.

Q: How many pitches should I send per day without getting flagged as spam?

A: With Zaram Web Mailer, you can send 50–100 emails per day safely (it spreads sends over hours and monitors your sender reputation). Without it, stick to 20–30 from your Gmail to avoid automatic flags. Quality matters more than volume—100 bad pitches to random people = 0 responses. 20 good pitches to the right niche = 1–2 quality leads.

Q: Can I pitch multiple times if I didn't get a response the first time?

A: Yes, if you space them out (5–10 days minimum) and change your angle. First email: "I noticed [problem] and have a solution." Follow-up (day 5): "Just in case this got missed—here's a free resource." Third touch (day 12): "I'll stop here, but if you're open to exploring [service] in the future, let me know." Never harass. If they don't respond after 3 touches, let them go. Move on to the next 50 leads.
