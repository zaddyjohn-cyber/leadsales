How to Get Agency Clients as a Freelancer in Nigeria: A Complete 2026 Guide

August 26, 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

Most Nigerian freelancers are stuck on Upwork, Fiverr, and Freelancer grinding out projects for ₦5,000–₦15,000 while competing against 500 other bidders. But the money is actually with agency clients—marketing agencies, web design studios, software development shops, and branding firms that need reliable freelancers to handle overflow work or specialized skills.

In this guide, I'll show you exactly how to get agency clients as a freelancer in Nigeria, with real numbers, actual timelines, and the tools that actually work.

Why Agency Clients Pay More (And More Reliably)

Before we talk strategy, let's be clear on why this matters.

An agency client typically pays you:

  • ₦50,000–₦200,000+ per month on retainers (not one-off projects)
  • Monthly recurring income that you can count on
  • Faster payment cycles—agencies have cash flow and pay on time
  • Multiple projects from one client (more stable than juggling 10 small clients)
  • Better feedback—they know what they want because they work with clients daily

Compare that to Upwork:

  • ₦8,000–₦20,000 per project (if you're lucky)
  • Upwork takes 5–20% commission
  • No guarantee of repeat work
  • Payment disputes are common
  • You're competing on price, not value

A freelancer earning ₦150,000/month from 2–3 agency retainers is far ahead of someone making ₦200,000 from 15 small Upwork gigs. The former has predictable income, free time to grow, and stress-free payments via Wise or Payoneer.

Real Numbers: A Nigerian graphic designer working with 2 agency clients at ₦75,000/month each earns ₦150,000 reliably. In USD at current rates (≈₦1,550/$1), that's ~$97. Over 12 months with zero gaps, that's $1,164—more than most Upwork portfolios will generate, and with 10x less stress.

Step 1: Identify Your Niche (And Stick to It)

Agencies don't hire generalists. They hire specialists.

An agency owner thinks like this: "We need a Facebook ads specialist because we do 5–10 campaigns per month" or "We need a WordPress developer because 30% of our client projects are sites."

If you tell an agency, "I do copywriting, design, video, and social media," they'll assume you're mediocre at all of it. If you say, "I specialize in Shopify store optimization and conversion rate testing," you're suddenly credible and hireable.

Pick one of these high-demand niches for Nigerian agencies:

  • Facebook/Google Ads Management
  • WordPress Development (especially WooCommerce stores)
  • Copywriting (especially sales pages and email sequences)
  • Shopify Store Setup & Optimization
  • Social Media Content Creation (reels, TikTok, Instagram)
  • Customer Service/Ticketing Support
  • SEO & Content Strategy
  • Figma UI/UX Design
  • Video Editing (especially for ads and YouTube)
  • Email Marketing & Automation

Why these? Because Nigerian agencies are either selling these services to their clients or running their own marketing. They need people. Read our guide on getting social media management clients if that's your direction.

Step 2: Build Proof (Your Portfolio & Case Studies)

Agencies won't hire you on potential. They want proof you've done the work before.

Here's what you need:

  1. 3–5 Case Studies showing before/after results. If you have real clients (even at low rates), document the results. Screenshots, numbers, testimonials.
  2. One Solid Portfolio Website (even a simple Carrd or Webflow site works). This signals professionalism.
  3. Social Proof: LinkedIn recommendations, Google reviews, or client testimonials on your site.
  4. A Clear Rate Card showing what you charge and what's included (more on this below).

You don't need 20 projects. You need 3–5 strong ones with measurable results. If you're just starting and haven't worked with real clients yet, do a few discounted projects for small businesses (even at cost) just to get case studies built.

Step 4: Craft Your Cold Email (The Message That Gets Responses)

Once you have the list, you need to send emails that get opened and replied to.

Your email structure:

  1. Subject line (short & specific): "Help with [their common problem]?" or "Quick idea for [Agency Name]'s [service]"
  2. Opening (2 sentences max): Show you researched them. "I noticed you're running a lot of Shopify client projects. I specialize in store optimization and saw an opportunity."
  3. The offer (3–4 sentences): What do you do? For agencies, mention overflow support. "I work with [similar agency type] to handle [specific task]—so your team stays focused on client strategy."
  4. Social proof (1 sentence): "Last quarter, I helped an agency increase store conversion rates by 23%."
  5. Call to action (very clear): "Are you open to a quick 15-min chat about how I can support your team?" NOT "Let me know what you think."
  6. Signature: Name, title, phone number, website.

Real example:

Subject: Help with Shopify scaling at [Agency Name]?

Hi [Name],

I noticed [Agency Name] recently launched 3 new Shopify stores for clients. I specialize in post-launch optimization and conversion testing—basically, I help agencies get 20–40% more revenue from stores without extra ad spend.

I work with studios that need overflow support so the core team stays on strategy. Last quarter, I helped a similar agency increase their average store conversion rate from 1.8% to 2.2%—that's an extra ₦800k+ yearly per store.

Would you be open to a 15-min call to explore if there's a fit?

[Your name]
[Your phone on WhatsApp]
[Your website]

This works because it:

  • Shows you did homework (you mentioned their actual work)
  • Focuses on their pain (overflow/scaling)
  • Offers a specific skill (not "I'm a freelancer")
  • Includes proof (a real result)
  • Makes a simple ask (15 mins, not "hire me")

Step 5: Follow Up (The 3-Email Sequence)

Most freelancers send one email and give up. Agencies are busy. They might not see it, or they saw it but didn't have time to respond.

Your follow-up sequence:

  1. Email 1 (Day 1): Your main pitch (from above)
  2. Email 2 (Day 5): Short and soft. "Hi [Name], just following up on my note below. No pressure—just thought it was worth a conversation. Cheers."
  3. Email 3 (Day 12): Add value. Share a relevant article, case study, or industry insight. "Came across this article on [relevant topic] and thought of your team. Check it out when you have time."

If no response after 3 emails over 2 weeks, move on. Some won't respond. But about 3–5% will engage, and that's enough to build a pipeline.

Track all of this in a spreadsheet. Learn how to use Google Sheets to organize your cold email campaigns—it keeps you accountable and shows which agencies are warm leads.

Step 6: Set Your Pricing (Retainers, Not Projects)

Agencies don't want to hire you for one-off projects. They want ongoing support. That means retainers.

How to price retainers:

Don't charge hourly. Estimate monthly work hours and multiply by your rate.

Example: If you're a Facebook Ads specialist:

  • Estimated work: 20 hours/month managing 2–3 campaigns, optimization, reporting
  • Your hourly rate: ₦5,000–₦8,000 (if you're solid)
  • Monthly retainer: 20 hours × ₦6,000 = ₦120,000/month

Undercut this? No. Overshoot it? Maybe, if you're expert-level. A typical Nigerian agency paying freelancers expects to pay ₦60,000–₦250,000/month depending on skill and demand.

At ₦120,000/month × 12 months, that's ₦1.44M yearly from one client. That's life-changing money in Nigeria. And you can take 2–3 of these simultaneously and hit ₦300k–₦500k/month.

Step 7: Close the Deal (What to Say on the Call)

You got a reply. They want to chat. Here's how to convert:

  1. Listen first. Ask about their current challenges, client types, and what they're struggling with. Don't pitch yet.
  2. Match your offer to their problem. "Okay, so you're managing 5 Shopify clients but struggling with post-launch optimization. That's exactly what I do."
  3. Show past work. Share your case study. "Here's how I increased conversion rates for a similar agency. Took 30 days."
  4. Define the scope. "For your team, I'm thinking 15–20 hours/month doing [specific tasks]. That frees up your developer for higher-level stuff."
  5. Confirm the rate. "My rate for this is ₦120,000/month, paid via Wise/Payoneer on the 25th. Does that work?"
  6. Close simple. "Great. I'll send a one-page agreement via email. Once you sign, I'll start [specific date]."

Don't overcomplicate it. Most Nigerian agencies operate on handshakes and email agreements, not contracts. Keep it simple and professional.

Step 8: Deliver & Renew

Once you're hired, your job is simple: do excellent work, communicate regularly, and make their life easier.

After 3 months, have a brief check-in: "Hey, how's the partnership going? Any feedback?" This signals you care and opens the door to rate increases or expanded scope after 6–12 months.

Most agencies that hire freelancers keep them for 2+ years if they're reliable. That's your path to ₦200k–₦500k/month stable income.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What if I'm a complete beginner with no portfolio?

A: Do 2–3 small projects at a discount (even free, if needed) for real businesses. Get measurable results and testimonials. Then you have proof. Most agencies care about results, not credentials. A beginner with a ₦200,000 portfolio project beats someone with "10 years experience" but no real results to show.

Q: How many agencies should I contact in my first campaign?

A: Start with 50–100 in your first week. Expect a 3–5% response rate (so 1.5–5 replies). After 200 emails, you should have 8–10 warm leads. Prioritize quality over volume—research each agency briefly before emailing.

Q: Can I work with multiple agencies at the same time?

A: Yes. 2–3 retainers at 15–20 hours/month each is realistic. That's 30–60 hours/month total, leaving room to grow. Just make sure there are no conflicts of interest (don't work for competing agencies in the same city or niche).

Q: What if an agency wants to negotiate my rate down?

A: It's normal. You can negotiate scope instead of price. Say: "I can do ₦100k/month but that's 10 hours. Or we can do ₦120k for 15 hours and I'll handle [extra task]." Giving them options feels less like rejection and more like collaboration.
