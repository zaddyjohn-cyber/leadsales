Step 3: Clean and Segment Your List

Once you download your leads, spend 30 minutes cleaning the data:

  • Remove duplicates
  • Delete any entries with obviously fake or incomplete emails
  • Group by industry or by how relevant they are to your service
  • Prioritize: which 50–100 companies do you most want to work with?

Start with your top 100 most relevant leads. This isn't about volume—it's about precision. A personalized email to 100 real prospects will get you more clients than a generic blast to 5,000.

Step 4: Craft Your Cold Email Pitch

This is critical. Your email should:

  1. Reference something specific about their business. "I noticed your e-commerce store is getting [traffic stat] but your checkout page doesn't have live chat"
  2. Show you've done your homework. Mention a competitor doing well, a gap in their market, or a problem you've solved for similar businesses
  3. Make a specific offer. "I've helped 3 other fashion retailers increase checkout completion by 18%. Would a quick 15-min audit be useful?"
  4. Keep it short. 50–100 words max. Most people skim emails on their phone

Common Mistakes Nigerian Freelancers Make with Lead Databases

Mistake 1: Buying a List and Sending Generic Pitches

A lead database only works if you personalize. Generic "We offer web design!" emails get 0.5% response rates at best. Personalized, specific pitches get 8–15%. Spend the extra 2 minutes mentioning their company, a specific problem, or a competitor comparison.

Mistake 2: Not Following Up

First email gets ignored. Second email (3 days later) often gets a response. You need to send 3–5 follow-ups. That's why Zaram Web Mailer is valuable—it automates this so you don't have to manually follow up 200 times.

Mistake 3: Not Qualifying Your List

Downloading 50,000 random leads and spamming them all wastes time and damages your sender reputation. Start with 100–500 of your most relevant, highest-intent leads. Test your pitch on them first. Once you see what works, scale up.

Mistake 4: Pitching Too Soon

Don't lead with a sales pitch. Lead with a specific problem you've noticed or solved. "I noticed your site doesn't have a contact form" is way better than "I can build websites." Give value first, ask for the meeting second.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is cold emailing Nigerian businesses legal or considered spam?

A: Cold emailing is legal in Nigeria. As long as you're sending unsolicited business emails to verified business emails and decision-makers—not blasting random addresses or misleading subject lines—it's legitimate B2B outreach. In fact, many Nigerian businesses expect and welcome cold outreach. Where it becomes illegal is if you're sending bulk emails without unsubscribe options or using deceptive practices. Best practice: include a simple unsubscribe line in your emails, keep subject lines honest, and send real personalized pitches, not spam blasts. Tools like Zaram Web Mailer are built with deliverability standards that keep you compliant.

Q: How much should I charge for projects I land through direct outreach?

A: 2–3x more than Upwork rates. If you charge ₦8,000–₦15,000 on Upwork, aim for ₦30,000–₦60,000 for direct clients. Here's why: direct clients expect professionalism, exclusivity, and direct communication—they're willing to pay. A business with budget already allocated for a project values speed and quality over price. Start with ₦50,000 for a small project and adjust based on feedback. You'll find clients are often comfortable paying more for someone who pitched them directly and understood their specific problem. For retainers, typical rates are ₦100,000–₦500,000/month depending on scope.

Q: What if I get a low response rate from my first lead list?

A: Don't panic—test before you scale. A 5% response rate (10 responses from 200 emails) is actually solid. If you're getting 1–2% or less, try these fixes: (1) Rewrite your pitch to be more specific and personalized. (2) Check that your sender email looks legitimate (not a generic Gmail account). (3) Make sure you're targeting the right decision-maker for your service. (4) Test a different segment of the database (maybe you're pitching e-commerce stores when you should target agencies, etc.). (5) If you're using Gmail, you might be hitting spam filters—switch to a professional domain email. Most freelancers don't see good response rates until they've tested 2–3 different pitches and refined their targeting.

Q: Can I use a lead database for services other than web design?

A: Absolutely. Lead databases work for copywriting, social media management, VA services, bookkeeping, video editing, graphic design—any B2B service. The key is segmenting your list by industry. If you do copywriting for e-commerce stores, filter your database to show only e-commerce businesses. If you do bookkeeping, filter by accounting-related keywords. The more specific your targeting, the higher your response rate. A database like Freelance LeadsHub lets you download multiple lists for different niches, so you can test what works best for your skillset.
