The reason? Business owners in Nigeria are underserved. They're running operations, chasing sales, managing staff—they're not trawling freelance sites. They need someone to pick up the phone and say "I can fix this."

Method 1: LinkedIn (Free, Takes Time)

LinkedIn is the goldmine nobody talks about in Nigeria. Yes, Nigerians are on WhatsApp and Facebook, but LinkedIn is where decision-makers are—especially in services, tech, e-commerce, and finance.

How to do it:

  1. Search for businesses in your niche (e.g., "logistics company Lagos," "marketing agency Nigeria")
  2. Filter by company size (10–50 employees = usually owner-reachable)
  3. Find the founder or CEO in the company page
  4. Click their profile → look for phone number in "Contact Info" section
  5. If not listed, click "Message" and send a personalized 2-sentence intro

Pro tip: Many business owners list their WhatsApp number on LinkedIn. Click the phone icon—it often opens WhatsApp directly.

Cost: Free (or ₦3,500/month for LinkedIn Premium to see more details)

Reality check: This works but it's slow. You'll spend 2–3 hours finding 10 solid leads. And not everyone replies. But for niche targeting? LinkedIn is precise.

Method 2: Google Maps & Business Directories (Free, Quick)

If you want phone numbers fast, Google Maps and Nigerian business directories are unbeatable.

Steps:

  1. Open Google Maps → search for your target business type and location (e.g., "digital marketing agencies Lagos," "accounting firms Abuja")
  2. Click each business result → phone number is usually right there
  3. WhatsApp them directly (most business numbers in Nigeria have WhatsApp)

Nigerian directories that work:

  • Jiji.ng – huge classified site, business owners list contact info
  • Nairaland.com – especially the business forum, where owners post their numbers
  • Yellow Pages Nigeria (yellowpagesnigeria.com) – actually has verified business listings
  • 101.com.ng – another classified platform with business contacts

Cost: Free

Downside: You're doing this manually. 50 leads takes 45 minutes to an hour. If you're targeting hundreds of businesses, this gets tedious fast.

Method 3: Facebook Business Pages (Free, Underrated)

Most Nigerian business owners are on Facebook. They post their phone number right on their business page. This is genuinely overlooked.

How:

  1. Search "[Your niche] + [Location]" on Facebook (e.g., "bookkeeping services Lagos")
  2. Filter results to show Pages
  3. Click the page → scroll to "Contact Info" at the top
  4. Phone number is usually there. WhatsApp link sometimes too.

Bonus: You can see their posting activity. Are they posting weekly? Active engagement? Good—they're actually running the business, not dormant. If they haven't posted in 6 months, skip them.

Cost: Free

Method 4: Instagram Business Profiles (Free, Good for Creative Services)

If you're targeting e-commerce brands, salons, fashion, events, or content creators, Instagram is loaded with business owners who list their WhatsApp or phone number in their bio.

How:

  1. Search hashtags relevant to your niche (e.g., #LagosEvents, #NigeriaEcommerce, #AbujaBeautyBrand)
  2. Click profiles that look professional (verified tick, consistent posts, engagement)
  3. Check the bio → many have "DM for inquiries" or direct links to WhatsApp
  4. Click the WhatsApp link or DM them

Cost: Free

Key insight: Instagram business owners in Nigeria respond faster than LinkedIn because they're used to casual, quick conversations. You can pitch on WhatsApp same day, and they'll usually respond within hours if interested.

Method 5: Industry Events & Networking Groups (Mixed Cost)

Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt—every city has business forums, chamber meetings, trade shows, and networking events. This is old school but works.

Where to look:

  • Lagos Chamber of Commerce – they have directories and monthly meetings
  • Local business groups – Facebook groups like "Lagos Business Owners," "Nairobi Entrepreneurs Nigeria," etc.
  • Industry associations – e.g., Association of Nigerian Photographers, Digital Marketers Association Nigeria
  • Co-working spaces – Workspace, The Hive, etc. host founder meetups where you can network in person

Cost: Event tickets usually ₦5,000–₦15,000; networking is free in groups

Reality: You'll meet people, get WhatsApp numbers, build relationships. But it takes time and energy. Most Nigerian freelancers skip this because it feels "old" compared to digital methods. That's exactly why it works—less competition.

How to Use Phone Numbers Once You Have Them

Finding phone numbers is step one. Converting them into clients is step two. Here's the playbook:

Step 1: WhatsApp First (90% of Nigerians use WhatsApp)

Don't call cold. WhatsApp first with a 2-sentence message:

"Hi [Name], I noticed [company name] does [X service]. I help [similar companies] by [specific result]. Would you be open to a quick chat this week?"

Send at 10 AM on weekdays. Avoid nights and weekends when businesses aren't checking messages.

Step 2: Follow Up with Cold Email

If WhatsApp doesn't work in 3 days, send an email. You now have both channels. Once you have your lead list downloaded, load it into Zaram Web Mailer — it handles bulk sending, personalization, and follow-ups automatically so your emails don't hit spam filters. This way you can reach 500 people in one afternoon without manually typing each email.

Step 3: Call Only After Engagement

If they reply on WhatsApp or email, that's when you call. Cold calling without context? Low response. Calling someone who's already engaged? High close rate.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Buying lists from untrusted vendors: You'll get dead numbers, wrong people, or spam. Use verified sources like Freelance LeadsHub or your own research.
  • Generic messages: "Hi, I'm a freelancer. Can I work for you?" Delete. Mention something specific about their business in your first message.
  • Spamming the same person on multiple platforms: WhatsApp + email + Instagram in one day = annoying. Space it out by 2–3 days.
  • Not qualifying leads: Check if they can actually afford you. A solopreneur doesn't have ₦500,000/month for a marketing manager. A 20-person company does.
  • Giving up too fast: It takes 4–7 touches before most people respond. Send your message once, follow up twice, then move on. Don't spend 10 emails on one person.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is it legal to contact business owners from phone lists in Nigeria?

A: Yes. In Nigeria, B2B cold outreach is generally legal and accepted. Business phone numbers are public information—that's why they're listed on Google, Facebook, and Yellow Pages. What matters is your message: be professional, respect their time, and stop if they ask. Spamming is different from outreach. One person asking about services = professional. Sending 100 unsolicited sales messages daily = spam. Stay on the professional side and you're fine.

Q: What's the best time to contact business owners in Nigeria?

A: Tuesday through Thursday, 10 AM–2 PM Lagos time (9 AM–1 PM GMT). Business owners are checking messages during their morning routine and mid-day break. Avoid Mondays (chaos), Fridays after 3 PM (checking out mentally), and weekends entirely. If you're using automated tools like Zaram Web Mailer, schedule sends for 10 AM so they land in their priority inbox, not buried below 50 other messages.

Q: How many leads should I contact per week to get real results?

A: 100–200 per week minimum if you're serious. At a 3% reply rate, that's 3–6 replies per week. From 3–6 replies, you might qualify 1–2 as real prospects. From 1–2 prospects, you might close 1 client per month initially. Volume matters. If you're only contacting 10–20 people per week, you're not giving yourself enough opportunity to hit the law of averages. That's why a targeted lead list beats manual Googling—you can reach hundreds in the time it'd take to find 30 manually.

Q: Should I use my personal WhatsApp or business WhatsApp for outreach?

A: Personal WhatsApp is fine starting out (and most successful Nigerian freelancers use it). But as you scale, get a business line—either a second SIM in Nigeria (costs ₦3,000–₦5,000), or use WhatsApp Business app (free). This separates your work from personal life and looks more professional. Also, if your personal number gets blocked or reported, your real contacts stay safe. As you grow and hire a team, they can use their own numbers too. Simple to scale.
