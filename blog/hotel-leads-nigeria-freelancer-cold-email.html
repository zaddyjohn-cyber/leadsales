Compare that to what you'd earn on Upwork: ₦10,000–₦30,000 per project, competing against freelancers from Pakistan and the Philippines willing to work for pennies.

Hotels and restaurants in Lagos, Abuja, Enugu, Kano, and Port Harcourt are actively looking for freelancers. They need someone to manage their Instagram, write guest blog posts, fix their website, or set up email sequences to fill rooms during low season. These are recurring contracts, not one-off gigs.

Real Example: A mid-range hotel in VI, Lagos (100–150 rooms) doing ₦2M+ in monthly revenue doesn't have an in-house marketing team. They'll pay ₦250,000–₦400,000/month to a freelancer who can manage their booking funnel, email campaigns, and social content. That's a contract that lasts 6–12 months. Do 3–4 of those simultaneously, and you're earning ₦750K–₦1.2M monthly (roughly $1,500–$2,400 USD at current rates).

Step-by-Step Cold Email Strategy for Hotel Leads

Step 1: Download Your Lead List

Go to freelanceleadshub.shop, select "Hotels & Hospitality" as your niche and "Nigeria" as your country. Choose the Growth Plan (17,000 leads for ₦40,000) if you're committed. Download the CSV file with email addresses, names, phone numbers, and websites.

Step 2: Segment Your List by Hotel Type

Not all hotels are the same. You'll get better response rates if you segment:

  • Luxury 5-star hotels (Lagos VI, Ikoyi, Abuja) — likely have in-house teams but need specialized services
  • Mid-range hotels (3–4 stars) — most responsive, have budgets, but less internal support
  • Budget hotels & guesthouses — large volume, often owner-operated, quick decision-making
  • Resorts & leisure — need seasonal promotions, highly responsive
  • Restaurants & event venues — smaller budgets but faster turnaround

Start with mid-range hotels and resorts. They have the best ROI—they're responsive, have real budgets, and make decisions faster than luxury chains.

Step 3: Craft Your Cold Email

Your email should be short, specific, and show you understand their business. Here's a template:

Subject: Quick idea for [Hotel Name]'s bookings

Hi [Owner/Manager Name],

I work with 3–4 hotels in Nigeria on their booking funnels and email marketing. One client in Lagos went from 12 bookings/month to 28 just by fixing their email sequences and Google Ads strategy.

I noticed [Hotel Name] doesn't have an email retargeting system for past guests. That's money left on the table—especially during low season.

If it makes sense, I'd like to do a quick 15-min audit and show you exactly what's missing (no pitch, just insights).

Available this week?

[Your name]
+234 [phone]

Notice: It's short (4 sentences), mentions a real result, shows you researched them, and asks for a specific next step (15-min call).

What to Pitch: High-Demand Services for Hotels

Not sure what service to offer? Here are the most profitable for hotel clients:

Social Media Management (Instagram & Facebook)

Most hotels post randomly—no strategy, no consistency. You can charge ₦150,000–₦300,000/month to post 4–5x per week, engage with comments, and run simple ads for their best rooms. Tools like Buffer or Later make this scalable.

Email Marketing & Booking Funnels

Hotels collect emails from past guests but don't use them. Build a simple Mailchimp or ConvertKit funnel that re-engages past guests, promotes off-season deals, and recovers abandoned bookings. Charge ₦200,000–₦400,000 for setup + ₦100,000–₦150,000/month for management.

Website Optimization & Copywriting

Many hotel websites are slow, confusing, or have weak conversion copy. A freelancer who can rewrite the homepage, improve booking page clarity, and fix site speed can charge ₦250,000–₦600,000 for a one-time project.

Google Ads & SEO

Hotels lose bookings to competitors ranking higher on Google. If you know SEO or Google Ads, you can charge ₦150,000–₦400,000/month to get them more bookings. Even a 30% increase in bookings justifies your fee immediately.

Common Objections & How to Handle Them

"I'm not experienced enough"

Lie. You have 1–2 demo projects or past work. Build a portfolio with 2–3 before you start cold emailing. Hotel managers don't ask for 10 years of experience—they ask "Can you deliver results?" Show them case studies (even if they're your own projects or test runs).

"Email bounces will ruin my sender reputation"

It won't. Zaram has built-in verification that filters out bad emails before sending. If you follow their best practices (warm-up period, rate limits, clean list), you'll maintain a 95%+ delivery rate. Nigerian ISPs are lenient compared to US/UK networks.

"Hotels are too big/corporate to respond to cold email"

Wrong. Mid-range hotels (3–4 stars) and resorts actively respond to cold email. They're small enough that decisions happen fast, big enough that they have budgets. That's the sweet spot.

"I don't have a professional website"

Don't need one to start. Your portfolio and email are enough. Once you close your first 2–3 clients, invest ₦80,000–₦150,000 in a basic portfolio site. But it's not a blocker.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How many hotel leads should I download to start?

A: Start with the Growth Plan (17,000 leads for ₦40,000). You'll segment by location and hotel type, so you'll realistically send to about 1,500–3,000 of the highest-fit leads in your first campaign. The rest stay in your database for future outreach or to test different angles. Don't waste money on 30,000 leads until you've validated that cold email works for you.

Q: What's the best response rate I should expect from cold email to hotels?

A: With a decent email (personalized, showing you know their business) and a good list, expect 1–3% response rate. That means from 1,000 emails sent, you'll get 10–30 replies. Not all replies are sales-ready; maybe 5–10 actually book a call. From that, you'll close 1–2 deals. This is normal and healthy for B2B cold email in Nigeria.

Q: Should I call hotels directly instead of emailing?

A: No. Cold calling in Nigeria often annoys business owners—they're busy, and an unexpected call feels intrusive. Email is professional, leaves a paper trail, and they can respond when convenient. However, once they reply interested, call them the same day. That's when the phone is powerful.

Q: How long until I make my first sale from cold email?

A: 2–6 weeks from your first batch of emails, assuming good targeting and follow-ups. Some people close deals within 48 hours; others take 4–5 weeks of back-and-forth before deciding. The key is consistency—keep sending daily, following up properly, and qualifying leads. Don't expect results after 3 emails then give up.
