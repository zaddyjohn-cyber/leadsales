How to Find Business Owners on Instagram Nigeria: The Complete Cold Outreach Guide

24 August 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

You're scrolling through Instagram, seeing businesses post every day. You think: "I could help these people. Why am I competing with thousands of freelancers on Upwork for ₦5,000 projects when I could pitch directly to business owners?"

That's the right instinct. But finding business owners on Instagram Nigeria who actually need your service—and have the budget to pay you properly—is harder than it looks. Random Instagram DMs convert at less than 1%. Generic comments on posts get ignored. And you waste hours searching when you could be closing deals.

This guide shows you exactly how to find business owners on Instagram Nigeria, the real conversion rates you should expect, and why the smartest Nigerian freelancers are moving away from manual searching to targeted lead lists.

Why Instagram for Finding Business Owners in Nigeria?

Instagram isn't just for influencers anymore. In Nigeria, Instagram is where small and mid-sized business owners spend their time—fashion retailers, event planners, real estate agents, photographers, coaches, and service providers. They post daily, engage with customers, and crucially, they show their contact information in their bio.

Unlike Fiverr or Upwork where you're competing on price with thousands of freelancers offering ₦3,000–₦10,000 projects, Instagram gives you direct access to decision-makers. A fashion retailer with 15,000 followers who needs help with product photography or Instagram content creation might pay ₦150,000–₦500,000 (or $200–$600 USD equivalent) for a proper project. That's real money.

The catch: You have to find them, filter them, and pitch them the right way. Let's break down how.

Method 1: Manual Instagram Search & Hashtag Mining

This is free but time-consuming. Here's the honest breakdown:

Step 1: Identify Your Niche Hashtags

If you're a content creator, photographer, or social media manager, search hashtags that business owners in your target industry actually use. Examples:

  • Fashion/E-commerce: #NigerianFashion, #LagoseBusiness, #NaijaFashionBrand, #NigerianOnlineBusiness, #SMEsNigeria
  • Real Estate: #LagosProperty, #NigerianRealEstate, #PropertiesForSaleNigeria, #LandForSaleNigeria
  • Events: #NigerianEventPlanner, #LagosEvents, #EventsNigeria, #WeddingPlannerLagos
  • Photography: #NigerianPhotographer, #WeddingPhotographyNigeria, #ProductPhotography
  • Coaching/Services: #BusinessCoachNigeria, #NigerianCoach, #ServiceProviderNigeria

Step 2: Filter by Engagement & Business Signals

Don't just collect random accounts. Look for:

  • Accounts with 2,000–100,000 followers (sweet spot for service-based businesses with real budget)
  • Posts with consistent engagement (50+ likes per post = they're active and have real audience)
  • Bio includes website link, email, or phone number (they're open to business)
  • Posted in the last 7 days (they're still active, not abandoned accounts)
  • Content that shows they're offering paid services (not just hobby posting)

Step 3: Manually Record Contacts

For each qualified account, note down:

  • Business name
  • Instagram handle
  • Email (from bio or website)
  • Phone (from bio if available)
  • Website URL
  • Service type

This works, but realistically you'll spend 2–3 hours finding and verifying 20–30 contacts. That's ₦0 earned while you're searching.

The Manual Search Reality: One Nigerian freelancer we spoke to spent 4 hours finding 45 Instagram business owners, manually. He got 3 replies, 1 conversion = ₦80,000 project. His hourly rate for that search? ₦20,000/hour if the deal closes. But you need volume to make that math work consistently.

Method 2: Use Instagram's Built-in Business Tools (Limited But Free)

Instagram Business Account Features

If you're pitching to businesses, make sure you also have an Instagram Business Account (not Creator Account). Switch in Settings → Account Type. This lets business owners see:

  • Your category (Photographer, Content Creator, etc.)
  • Your contact button (phone/email/message CTA)
  • Your link in bio prominently displayed

Instagram Location Tags & Business Category Filters

Instagram's built-in search does have some filtering, but it's limited. You can search by:

  • Location: Search "Lagos" or "Ibadan" and filter by businesses
  • Category: Some accounts are tagged as "Professional Services," "Fashion Brand," etc.

But this gives you broad results, not precise targeting. You'll still need to manually scroll and filter.

Method 3: Cross-Reference with Google Maps & Business Directories

Many Nigerian businesses are on both Instagram and Google Maps or business directories. You can reverse-engineer this:

  1. Go to Google Maps, search "Fashion brands Lagos" or your niche
  2. Visit each business's Google profile, click their website link
  3. Check if they have Instagram (usually linked on their website or contact page)
  4. If they have Instagram + website + email, you've got a hot lead

This cross-verification takes longer but gives you higher-quality contacts because these are established businesses with real online presence.

How to Pitch Effectively Once You Have the Leads

Having the lead list is only step one. Your pitch matters.

Step 1: Use Cold Email, Not Instagram DM

Instagram DMs are noisy. Business owners get dozens of pitches per week. Email is more professional, searchable, and has better deliverability if done right.

Once you have your lead list from Freelance LeadsHub, load it into Zaram Web Mailer. This tool handles bulk sending, automatic follow-ups, and avoids spam filters—critical because Nigerian ISPs and Gmail can flag bulk emails if sent wrong.

Step 2: Personalize Your Subject Line & Hook

Subject lines that work:

  • "Quick idea for [Business Name] Instagram growth"
  • "Your product photos are great—here's what could improve them"
  • "I helped [Similar Business] increase bookings by 40%—want the same?"

Generic subject lines get 5–8% open rates. Personalized ones get 20–35%.

Step 3: Lead with Value, Not Your Services

Bad pitch: "I'm a social media manager. I offer content creation, posting, engagement strategy, and analytics reporting. My rates start at ₦80,000/month."

Good pitch: "Hi [Name], I noticed your Instagram posts get solid engagement, but you're posting inconsistently (last post was 5 days ago). Most fashion brands lose 30% of potential sales when they go dark for 3+ days. I help brands like yours post 4x/week without the time commitment. This usually brings 25–40% more inquiries. Worth a chat?"

The second pitch shows you researched them, understand their specific problem, and have proof you solve it.

Step 4: Include a Clear, Low-Friction CTA

Don't ask for a 30-minute call on the first email. Ask for 2 minutes of their time:

"Reply to this email with a quick 'yes' if you're open to a 15-minute call this week. No pressure—if it's not a fit, I'll remove you from my list."

This gets 3–5x higher response than "Schedule a call here: [Calendly link]"

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is it legal to cold email business owners in Nigeria?

A: Yes. Cold emailing is legal in Nigeria as long as you're sending to business email addresses (not personal emails harvested without consent) and you include an unsubscribe option. The NITDA (National Information Technology Development Agency) doesn't have strict anti-spam laws like GDPR or CAN-SPAM, but best practice is to respect opt-outs. Tools like Zaram Web Mailer include unsubscribe headers to stay compliant.

Q: How do I convert Nigeria naira to USD for pricing my services?

A: Use the current CBN (Central Bank of Nigeria) rate or XE.com for real-time rates. As of mid-2026, the exchange rate hovers around ₦1,200–₦1,500 per $1 USD depending on black market vs. official rates. Most Nigerian freelancers use Wise (formerly TransferWise) or Payoneer for conversion because the rates are better than banks. If you're charging in naira locally but want to quote in USD for international clients, use ₦1,250 per $1 as a safe middle-ground rate.

Q: Can I use Instagram DMs instead of email for cold outreach?

A: You can, but email performs better. Instagram DMs have a 2–5% response rate for cold pitches. Emails (when personalized) get 10–20% response. Plus, DMs are limited to 1 attachment and don't have read receipts that show you opened their message. Email is more professional and trackable. If you do use DM, keep it short: 2–3 sentences, link to your portfolio or website, and ask for their email so you can send more info.

Q: How much should I charge Nigerian business owners for services like social media management?

A: Depends on business size and your experience. For solo freelancers: ₦80,000–₦150,000/month for 4 posts/week + engagement. For agencies or experienced freelancers: ₦200,000–₦500,000/month. For premium clients (high-revenue businesses): ₦500,000–₦2,000,000/month. Always ask about their current revenue and marketing budget before quoting. A fashion brand doing ₦50 million/year in revenue can easily spend ₦300,000/month on content. A startup doing ₦2 million/year cannot. Adjust your pricing to the client's ability to pay, not your cost.
