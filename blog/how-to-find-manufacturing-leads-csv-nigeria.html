Method 2: Manual Research (If You Want to Do It Yourself)

Not everyone trusts pre-made lists, and fair enough. Here's how to build your own manufacturing lead CSV if you prefer:

Step 1: Find Nigeria Manufacturing Directories

  • FIRS Manufacturing Registry (firs.gov.ng) — lists registered companies; exportable but requires account setup
  • Nigerian Association of Small-Scale Industrialists (NASSI) — publish directories; call them for CSV exports
  • Manufacturers Association of Nigeria (MAN) — man.org.ng has member lists; request the industrial database
  • State Economic Development Agencies — Lagos State LSETF, Kano KASB publish SME lists
  • Google Maps — search "manufacturing company Lagos" or "factory Kano," then manually extract business names
  • LinkedIn Company Pages — filter for "Manufacturing," "Industry," location Nigeria; download with a script or manually collect

Step 2: Extract Contact Information

For each company, find:

  • Company website (usually has "Contact Us" page)
  • Owner/MD email (often firstname@companyname.com or info@)
  • Phone (Google Maps or company site)
  • Business registration number (CAC or state registry)

Use free tools like Hunter.io (limited free tier) or RocketReach to verify emails before adding them to your list. This prevents high bounce rates.

Step 3: Build Your CSV

Create a spreadsheet in Google Sheets with these columns:

  • Company Name
  • Contact Person (if known)
  • Email
  • Phone
  • State
  • Business Type (e.g., "Plastic Manufacturing," "Food Processing")
  • Website
  • Last Contacted (leave blank initially)

Then download as CSV.

Honest Assessment of Manual Research:

This method works, but:

  • Email bounce rates are usually 15–25% because you're not verifying at scale
  • You'll hit data limits if on a cheap mobile plan

Use Google Sheets to track your cold email campaigns. Log:

Nigeria-Specific Considerations

There are some realities unique to freelancing and outreach in Nigeria:

Email Reliability

Corporate email bounces are common in Nigeria. Manufacturing companies might have gmail accounts for their owners instead of company domains. Build bounce management into your expectations—aim for 85% deliverability, not 100%.

Communication Preferences

Cold email is your first touchpoint, but don't be surprised if manufacturers prefer WhatsApp or phone calls once they're interested. Have your number ready on your email signature.

Payment Methods

Most manufacturing companies pay via bank transfer (Zenith, GTBank, Access). Some use Payoneer or Wise if they're export-focused. Build 2–4 week payment delays into your cash flow projections—manufacturing doesn't move fast.

Language and Tone

Keep your emails professional but warm. Nigerian business culture values respect and relationship-building. A cold email that reads like a robot won't work. Add a human touch.
