Cold Email Templates for Nigerian Freelancers That Get Replies

30 July 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

If you're a Nigerian freelancer stuck on Upwork or Fiverr, competing with thousands of others for ₦5,000 projects, cold email is your escape hatch. The problem isn't your skills—it's that you're playing the algorithm game instead of talking directly to businesses that need you.

Cold email works. But only if your message actually makes it to someone's inbox and says something they want to hear. Most Nigerian freelancers never get replies because they either send generic templates (which sound generic in any language), or they don't know which businesses to contact in the first place.

This guide gives you three cold email templates proven to get replies from businesses in Nigeria and internationally, explains why they work, and shows you the fastest way to build a lead list so you actually have someone to send these emails to.

Why Cold Email Works Better Than Platforms for Nigerian Freelancers

Let's be honest: Upwork takes 20% commission. Fiverr takes 20%. You're bidding against 50 other freelancers for every job, and the client picks based on lowest price, not quality. A ₦50,000 web design project becomes ₦40,000 after commissions. After data, electricity, and transport, you're left with thin margins.

Cold email cuts out the middleman. When you email a business owner directly, there's no commission. No algorithm. No auction. You're having a conversation.

The numbers matter: A good cold email reply rate sits between 5–15%. That means if you send 100 emails, you'll get 5–15 conversations. From 15 conversations, you'll likely close 2–3 clients. At ₦200,000–₦500,000 per project, that's real income—not platform scraps.

But here's the catch: You need the right email, sent to the right person, at the right time. All three elements matter.

The Three Cold Email Templates That Work

Template 1: The Direct Problem-Solution Email (Best for Web Design, Development, SEO)

Subject: Your website isn't converting [Company Name]

Hi [FirstName],

I checked [Company Name]'s site yesterday and noticed two things:

1. Your homepage doesn't mention what problems you solve—just what you sell
2. Your contact form is buried on page 3 of your navigation

These are the exact reasons businesses like yours lose 40% of website visitors before they even apply.

I help [industry] businesses redesign their sites for conversion. Most clients see a 25–35% increase in form submissions within 60 days.

Would it make sense to grab 15 minutes next week to walk through what's costing you now?

Best,
[YourName]
[Your Rate or Portfolio Link]

Why this works:

  • Specific observation (not generic praise)
  • Clear problem statement (they recognise themselves)
  • Quantified result (25–35% is believable, not "10x ROI")
  • Low commitment ask (15 minutes, not a full demo)
  • No fluff, no "I hope this email finds you well"

Template 2: The Social Proof Email (Best for Copywriting, Content, Marketing)

Subject: We increased a company like yours by 340% in email revenue

Hi [FirstName],

I worked with a [industry] business in [city/region] last month that was losing money on their email list.

After rewriting their email sequence, they went from ₦45,000/month to ₦190,000/month in 90 days. No paid ads. Just better emails.

I mention this because [Company Name]'s industry has the same problem—most businesses send emails that read like automated broadcasts.

I've helped 12+ Nigerian businesses fix this. If you're open to a quick conversation about whether the same approach could work for you, I'm around Tuesday–Thursday.

Cheers,
[YourName]
[Website or Loom demo link]

Why this works:

  • Specific case study (real numbers in NGN, real location)
  • Relatability ("company like yours")
  • Proof of past wins (builds credibility)
  • Narrow availability window (makes you seem in-demand)
  • Positioned as a chat, not a pitch

Template 3: The "Saw Your Post" Email (Best for Building Rapport, All Services)

Subject: Thought of you when I saw your LinkedIn post

Hi [FirstName],

Saw your post about [specific thing they posted about] last week and thought it was sharp.

The reason I'm reaching out: Most [industry] businesses talk about wanting to [goal], but they don't have the backend systems to actually execute it.

I specialise in building those systems—and I've documented the process in a free guide I put together for Nigerian businesses.

No pitch—just thought it might be useful. If you want to grab coffee (virtual or real) and pick my brain about your goals, I'm available.

Best,
[YourName]
[Free resource link or calendar]

Why this works:

  • Shows you actually pay attention (not mass sent)
  • Genuine compliment (not saccharine)
  • Positions you as a builder/strategist, not a vendor
  • Free value first (lowers their guard)
  • Open-ended but specific enough to feel personal

Real Talk: These templates only work if you customize them. "Hi [FirstName]" should actually be "Hi Chidi" or "Hi Shade." One line of research takes 60 seconds and turns a 5% reply rate into a 12% reply rate. Batch your research: spend 2 hours building a list of 50 companies, then spend 1 hour customizing one line per email. That's it.

Common Mistakes Nigerian Freelancers Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Mistake 1: Using a Gmail Address That Doesn't Match Your Brand

The Problem: Emailing from "freelancer.ahmed.work@gmail.com" reads as a side hustle. Businesses want to hire professionals.

The Fix: Set up a professional domain email (yourname.com or yourservice.com) for ₦2,000–₦5,000/year. Takes 20 minutes. Doubles reply rates because you look like a real business, not a random person.

Mistake 2: Sending Before You Have a Proof Point

Don't cold email when you have zero clients. Get your first 2–3 clients using other methods first. Once you have a case study or testimonial, cold email becomes your scaling engine.

Mistake 3: Following Up Too Aggressively

Four follow-ups is the limit. After that, you're spam. Space them out: Day 1, Day 5, Day 12, Day 21. If they haven't responded by Day 21, move on.

Mistake 4: Talking About Yourself Instead of Their Problem

"I'm a freelancer with 5 years of experience" does nothing. "Your homepage loses 40% of visitors" does. Every sentence should be about them, not you.

Mistake 5: Ignoring Reply Rate Data

Track what's working. If Template 1 gets 8% replies but Template 3 gets 4%, use Template 1 more. If subject lines with numbers get 2x opens, use them. Monitor your reply rates obsessively.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How long before I get replies to my cold emails?

A: Most replies come within 24–72 hours of sending. A few trickle in over the next 2 weeks. This is why batching matters—send on Thursday, you'll get your first replies by Friday evening. If you're using Zaram Web Mailer and your emails are properly customised with the recipient's first name and a specific detail, you should see at least one reply within 48 hours of your first batch of 20 emails. If you get zero replies after 100 emails, your subject line is probably the problem—test a different angle.

Q: Can I use cold email if I'm in Nigeria but targeting international clients?

A: Yes, absolutely. In fact, it's your advantage. British and American businesses often prefer working with Nigerian freelancers because rates are lower without sacrificing quality. Use the same templates but adjust for timezone: "I'm in Lagos but available for calls 8–11 AM UK time" or "US hours work for me." Note your payment method too: "I invoice via Wise and PayPal" removes friction. Get your lead list from Freelance LeadsHub by selecting UK or US as your target country, and you're set.

Q: What's the difference between cold email and spam, and will I get my email blocked?

A: Cold email is personalised, relevant, and provides value. Spam is mass, generic, and only benefits the sender. Gmail and Outlook know the difference. As long as you're using a professional domain, personalising emails (not blast-sending identical copies), and following up reasonably (not 10 times), you won't get blocked. Using Zaram Web Mailer also helps because it's built to avoid spam filters—it throttles your sends, avoids trigger words, and manages your sender reputation automatically. The key is: never buy a list of 10,000 random emails and blast them. Always start small, build reputation, then scale.
