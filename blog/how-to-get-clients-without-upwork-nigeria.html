How to Get Clients Without Upwork: The Nigerian Freelancer's Guide to Direct Outreach

2026-08-03 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

You know the problem. On Upwork, you're bidding against 200 other freelancers for a ₦50,000 project. Your profile looks good. Your reviews are solid. But the client picks someone cheaper from Pakistan or the Philippines. You spend 10 hours a week searching, bidding, and never landing anything substantial.

This is why how to get clients without Upwork in Nigeria isn't a nice-to-have question anymore—it's survival.

The truth: The freelancers earning ₦500,000+ per month in Nigeria aren't living on Upwork. They're reaching out directly to businesses that need their skills. They're building relationships. They're getting hired at rates 3–5x higher than platform rates because they're not competing on price—they're selling solutions.

This guide will show you exactly how to do it. We'll walk through real strategies, real numbers, and the specific tools that work.

Why Upwork (and Fiverr) Don't Work for Nigerian Freelancers

Let's be honest about the numbers first.

On Upwork, the average freelancer in Nigeria earns between $8–$25 per hour depending on skill level. That translates to roughly ₦6,400–₦20,000 per hour at the current 1 USD = ₦800–₦820 exchange rate (as of mid-2026). After Upwork's 20% cut, you're looking at ₦5,120–₦16,000 in your account. Then Payoneer takes another 2%, and by the time it hits your Nigerian bank, you've lost another percentage to conversion fees.

The math doesn't work for serious freelancers.

But there's a bigger problem: you're competing on commoditized work. Clients pick based on price, portfolio, and reviews—not on relationship or understanding of their business. You're one of 500 bids they'll receive. Even if you win, the client rarely comes back because they know they can always find someone cheaper next time.

Direct outreach flips this. You're reaching businesses that:

  • Have actual budgets (not hobbyists browsing platforms)
  • Don't know where to find reliable freelancers
  • Value relationship and expertise over price
  • Will pay 2–5x platform rates for quality work
  • Hire you for multiple projects (retainers are common)

A graphic designer earning ₦30,000 per logo on Upwork could earn ₦150,000–₦250,000 per project from a direct client who values their work and doesn't shop around.

The Direct Outreach Method: Step by Step

Step 1: Define Your Niche and Ideal Client

You can't pitch everyone. The freelancers who succeed narrow down ruthlessly.

Instead of "I do web design," say: "I design e-commerce websites for fashion retailers in Nigeria who want to sell online but don't have technical skills."

Why? Because when you reach 50 fashion retail owners with a message that speaks directly to their pain point, you'll get more responses than pitching 500 random businesses with a generic message.

Your niche should overlap three things:

  1. What you're genuinely good at (writing, design, coding, marketing, etc.)
  2. What businesses in Nigeria actually need (not what you think they need)
  3. What you can charge premium rates for (not the cheapest service in the market)

Examples that work well in Nigeria:

  • Social media management for small e-commerce brands
  • Website copywriting for SaaS companies targeting African markets
  • Virtual assistant services for busy entrepreneurs
  • Accounting/bookkeeping for SMEs
  • Content writing for financial services companies
  • Email marketing automation for e-commerce
  • SEO for local service businesses (plumbers, electricians, real estate)

Pick one. Write it down. You'll use this to find your target businesses in the next step.

Step 3: Write a Cold Email That Actually Works

Generic cold emails get deleted. Personalized ones get responses.

Here's the structure that converts in Nigeria:

  1. Subject line: One sentence, specific to their business (not clickbait)
  2. Opening: Show you know who they are (mention their product, recent post, something specific)
  3. Problem statement: Name the specific problem your service solves for them
  4. Social proof: 1–2 results you've delivered (other clients, projects, metrics)
  5. Call to action: One clear next step (e.g., "Can I send you a quick mockup?")
  6. Close: Sign-off with name, title, phone

Example for a social media manager targeting e-commerce:

Subject: Increasing orders for [Brand Name] on Instagram

Hi [Owner Name],

I came across [Brand Name]'s Instagram last week—your product shots are really clean, but I noticed your engagement rate is about 2–3% (typical for e-commerce). Most of your followers aren't seeing your posts.

I've helped 8 fashion brands in Lagos grow from 2–3% to 8–12% engagement in 3 months, which usually translates to a 40–60% increase in website clicks and orders.

Would it make sense to hop on a 15-minute call and let me show you what I'd do for [Brand Name]?

Best,
[Your Name]
Social Media Manager
[Your Phone Number]

See what happened? No fluff. Specific problem. Proof you can solve it. Clear next step.

Other Proven Methods to Find Clients Without Upwork

Referrals and Existing Client Networks

Your easiest clients come from people who already trust you.

Ask every past client (from Upwork, past jobs, anywhere) to refer you. Offer a referral bonus: ₦5,000–₦10,000 for every referred client who signs a contract. Clients refer other clients they know who need the same service.

This is the long game, but it compounds. After 12 months of referral-based outreach, you might have 5–8 clients on retainer—far more stable than Upwork's feast-or-famine cycle.

Learn more about building a referral system that actually works for Nigerian freelancers.

LinkedIn Outreach

LinkedIn is where Nigerian business owners actually spend time (unlike Twitter, which is mostly entertainment).

Strategy:

  1. Search for your ideal client on LinkedIn (e.g., "business owner" + "fashion" + "Lagos")
  2. Visit their profile, see what they post about
  3. Send a personalized connection request (not just "Let's connect")
  4. Wait 2–3 days, then send a message with your pitch

LinkedIn messages get opened far more than cold emails. The downside: you can only send a limited number of messages per week unless you're paid. But if you're selective (quality over quantity), it works.

Facebook Groups and Online Communities

Join groups where your ideal clients hang out:

  • Nigerian e-commerce sellers groups
  • Business owner networks
  • Industry-specific groups (accountants, marketers, real estate agents)

Don't spam. Instead, answer questions, provide value, and mention your service when relevant. Build authority. When someone asks "Does anyone know a good copywriter?" you're top-of-mind because you've been helpful for weeks.

Networking Events and Workshops

In-person relationships still matter in Nigeria. Attend:

  • Chamber of Commerce meetups
  • Industry conferences
  • Co-working spaces (Ventures, Co-creation Hub, etc.)
  • Business breakfasts and networking lunches

One good conversation at a networking event can lead to a ₦500,000 project. The ROI of 2–3 hours and ₦3,000–₦5,000 in transport/refreshments is incredible if you close even one client.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: No niche targeting
Pitching "I do everything" gets deleted. Be specific.

Mistake 2: Generic emails
"Hi, I'm a freelancer" gets no responses. Show you know their business.

Mistake 3: Not following up
People miss emails. Send a follow-up 5 days later, then once more 10 days after. You'll double your response rate.

Mistake 4: Asking for a job instead of offering value
Bad: "Hire me as your copywriter."
Good: "Can I send you a free sample of how I'd rewrite your homepage?"

Mistake 5: Not tracking results
You send 100 emails and get 5 responses. That's a 5% response rate. Send 100 more, get 8. Now you know what works. If you don't track, you're guessing.

Scaling Beyond One-to-One Outreach

Once you have a system that works, scale it.

If cold email brings in 2 clients/month at ₦200,000 each, that's ₦400,000/month profit. But you could hire a VA (₦30,000–₦50,000/month) to handle outreach, lead tracking, and email follow-ups while you focus on closing and delivery.

Or expand to a second niche. Your social media template works for fashion brands—test it on real estate agents or fintech startups.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Will cold emailing get my domain/email blacklisted?

A: Only if you send badly. Spam filters blacklist accounts that send thousands of emails with high bounce rates or from newly created domains. Zaram Web Mailer handles deliverability specifically—it warms up your domain, spaces out sends, and avoids the patterns that trigger spam filters. Nigerian domains and IP addresses need special care (they're often marked as risky), but the right tool manages it. If you're sending 30–50 well-targeted emails per day (not 500), you won't have issues.

Q: How much should I charge if I'm getting direct clients?

A: 2–3x what you'd charge on Upwork, minimum. If you're earning ₦50,000 for a logo on Upwork, a direct client should pay ₦150,000–₦200,000. Why? Because they're not competing bids, you have a direct relationship, and they value reliability. Direct clients also buy retainers (monthly fees) instead of one-off projects. Charge ₦300,000–₦500,000/month for ongoing services (social media, copywriting, VA work). Use our freelance rate calculator for Nigeria to find your baseline.

Q: How long until I get my first response from cold emails?

A: You'll usually get your first response within 3–5 days of sending. Response rate for well-written cold emails in Nigeria averages 3–7% (meaning 3–7 responses per 100 emails sent). Some people respond within hours; others take 2–3 weeks. Always include a follow-up sequence—your second email should go out 5 days later, and a third 10 days after that. This can double your response rate without being annoying.

Q: Can I use a Gmail or Outlook address, or do I need a business domain?

A: Gmail works, but a business domain (your name or business name) looks more professional and gets better email deliverability. You can register a domain for ₦1,500–₦3,000/year and set up email forwarding through your hosting provider. It takes 20 minutes and is worth it. If you're just starting, Gmail is fine—upgrade later when you're landing clients and want to look more established.

The freelancers in Nigeria earning ₦300,000–₦1,000,000+ per month aren't on Upwork anymore. They've moved on to direct outreach, retainers, and client relationships. You can do the same, but it requires doing three things most freelancers won't:

Spend the next 2 weeks building your system. By week 3, you'll have your first 100 leads and your first cold email template. By week 4, you'll have sent your first batch of emails. By week 8, you'll have your first real client who found you through direct outreach.

That's how you get clients without Upwork.
