How to Find Agencies Hiring Freelancers in Nigeria: A Direct Lead Generation Guide

22 August 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

The problem with Upwork and Fiverr is obvious: you're competing with thousands of other freelancers for the same clients, and the platform takes 20–30% of what you earn. For a Nigerian freelancer earning in USD or EUR, that cut hurts.

What if instead of waiting for agencies to post jobs, you went directly to them with a pitch? This is how top freelancers in Nigeria scale their income. But first, you need to know how to find agencies hiring freelancers in Nigeria—and that's what this guide covers.

By the end, you'll have a repeatable system to identify real agencies, build a lead list, and land contracts that pay ₦200,000–₦500,000+ per month (or $400–$1,000+ USD). No platform middleman. No bidding wars.

Why Finding Agencies Directly Matters for Nigerian Freelancers

Let's be real about the numbers. On Upwork, a junior freelancer (say, a content writer or VA) might earn ₦50,000–₦150,000 per month after platform fees. Senior freelancers do better, but they're still fighting algorithm changes and rate compression.

Agencies, on the other hand, have recurring work. They hire freelancers for retainer roles—long-term, predictable income. An agency that needs 5 blog posts per week will pay you every single week for months or years. That's stability.

Here's what the math looks like:

  • Upwork route: Land 1 client, earn ₦100,000, lose 30% to fees = ₦70,000 after Upwork takes their cut. Client leaves after 3 months.
  • Agency route: Land 1 retainer at ₦200,000/month, no platform fees, contract typically lasts 6–12 months. Total: ₦1.2–₦2.4M from one client.

The difference isn't marginal. It's transformative. But you only get there if you know where to look and how to pitch.

The Core Challenge: Nigerians Don't Know Where to Look

Most Nigerian freelancers ask vague questions like "Where do I find clients?" But they don't have a system. They scroll LinkedIn, check job boards, and hope something sticks.

The truth: there are thousands of digital agencies in Nigeria and across Africa actively hiring freelancers right now. They're not advertising on Upwork because they don't need to—they prefer direct relationships. They're on LinkedIn, their own websites, or running quietly in the background serving clients internationally.

Your job is to find them systematically. Not randomly. Not by luck. By using the right tools and strategies.

Method 2: LinkedIn Outreach (Free, But Slower)

If you're on a budget, LinkedIn is still valuable. Here's the system:

  1. Search for agency owners and hiring managers. Use searches like:
    • "Digital Marketing Agency" + Location: Nigeria
    • "Founder" + "Agency" + Location: Lagos / Abuja / Port Harcourt
    • "Hiring Manager" + "Creative Services"
  2. Visit their company pages. Check the "People" tab to see staff and decision-makers.
  3. Send personalized connection requests. Not a generic "Let's connect"—mention a specific service you offer and why you're interested in their work.
  4. Wait 3–5 days, then message. Once connected, send a brief pitch. See the section below for pitch templates.

The challenge with LinkedIn: it's slow. You might send 20 connection requests and get 8 accepts. Of those, maybe 2 will actually respond to your message. That's a 10% response rate at best.

For speed and scale, combining LinkedIn with a lead database from Freelance LeadsHub is smarter—you get verified emails and can move to direct outreach faster.

Method 3: Google Maps & Local Search (For Brick-and-Mortar Agencies)

How to Cold Email Agencies Once You Have Their Contact Info

Once you've built your list (whether from Freelance LeadsHub or manually), the next step is outreach. Cold email, when done right, has a 15–25% response rate for freelancers targeting agencies.

The Cold Email Formula

Subject line: Keep it short and specific.

  • "Quick question: [Agency name]'s blog strategy" (if you researched them)
  • "Design support for [Agency name]?" (if you're a designer)
  • Avoid: "Let's work together" or "Freelance opportunity"

Body (template):

Hi [Name],

I noticed [Agency name] produces [specific type of work—e.g., "really solid case studies on SaaS clients"]. I'm a [your role—e.g., "content writer"] based in Nigeria, and I work with agencies like yours who need [specific service—e.g., "high-volume blog content, 2–3x per week"].

I'm not looking for one-off gigs—I work on retainers. My rate is [your rate in USD and/or NGN]. I can start immediately.

If this is something you'd consider, I'd love to chat. Otherwise, no worries—I appreciate the inbox real estate.

Best,
[Your name]

That's it. No fluff. No "hoping this email finds you well." Just specificity and respect for their time.

Expected Income & Timeline: What Real Freelancers Are Making

Let's ground this in reality. Here's what Nigerian freelancers are actually earning from agencies right now (2026 rates, with naira at ~₦1,500 to $1 USD):

  • Entry-level (0–1 year experience): $200–400/month per retainer. Usually part-time work while building skills. Could earn ₦300,000–₦600,000 if working 2–3 agencies.
  • Mid-level (1–3 years): $400–800/month per retainer. Full-time from 2–3 retainers = ₦900,000–₦1.8M monthly.
  • Senior (3+ years + portfolio): $800–2,000+/month per retainer. Selective with clients, earning ₦1.2–₦3M+ monthly from 2–3 quality agencies.

Timeline to first retainer: 4–8 weeks from your first pitch if you're professional and persistent.

Payment Methods: Getting Paid from Nigerian Agencies

Most Nigerian agencies pay via:

  • Bank transfer (within Nigeria): Direct to your Zenith, GTBank, or UBA account. Fastest locally.
  • Wise / Remitly / Payoneer: For USD earnings. Open these accounts before you start pitching.
  • Crypto (Bitcoin/USDT): Some tech-forward agencies prefer this. Learn how to set up and receive crypto payments securely as a Nigerian freelancer.

Have all three options ready before signing contracts. You don't want to delay payment because you didn't have a Wise account set up.

Common Mistakes Nigerian Freelancers Make When Pitching Agencies

Avoid these and your response rate will jump dramatically:

  • Being too generic. "I'm a freelancer looking for work" gets deleted immediately. Be specific: "I write 2,000-word SEO blog posts for SaaS companies."
  • Asking for too much too soon. Don't ask "What's your budget?" first. State your rate confidently.
  • Having no portfolio. Even if you're new, build 2–3 samples. Agencies won't hire on faith.
  • Following up once and giving up. Send 3–4 follow-ups over 2 weeks. Most replies come on follow-up 2 or 3, not the initial email.
  • Not reading their website first. Mention something specific about their work. It shows you're serious, not mass-pitching.

Scaling: Moving from 1 Agency to 3–5 Retainers

Once you land your first retainer, the goal is to systematically add 2–3 more. Each new retainer compounds your income:

  • 1 retainer at ₦250,000/month = ₦250,000
  • 2 retainers at ₦250,000 each = ₦500,000
  • 3 retainers at ₦200,000–₦300,000 average = ₦750,000–₦900,000

The key: don't oversell yourself. Most successful Nigerian freelancers cap at 3–4 retainers because quality suffers otherwise. Each retainer requires time management, especially with internet inconsistency and NEPA issues.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is it better to find agencies in Nigeria or internationally?

A: Internationally-focused agencies (those serving US/UK clients) pay 2–3x more than local Nigerian agencies. A Nigerian agency might pay ₦150,000/month for content writing; an international one pays ₦300,000–₦600,000. Both exist in Nigeria. Prioritize international agencies, but don't ignore local ones—they're easier to qualify and follow up with. Use a lead database like Freelance LeadsHub to filter by industry type to identify international-facing agencies.

Q: How long does it take to get a response from cold emails?

A: Expect 15–25% open rate and 5–15% reply rate. First replies usually come within 2–7 days of your initial email. Follow-ups (on days 3, 7, and 14) generate 40–50% of responses. So give it a minimum of 2 weeks per batch before concluding the campaign didn't work. Many freelancers give up after one email—that's the biggest mistake.

Q: What if I don't have a portfolio yet?

A: Build one immediately. If you're a writer, write 3 sample blog posts on topics related to your target industry. If you're a designer, create 3 mockups. If you're a VA, document your processes in a Notion page. You don't need real client work—you need proof of competence. Most agencies will accept samples, especially if you're entering your first contract.

Q: Should I apply to job postings or only cold email?

A: Do both. Job postings have higher competition but faster turnaround. Cold email has lower response rate but higher conversion once you connect. The ideal strategy: spend 60% of your time on cold outreach (using Freelance LeadsHub + Zaram Web Mailer) and 40% on job boards. You'll fill your pipeline faster this way.
