How to Get Bookkeeping Clients in Nigeria as a Freelancer: A Practical Guide

31 July 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

Bookkeeping is one of the most stable freelance skills in Nigeria right now. Businesses need their books balanced, invoices tracked, and financial records organized — and they'll pay for it consistently. But here's the problem most Nigerian bookkeepers face: they're not getting enough clients. They're either stuck on Upwork undercutting each other at ₦5,000–₦10,000 per month, or they're grinding on LinkedIn with zero replies.

This guide shows you how to get bookkeeping clients in Nigeria using a direct-outreach strategy that works. You'll learn realistic pricing, where to find real businesses (not just competition on freelance platforms), and how to pitch them so they actually respond.

Why Bookkeeping is a High-Demand Skill in Nigeria Right Now

Before we dive into tactics, understand why bookkeeping is valuable here:

  • Growing SME sector: Nigeria has over 41 million registered SMEs, most of which are disorganized with their finances. This is your market.
  • Tax compliance pressure: Businesses need proper records for FIRS (Federal Inland Revenue Service) and VAT filing. They can't afford to get this wrong.
  • Accounting software adoption: More Nigerian businesses are moving to Xero, QuickBooks Online, and Wave — skills that command premium rates.
  • Dollar income potential: If you work with foreign businesses (export-focused Nigerian companies, diaspora businesses), you can earn USD 500–₦2,000+ per month per client.

The real issue isn't demand — it's that most Nigerian bookkeepers don't know how to find and pitch clients outside of the race-to-the-bottom platforms.

Realistic Income Expectations: What You Can Actually Earn

Let's be honest about numbers. This matters for planning your pitch strategy.

Local Nigerian Clients (Naira Income)

  • Small sole proprietors or single-store retailers: ₦15,000–₦40,000/month for basic monthly reconciliation and tax filing prep
  • Growing SMEs (5–20 employees): ₦50,000–₦150,000/month for full bookkeeping, payroll support, and reporting
  • Established businesses (20+ employees): ₦150,000–₦500,000+/month if you manage their full financial operations

Reality check: If you get 5 local clients at an average of ₦80,000/month, that's ₦400,000 per month (roughly $270 USD at current rates). Add 2–3 USD clients at $300/month each, and you're at $570–$870/month. That's sustainable income in Nigeria.

Foreign/USD Clients

  • Small US/UK businesses: $300–$800/month for monthly bookkeeping
  • Medium businesses: $800–$2,000+/month for multi-currency, tax-ready records
  • Retainer + project basis: Many charge $1,500–$5,000 for quarterly tax prep or financial cleanup projects

The strategy changes based on where you find clients. Local clients need to see you're trustworthy; foreign clients need proof you understand their software and timezones.

The Cold Email Strategy: Finding Businesses That Need Your Help

Most Nigerian bookkeepers wait for clients to come to them. You won't. You're going to reach out directly to businesses that match your profile.

Step 1: Define Your Target Business Profile

Don't pitch everyone. Pick a niche. Examples:

  • E-commerce businesses (they have complex inventory and sales records)
  • Import/export firms (multi-currency chaos that requires expert bookkeeping)
  • Service-based businesses (salons, gyms, consultancies with employee payroll)
  • Real estate agents/property developers
  • Manufacturing or trading companies

Why niche? Your email will be more specific, your solution feels custom-built, and you'll get 3–5x better response rates than generic "I do bookkeeping" pitches.

Step 3: Write Your Cold Email

Your email needs to do three things: get attention, establish credibility, and create urgency to reply.

Email template that works:

Subject: Quick question about your monthly books - [Business Name] Hi [Name], I noticed [Business Name] handles a fair bit of [import/inventory/payroll/transactions]. Most businesses I work with in your space are either scrambling to catch up with FIRS filings or sitting on disorganized records that make tax time stressful. Sound familiar? I help businesses like yours get monthly books organized so filing season isn't a nightmare. Usually takes 2–3 hours monthly for an established system. Quick question: who's currently handling your bookkeeping? And would it help if I showed you what a clean, tax-ready monthly report looks like for a business your size? No pressure either way. Best, [Your name] [Your service offer / phone]

Why this works: You're not selling. You're asking if they have a problem and offering to show a solution. Response rates typically range from 5–12% with this approach (compared to 0.5–2% on generic pitches).

Building Trust When Pitching Bookkeeping Services

Bookkeeping is trust-based. A business won't hire you to manage their financial records without proof you're competent and won't disappear.

What to Include in Your Follow-Up (After They Show Interest)

  • Sample report: Show them what organized monthly financials look like (use fake data, never real client data)
  • Software proficiency: State which platforms you're certified in — Xero, QuickBooks Online, Wave, etc. Nigerian businesses increasingly use these, not just Excel.
  • Turnaround time: "Financials ready by the 5th of the following month" — specificity builds confidence
  • One reference or case study: Even just "I currently manage books for a 15-person trading company, 3-month turnaround from disorganized records to monthly reports" carries weight

Pricing Conversation

Don't quote upfront in your first email. After they engage, ask:

  • "How many transactions monthly?" (affects workload)
  • "Who's doing your bookkeeping now?" (tells you if they'll need training time)
  • "What software are you using?" (Xero is easier to integrate; Excel files mean manual work)

Then quote based on complexity, not desperation. If they've got 200+ monthly transactions and messy records, that's ₦80,000–₦150,000/month. Don't drop to ₦40,000 because you're hungry. They respect confidence in pricing.

Where to Find More Clients Beyond Cold Email

Cold email is high-leverage, but diversify:

Referral Networks

Tax accountants, business lawyers, and bookkeeping agencies get calls for bookkeeping help they don't provide. Build relationships. Offer them 10–20% commission per referral. One accountant who trusts you can send 2–3 clients/month.

LinkedIn (Done Right)

Don't post motivational quotes. Post actual client wins (anonymized): "Helped a trading company reduce tax filing time by 40% by automating their monthly reconciliation." Get engagement from business owners. Then DM those who comment. LinkedIn engagement → DM → cold email is warmer than cold email alone.

Join Nigerian Business Communities

Nairobi-based business groups, Founder Institute Nigeria, local Chamber of Commerce events. In-person still wins in Nigeria. You meet a business owner, solve one problem, they hire you for recurring bookkeeping.

Common Mistakes Nigerian Bookkeepers Make

Mistake 1: Competing on Upwork Instead of Building Direct Clients

Upwork takes 20% commission. You're fighting 500 other Nigerian bookkeepers. Earn ₦50,000 there, take home ₦40,000 after fees and FIRS tax. OR find one direct client at ₦80,000/month and keep ₦72,000. The math is obvious.

Mistake 2: Not Following Up

Send one email, get no reply, assume they're not interested. Wrong. Most business owners are busy. Send 3–4 follow-ups over 2–3 weeks. The person who replies on follow-up #3 is just as good a client as someone who replies immediately.

Mistake 3: Underpricing to Compete

I see Nigerian bookkeepers charging ₦10,000/month. That's ₦2,500/week. At 10 hours of work, that's ₦250/hour. Don't. Price for value. If you save a business ₦500,000 during tax filing by having clean records, ₦100,000/month is a steal.

Mistake 4: Not Niching

You're a "general bookkeeper." Better: "I help import/export businesses reconcile multi-currency transactions and stay FIRS-compliant." Specificity = higher rates + easier pitching.

The Timeline: How Long Until Your First Client?

Realistic expectations:

  • Week 1: Download leads, write your email template, send first 30–50 emails
  • Week 2: Get initial replies (usually 1–3 interested), send follow-ups to non-responders
  • Week 3: Close your first 1–2 clients (typical deal cycle is 7–14 days once they engage)
  • Month 2: Have 3–5 consistent clients, each paying ₦50,000–₦150,000/month
  • Month 3+: Referrals kick in, you can slow outreach, focus on delivering great work

If you're impatient, this feels slow. If you're strategic, it's the fastest path to consistent income in Nigeria.

Scaling: From One-Off Client to Recurring Revenue

Once you have 5–7 consistent bookkeeping clients (taking 2–3 hours/week each), you've got ₦400,000–₦600,000/month in recurring income. From there:

  • Stop cold emailing, lean on referrals — those 5 clients know other businesses
  • Hire a junior bookkeeper — have them handle data entry, you handle client relations and complex reconciliations
  • Raise rates for new clients — once you're not desperate, clients respect higher prices
  • Specialize deeper — become the "bookkeeper for e-commerce businesses" in Lagos, charge premium rates

This is how Nigerian freelancers move from gig-based income to business income.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What's the minimum number of clients I need to make a livable income in Nigeria as a bookkeeper?

A: Typically 4–6 clients. At ₦100,000/month average per client, that's ₦400,000–₦600,000 monthly. After internet, tools, and tax, you're left with ₦300,000–₦450,000, which is a solid middle-class income in most Nigerian cities. If you want ₦1 million/month, you need 10–12 clients or a few high-value USD clients alongside local ones.

Q: How do I handle clients in different timezones if I take on US/UK clients?

A: Set clear boundaries. Tell US clients "I work 9 AM–5 PM Lagos time, respond to emails within 24 hours." Most don't need real-time interaction; they need monthly reports on time. Use async communication (email, Slack) instead of calls. If they need more real-time support, charge extra. One call/month at a set time usually satisfies US clients.

Q: Is it better to specialize in one accounting software or learn multiple?

A: Learn 2–3. Xero and QuickBooks Online are the most common globally. Wave is free and popular with Nigerian startups. Excel is still used by 40% of Nigerian businesses. Depth in Xero + breadth in the others is ideal. Specializing helps: "Xero specialist" is stronger than "I do bookkeeping on any platform."

Q: How do I convince a business owner to switch from their current bookkeeper to me?

A: Don't pitch a switch. Pitch an upgrade. In your email, ask "who's currently handling your books?" If they say "my sister does it" or "I use Excel," you're not replacing anyone — you're professionalize what's already happening. Emphasize what they gain: tax-ready reports, time savings, compliance. Offer a 1-month trial. Most will take it if the value is clear.
