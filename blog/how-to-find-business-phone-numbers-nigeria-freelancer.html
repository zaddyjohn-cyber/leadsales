How to Verify & Validate Business Phone Numbers

Finding a phone number is one thing. Ensuring it's current and belongs to a decision-maker is another.

Quick Verification Steps

  1. Call and confirm. Ring the number and ask for the business owner/manager. This takes 1–2 minutes and tells you if the number is active.
  2. Cross-reference on Google Maps. Search the business name and check if the phone number matches the Google listing.
  3. Check social media. Look for the business on Instagram, Facebook, or LinkedIn. Often, verified pages list contact numbers in the bio or about section.
  4. Visit their website. If they have a website, the contact page should confirm the phone number and often the decision-maker's name.

This process takes 2–3 minutes per lead. For a list of 100 leads, you're looking at 3–5 hours of validation work. Painful but worth it—you'll eliminate dead numbers and outdated contacts before you waste time cold calling.

Cold Calling vs. Cold Email: Which Works Better in Nigeria?

Cold calling is more personal and gets faster responses. But it's exhausting, and you'll face rejection 80% of the time.

Cold email is scalable. You send once, follow up twice, and move on. Higher volume, lower emotional toll.

The hybrid approach (best for Nigeria):

  1. Build your lead list (phone numbers + emails)
  2. Send a cold email first (warm them up, establish credibility)
  3. Follow up with a phone call 3–5 days later if they don't respond
  4. If still no response, follow up with a WhatsApp message 7 days after that

Building Your Cold Email Outreach Strategy with Phone Numbers

Having phone numbers isn't enough. You need a system.

Step 1: Segment Your List

Don't treat all phone numbers the same. Group them by:

  • Industry (e.g., e-commerce, agencies, SaaS)
  • Business size (1–10 employees, 11–50, 50+)
  • Location (Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, other states)
  • Likelihood to buy (hot, warm, cold based on research)

Step 2: Craft a Simple Cold Email

Your email should:

  • Open with a specific compliment or observation. Not "Hi, I'm a freelancer." But "I noticed you're selling [product] on Shopify—I've helped 12 similar shops increase checkout conversion by 18%."
  • Show your value in 2–3 sentences. Don't pitch. Show results.
  • Include your phone number or WhatsApp. Make it easy for them to respond via phone.
  • Keep it under 100 words. Respect their time.

Example for a web designer:

"Hi [Name], I noticed [Company]'s website loads slowly on mobile (tested it just now). For e-commerce sites, that costs about 2–3% of revenue daily. I've fixed this for 15 similar shops in Lagos, cutting page load time by 60% and recovering about ₦40,000–₦80,000/month in lost sales. Happy to audit your site for free. Available for a 20-min call Wednesday or Thursday. [Your phone number]"

This works because it's specific, shows you've done research, offers value, and makes responding easy.

Step 4: Follow Up (This is Where Most Deals Close)

First email opens are low. But follow-ups are magic. Send a second email 3 days later if there's no response. Then a third 7 days after that.

If they still haven't replied, call them. Use that phone number. Introduce yourself briefly: "Hi, I sent you an email on Tuesday about [specific value]. Did you get a chance to see it?" This takes 30 seconds and has a 40%+ chance of getting a meeting scheduled.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Using outdated or scraped lists. Dead phone numbers waste your time. Invest in verified data (like Freelance LeadsHub) or spend time validating.

2. Cold calling without research. Don't call a bakery to pitch accounting services. Segment your list by relevance first.

3. Pitching instead of solving. Don't lead with "I'm a freelancer looking for work." Lead with "I noticed your site is costing you sales. Here's how I fix it."
