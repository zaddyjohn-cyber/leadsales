18 August 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

If you're a Nigerian freelancer competing on Upwork and Fiverr, you already know the reality: low rates, endless competition, and projects that pay ₦5,000–₦15,000 per gig. Meanwhile, local Nigerian businesses—agencies, e-commerce stores, tech companies, and service providers—are hiring freelancers directly and paying 3–5x more because they skip the platform middleman.

The problem isn't a lack of opportunities. The problem is you don't know who these businesses are or how to reach them.

This guide shows you exactly how to get an email list of Nigerian businesses for cold outreach—the same method that's helping freelancers land ₦200,000–₦500,000+ monthly retainers instead of racing to the bottom on freelance platforms.

Why Cold Outreach to Nigerian Businesses Works (And Why You Should Start Now)

Let's start with the numbers. On Upwork, a typical social media management gig in Nigeria goes for $50–$150/month (₦25,000–₦75,000). A local Nigerian business? They'll often pay ₦150,000–₦300,000/month for the exact same work—or $300–$600 USD if they're export-focused.

The reason is simple: direct hiring removes the platform fee, the review manipulation, and the endless bidding wars. Businesses that are serious about growth prefer working with one freelancer they can trust, not cycling through dozens of cheap contractors.

Cold email is the most cost-effective way to reach them. Here's why:

  • Direct access: You're talking to decision-makers (business owners, marketing managers, ops leads), not competing with thousands of freelancers on a platform.
  • Lower customer acquisition cost: A targeted email costs almost nothing to send. No platform fees, no algorithm manipulation needed.
  • Higher-value contracts: Businesses hiring directly typically offer retainers or project-based work at 2–3x platform rates.
  • Recurring revenue potential: Once you land a client through cold outreach, they're likely to keep you for months or years, unlike platform gigs.
  • Nigerian businesses are underserved: Most Nigerian companies still aren't used to getting cold pitches from freelancers. Low competition = higher response rates.

The catch? You need a list of actual business email addresses to reach them. That's what this guide covers.

Method 2: Manual Research Using Free Nigerian Business Directories

If you have more time and want to keep costs lower, you can research businesses manually using Nigerian directories and Google.

Step 1: Identify Your Target Niche and Cities

Before you start searching, get specific. Instead of "I'll pitch all Nigerian businesses," decide:

  • Niche: E-commerce stores, digital agencies, real estate companies, fintech startups, coaching businesses, etc.
  • Cities: Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Kano, or Ibadan (where most businesses are concentrated)
  • Business size: Small (1–10 employees), Medium (10–50), or larger

Why? A targeted list of 100 relevant businesses will outperform 1,000 random companies every time. Conversion rate scales with relevance, not volume.

Step 2: Search Nigerian Business Directories

These sites have public business listings with contact info:

  • Naijafy.com — Directory of Nigerian businesses with email and phone. Filter by industry and state.
  • BellaNaija Business Directory — Curated list of Nigerian companies, especially fashion, tech, and lifestyle brands.
  • Nigerian Stock Exchange (NSE) — If targeting bigger companies. Free public company registry.
  • LinkedIn Sales Navigator — Search Nigerian businesses by industry, company size, and location. Extract emails via tools like Hunter.io or RocketReach.
  • Google Maps + Google Search — Search "[Your Niche] businesses in Lagos" or "[Niche] companies Abuja." Visit company websites, find contact pages.

Step 3: Extract and Verify Email Addresses

Not every business website lists an email. Use these tools to find hidden emails:

  • Hunter.io — Free version finds 100+ emails per month. Paste company domain, it returns verified emails.
  • RocketReach — Similar to Hunter. Works well for Nigerian business emails.
  • Clearbit Connect — Chrome extension. Extracts emails from LinkedIn profiles and company sites.
  • Email-Finder.com — Free tool. Just input domain name.

Pro tip: If you can't find a public email, check the company's LinkedIn page, Facebook business account, or call their phone number and ask for the marketing manager's email. Yes, really—most will give it to you.

Step 4: Build Your Spreadsheet

Create a simple Google Sheet or Excel file with these columns:

  1. Company Name

  3. City
  4. Contact Person Name (optional, but helps personalization)

  6. Phone

Write an Email That Converts

Your subject line and opening sentence matter most. Here's the formula:

Subject Line (keep it short and specific):

  • "Quick question about your [Instagram/blog/website]"
  • "Help with content for [Company Name]?"
  • "We worked with [similar company]. Thought of you."
  • Avoid: "Hi," "Let's work together," "Amazing opportunity"

Email Body (template):

Hi [First Name],

I noticed [Company Name] is doing great work in [niche]. I work with [similar businesses] on [specific service: social media management, copywriting, etc.].

We recently helped [example company] increase [metric] by [percentage]. I thought you might be interested in exploring something similar.

Would you be open to a quick 15-min chat next week?

[Your Name]
[Your service]
[Phone/website]

Key rules:

  • Keep it short. 75–100 words max. Nigerian business owners get hundreds of emails daily.
  • Show proof. Reference similar work you've done or results you've seen.
  • Make it personal. Mention something specific about their business.
  • Include a clear CTA. "Can we chat?" is better than "Let me know if interested."
  • Use your phone number. Nigerians trust direct contact more than website forms.

  • 10–20% of replies turn into paid clients

So from 200 emails: ~4–10 opens, ~1–3 replies, and possibly 1 paying client within 2–3 weeks. That's a ₦150,000–₦500,000 contract from 200 emails.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Before you start, avoid these pitfalls:

  • Sending to too many unrelated businesses: A vague pitch to 1,000 random businesses loses to a targeted pitch to 100 relevant ones. Quality > quantity.
  • Using free Gmail for cold outreach: Gmail throttles bulk sends. Use a paid domain (Google Workspace is cheap) or a tool like Zaram Web Mailer.
  • Pitching too early: Don't pitch the entire scope of work in email 1. Email 1 is to get a reply. The call is to qualify them and scope work.
  • Not following up: First email gets ignored 80% of the time. Always send 2–3 follow-ups over 7–10 days before moving on.
  • Not personalizing: Mass-copied emails convert at 0.1%. A sentence mentioning their business or recent post increases it to 3–5%.
  • Ignoring data on what's working: Track which subject lines, which niches, and which times of day get the best responses. Double down on what works.

Real talk: Most freelancers give up after sending 20 cold emails because they get 0–1 replies. They don't understand the math. You need 100+ emails per month to see consistent results. Treat cold outreach like a system, not a one-off.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is cold emailing legal in Nigeria?

A: Yes. Cold emailing is legal in Nigeria as long as you comply with basic rules: (1) Include an unsubscribe link in your email footer, (2) Use a real business or personal name (not anonymous), (3) Include your contact details. The Nigerian Communications Commission (NCC) allows business-to-business cold emails. You only run into issues if you're spamming or impersonating someone. A simple professional pitch to a business email is completely fine. Zaram Web Mailer handles this automatically by adding unsubscribe links and managing compliance.

Q: How many emails should I send per day to avoid spam filters?

A: If you're using your own Gmail or Outlook, limit yourself to 50–100 per day to avoid getting flagged. If you're using a dedicated email tool like Zaram Web Mailer with a professional domain, you can safely send 200–500+ per day because the tool spaces out sends and uses reputation-building techniques. The tool is designed for this, so Gmail won't throttle or block you.

Q: What's the best time to send cold emails to Nigerian businesses?

A: Tuesday–Thursday, 9 AM–12 PM Lagos time (WAT). Business owners and managers check email most in late morning when they've dealt with urgent tasks. Avoid Mondays (inbox overload) and Fridays (wrapping up week). Also avoid holidays around Christmas, New Year, and Sallah festivals when businesses are slower. Zaram Web Mailer lets you schedule sends for specific days/times automatically.

Q: Can I use the same email template for all businesses, or do I need to personalize every one?

A: You need a template, but always personalize the opening and one specific detail about their business. For example, the body can be identical, but change: "Hi [First Name], I noticed [Company Name] is doing great work in [niche]" — that one sentence changes per email. Personalization increases reply rates from 0.2% to 3–5%. Use mail merge in Zaram Web Mailer or Google Sheets to automate this. You write one template, and it auto-fills names and company details from your list.
