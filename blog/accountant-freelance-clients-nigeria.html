Why Cold Email Works Better Than Job Boards for Nigerian Accountants

Let's start with the math.

On Upwork:

  • You pay 20% commission on every job
  • You bid against hundreds of freelancers
  • Average project value: ₦8,000–₦25,000
  • Time spent bidding, negotiating, dealing with disputes: 10–15 hours per week
  • Monthly realistic income: ₦40,000–₦120,000 (after fees and tax)

With cold email and direct clients:

  • No commission. You keep 100% of your fee
  • Retainer contracts: ₦80,000–₦500,000 per month per client
  • Recurring revenue (same client pays you every month)
  • You work with 3–5 consistent clients and make ₦250,000–₦1.5M+ per month
  • Time spent: 1–2 hours per day on actual client work, 2–3 hours per week on outreach

The difference is massive. And the secret is simple: you're not competing on price. You're solving a specific business problem.

Step 2: Create a Simple, Compelling Cold Email Template

Nigerian business owners are busy. They get 20+ emails per day. Your email needs to:

  1. Be short (under 100 words)
  2. Mention a specific pain (not generic "accounting services")
  3. Show you understand their business (personalised, not a template)
  4. Have a clear next step (no wishy-washy CTAs)

Template for E-commerce Businesses:

Subject: Quick question about your [Store Name] accounting

Hi [Name],

Most e-commerce sellers we work with lose ₦50,000+ yearly to unreconciled transactions and missed tax deductions.

I help online sellers track sales across platforms, reconcile with bank statements, and file tax returns correctly—without the stress of in-house accounting costs.

Quick call this week to see if it makes sense?

[Your name]
[Your phone]

Template for Service Businesses (Agencies, Logistics, Real Estate):

Subject: [Company name] + accounting

Hi [Name],

I noticed you're running a [business type]. Most owners like you are either:
- Doing their own books (eating up 5+ hours/week), or
- Overpaying for traditional accounting firms

I work with [similar business type] to handle bookkeeping, tax filing, and financial reports for ₦[your rate]/month. No long contracts.

Worth a chat?

[Your name]
[Your phone]

Key rules:

  • Use their first name (not "Dear Sir/Madam")
  • Mention something about their business (their website, what they sell, or their industry)
  • Lead with a number or pain point, not your credentials
  • Always include your phone number—calls convert better than email replies in Nigeria

Step 4: Qualify On the Phone Call

Someone replies. Now you need a 10-minute call to qualify whether they're a good fit. Don't jump into "What are your accounting needs?" Instead:

  1. Ask about their current setup. "How are you currently handling your books? Is it in-house, an accountant, or a mess?" (Usually it's option 3.)
  2. Ask about pain. "What's the biggest headache with that setup?"
  3. Ask about budget. "How much are you currently spending on accounting?"
  4. Ask about timeline. "If I could solve that by [next week/month], would you be ready to move forward?"

If they say "yes" to the last question, send them a simple proposal (one page, 3 pricing tiers, payment terms). If they hesitate or don't have budget, note them down and follow up in 2–3 months.

Common Mistakes That Kill Your Success (Avoid These)

  • Sending generic emails. "Hi, I offer accounting services" gets 1% reply rate. Specific pain-based emails get 10–15%.
  • Underpricing from the start. You can always lower your price later. You can't easily raise it. Start at ₦100,000/month minimum.
  • Not following up. 80% of sales happen on follow-up 2–3, not the first email. Use Zaram Web Mailer to automate this.
  • Trying to manually send emails. You'll send 20, get bored, and quit. Use bulk email software and send 200–300 per week consistently.
  • Not filtering by industry or location. Cold emailing random businesses wastes time. Use industry filters on your lead list (e-commerce, tech, logistics, real estate only).
  • Giving up after 2 weeks. Cold email takes 4–6 weeks to show results. If you quit at week 2, you're sabotaging yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How many cold emails should I send per week to get 1 accounting client?

A: Based on realistic conversion rates, send 150–200 cold emails per week. You'll get 12–30 replies (8–15% reply rate), book 3–8 calls (10–25% of replies), and close 1–3 clients (30–50% of calls). So roughly: 150 emails = 1–2 clients per week if you're targeting well. Most freelancers see their first client within 2–3 weeks of consistent outreach.

Q: Can I get accounting clients without cold email?

A: Yes, but it's slower. You can use WhatsApp outreach (see our guide on WhatsApp outreach for Nigerian freelancers), LinkedIn connection requests, or referrals from past clients. Cold email is fastest because it reaches people's inbox at scale. But if you hate email, WhatsApp works—it just takes longer and needs more personalization per message.

Q: What if businesses don't reply to my emails?

A: Most won't on the first email. That's normal. Follow up 2–3 times over 2 weeks (using Zaram Web Mailer to automate this). 80% of responses come on follow-up emails, not the original. Also, track your open rates—if fewer than 30% are opening your emails, your subject line is weak. If 40%+ open but only 5% reply, your email body isn't compelling enough. Test different subject lines and pain points until you hit 10%+ reply rate.

Q: Should I specialize in one industry or target all businesses?

A: Specialize. If you say "I do accounting for all businesses," you'll compete on price and experience. If you say "I help e-commerce sellers on Shopify and Jumia handle tax compliance and reconciliation," you're 10x more credible. Pick 2–3 industries (e.g., e-commerce, digital agencies, logistics), get really good at their accounting pain points, and mention that specific industry in your emails. Your close rate will jump from 20–30% to 50–60%.
