How to Use Google Sheets to Track Cold Email Campaigns: A Nigerian Freelancer's Guide

August 20, 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

You've sent 200 cold emails this week. But how many opened them? Who replied? Which subject line worked best? If you're scratching your head, you're not alone.

Most Nigerian freelancers either ignore tracking entirely—and lose money—or pay ₦5,000–₦15,000 monthly for tools like HubSpot or Mailchimp. But here's the truth: Google Sheets is free, powerful, and completely sufficient for tracking cold email campaigns when you know how to structure it.

In this guide, I'll show you exactly how to use Google Sheets to track cold email campaigns like a professional agency, without spending a naira on expensive software. By the end, you'll know which leads are worth following up, which emails are converting, and where to adjust your pitch.

Why Cold Email Tracking Matters (Especially in Nigeria)

Before we build your tracking sheet, let's talk numbers. A Nigerian freelancer earning ₦300,000–₦500,000 monthly typically spends 15–20 hours per week on outreach. That's real time, and time is money.

Without tracking, you're flying blind. You don't know:

  • Which industries actually reply (e.g., video production clients vs. logistics businesses)
  • What open rate you're actually achieving (typical cold email: 20–35%)
  • Which email template converts best
  • When to send follow-ups (3 days? 7 days?)
  • Your actual cost per lead and cost per conversion

A freelancer I worked with in Lagos was sending 50 cold emails weekly for 6 months with zero system. When we set up tracking, he discovered his open rate was only 12% (vs. the 25% benchmark). We changed his subject line, and it jumped to 31%. That single change generated an extra ₦180,000 in contracts that quarter.

Tracking doesn't just tell you what happened—it tells you what to do next.

Building Your Cold Email Tracking Sheet: Step-by-Step

Step 1: Set Up Your Master Column Headers

Open Google Sheets and create a new sheet. Name it "Cold Email Campaign [Month]" (e.g., "Cold Email Campaign August 2026").

Add these column headers in row 1:

  • A: Lead ID — Unique number (e.g., 001, 002, 003). Use =ROW()-1 to auto-generate
  • B: Company Name
  • C: Contact Name
  • D: Email Address
  • E: Phone
  • F: Website
  • G: Industry — e.g., "Video Production," "E-commerce," "Real Estate"
  • H: Email Template Used — e.g., "Template A," "Template B"
  • I: Subject Line
  • J: Date Sent
  • K: Email Opened? — TRUE/FALSE (or Yes/No)
  • L: Date Opened
  • M: Link Clicked? — TRUE/FALSE
  • N: Reply Received? — TRUE/FALSE
  • O: Date of Reply
  • P: Reply Type — "Positive," "Negative," "Question," "Not Interested"
  • Q: Follow-up Sent? — TRUE/FALSE
  • R: Follow-up Date
  • S: Status — "Prospect," "Qualified," "Deal Won," "Dead"
  • T: Deal Value (USD)
  • U: Notes

This might look like overkill, but you'll use every column. Trust me.

Step 2: Import Your Leads

If you're starting from scratch, the first thing you need is a quality lead list. Get one from Freelance LeadsHub—they provide downloadable CSV files with verified business emails, phone numbers, and websites segmented by niche and country. The Starter plan (₦20,000) gives you 8,500 leads, which is enough for 2–3 months of outreach.

Once you have your CSV:

  1. Go to File → Import → Upload or paste your CSV
  2. Choose "Insert new sheet"
  3. Click Import
  4. Paste or match the columns: Company Name, Contact Name, Email, Phone, Website
  5. Manually add the Industry column based on the data you downloaded

You now have your lead database. Don't delete it—keep it as a reference sheet.

Step 3: Prepare for Sending (Pre-Send Checklist)

Before you send a single email, fill in columns A–I for all leads you plan to contact:

  • Lead ID: Auto-numbered
  • Company/Contact Info: From your CSV
  • Industry: From your research (check their website or LinkedIn)
  • Email Template Used: Which version are you testing? (e.g., "Short & Direct," "Story-Driven," "Problem-First")
  • Subject Line: Copy-paste exactly what you'll send
  • Date Sent: Leave blank; you'll fill this after sending

This takes 2–3 minutes per batch of 50 emails, but it's critical. You're setting up the structure before the action happens.

Pro Tip: Color-code by industry. Select the Industry column, then use Format → Conditional Formatting to highlight "Video Production" in green, "Tech Startups" in blue, etc. This makes pattern-spotting instant.

Step 4: Send Emails with Tracking

Once your sheet is prepped, use Zaram Web Mailer to send your bulk emails. It integrates open and click tracking, and you can export the results back into your sheet.

Zaram provides tracking data automatically—opens show up in your email provider's analytics or via a tracking link you include in the email. When you send, also update column J (Date Sent) in your sheet with the send date.

Never send all 500 emails on Monday morning. Spread sends across 3–4 days, varying send times. Subject line "Quick question about [company]" sent Tuesday at 10 AM will have a different open rate than Friday at 6 PM.

Step 5: Populate Tracking Data (Opens, Clicks, Replies)

This is where discipline matters. Every day, spend 10 minutes updating your sheet with the previous day's results:

  • Column K (Email Opened?): Check your email provider's analytics or Zaram's dashboard. Mark TRUE if the email was opened within 48 hours of sending
  • Column L (Date Opened): If you're using Zaram, it logs the exact timestamp
  • Column M (Link Clicked?): Did they click your call-to-action link? Mark TRUE/FALSE
  • Column N (Reply Received?): Manual check—did they email you back?
  • Column O (Date of Reply): When did the reply arrive?
  • Column P (Reply Type): Was it positive ("Let's talk"), negative ("Not interested"), a question, or unclear?

Update this weekly. If you wait until the end of the month, you'll lose context and miss follow-up windows.

Creating Formulas to Measure Campaign Performance

Raw data means nothing. You need insights. Here are the formulas to add to your sheet (I'll put them in a "Metrics" section at the top or bottom of your sheet):

1. Open Rate Formula

In a cell, type:

=COUNTIF(K:K,TRUE)/COUNTA(D:D)*100

This divides total opens by total emails sent, multiplied by 100 for percentage. If you sent 100 emails and 28 opened, you get 28%.

2. Reply Rate Formula

=COUNTIF(N:N,TRUE)/COUNTA(D:D)*100

If 100 emails sent and 7 replies, you get 7% reply rate. (Industry benchmark: 2–5%.)

3. Conversion Rate (Deals Won / Emails Sent)

=COUNTIF(S:S,"Deal Won")/COUNTA(D:D)*100

Tells you what percentage of your outreach actually converted to paying work.

4. Cost Per Lead

If you paid ₦40,000 for 17,000 leads from LeadsHub and sent 500 emails, your cost per lead is ₦40,000 ÷ 500 = ₦80 per email.

Cost per conversion = ₦40,000 ÷ (number of deals) = your actual customer acquisition cost.

5. Average Deal Value

=AVERAGEIF(S:S,"Deal Won",T:T)

This calculates the average USD value of deals you've won. (See the example below.)

Real Example: Tracking a Campaign in Practice

Let's say you're a video editor in Ibadan and you send 100 cold emails to e-commerce businesses in Lagos:

Analyzing Your Data: What to Look For

1. Subject Line Performance

In your "Metrics" section, create a pivot table to compare open rates by subject line:

  • "Quick question about [company]" — 32% open rate
  • "Partnership opportunity" — 18% open rate
  • "I found [specific problem] on your site" — 41% open rate

Stop using "Partnership opportunity." It doesn't work for your audience. Double down on the specific-problem subject line.

2. Industry Performance

Do video production companies reply more than logistics businesses? Use COUNTIFS to measure:

=COUNTIFS(G:G,"Video Production",N:N,TRUE)

This counts replies only from the Video Production industry. Compare across industries and focus your effort where it works.

3. Template Effectiveness

If you're testing two email templates, compare their reply rates and conversion rates. This is how you optimize your pitch.

4. Follow-up Impact

Track when you sent follow-ups (column R) and how many turned into deals. You might find that following up 5 days later converts better than 3 days—that's gold.

Advanced: Conditional Formatting to Spot Trends Instantly

Select your Status column (S) and apply conditional formatting:

  • Green background for "Deal Won"
  • Yellow background for "Qualified" (positive reply, waiting on them)
  • Red background for "Dead" (negative or no follow-up)
  • Gray background for "Prospect" (no reply yet)

Instantly, you see your pipeline. You can scan 200 rows and spot trends in seconds.

Also format Column K (Email Opened?) with conditional formatting—green for TRUE, red for FALSE. You'll immediately see which batches had low open rates.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How accurate is open rate tracking in Google Sheets?

A: Open rate tracking depends on your email provider. Gmail marks emails as "opened" when images are loaded. Zaram Web Mailer uses pixel tracking, which is reliable to about 95%. The percentage might be 2–3% off in either direction, but it's good enough to spot trends. What matters is whether Template A has a 25% open rate and Template B has a 32%—the gap is real.

Q: Should I track phone calls as well as emails?

A: Absolutely. Add a column "Phone Contacted?" and "Phone Call Result?" Some Nigerian businesses prefer voice calls to emails. If you're doing outreach, include phone follow-ups after 3 days of no email response. Log the date, who answered, and what they said. You might find phone converts better than email for your niche.

Q: How many cold emails should a freelancer send per week in Nigeria?

A: Start with 25–50 per week to test your templates and subject lines. Once you see a 3%+ reply rate and your conversion is positive (e.g., ₦40,000 spent on leads = ₦200,000 in deals), scale to 100–200 per week. If you're already tracking with your sheet and know your numbers, you can be confident in scaling. Most successful Nigerian freelancers run 200–400 weekly once they optimize.

Q: What if I get a reply but the person ghosts after the first email back?

A: Mark them as "Interested but Unresponsive" in your Status column. Send one more follow-up 7 days later with a different angle (e.g., "Still interested in talking about improving your product videos?"). If no reply after that, mark as "Dead." Track this in your Notes column: "Ghosted after initial interest." Over time, you'll find that some segments (e.g., startups vs. established companies) ghost more than others—adjust your targeting accordingly.
