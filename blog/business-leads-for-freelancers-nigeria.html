10 August 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

Instead of waiting for clients to find you on a platform where they're haggling over price, you reach out to businesses directly with a specific solution to their problem. Done right, you can land ₦50,000–₦150,000 projects (or $120–$350 USD) without bidding wars.

Here are the seven methods I've seen work for Nigerian freelancers, plus the fastest way to execute them.

2. LinkedIn Direct Outreach (Free but Time-Intensive)

LinkedIn is packed with Nigerian business owners and decision-makers, but most freelancers use it wrong—they send generic connection requests and vague messages.

The right approach:

  1. Use LinkedIn search filters to find business owners in your target industry (e.g., search "owner" or "founder" in Nigeria)
  2. Look at their recent activity—what are they talking about?
  3. Send a personalized message referencing something specific (e.g., "I saw you posted about scaling your content—I've helped 5 brands in your space go from 2K to 20K followers")
  4. Ask for a brief call, not a project pitch yet

Reality check: This takes 3–5 hours per week but is free. You might get 1–2 qualified leads per week if you're consistent. That's ₦50,000–₦100,000/week in potential revenue, but only after weeks of grinding.

Most freelancers burn out because they don't see immediate returns. That's why a targeted lead list accelerates the process—you're not searching and qualifying, you're just pitching to pre-screened businesses.

3. Local Business Directories and Chambers

Nigerian business directories are goldmines that most freelancers ignore.

Check these:

  • Nigerian Business Directory (nigerianBusinessDirectory.com.ng) – searchable by industry and state
  • Lagos Chamber of Commerce (lcci.ng) – especially strong for businesses in Lagos and Southwest
  • Chambers of Commerce in your state – Google "[Your State] Chamber of Commerce Nigeria"
  • Business registrars like CAC (Corporate Affairs Commission) – you can search registered businesses by sector

The downside: Many directories are outdated or incomplete. You'll find 100 leads but 30% of contact details will be wrong.

The upside: They're free, and you can verify numbers quickly with WhatsApp or a quick call.

4. Google Maps and Local Search (Hyperlocal Method)

If you're targeting specific cities or industries, Google Maps is a direct lead source.

Here's the tactic:

  1. Open Google Maps
  2. Search for a business category (e.g., "digital marketing agencies Lagos" or "e-commerce stores Abuja")
  3. Scrape names, phone numbers, and websites from the listings
  4. Cross-reference websites to find decision-maker emails (use tools like Hunter.io or RocketReach)

Time investment: 2–3 hours to build a list of 100 local businesses. This is ideal if you want to work with clients in a specific city and offer local services like local SEO or video marketing.

5. WhatsApp Business Groups and Facebook Groups

This is uniquely Nigerian. Business owners hang out in WhatsApp groups—industry groups, business owner mastermind groups, state business associations.

Find groups relevant to your niche:

  • Search Facebook groups: "Nigeria [Industry] Business Owners" (e.g., "Nigeria E-commerce Business Owners")
  • Ask fellow freelancers or friends for WhatsApp group invites
  • Join state-based entrepreneur groups
  • Participate genuinely—answer questions, share value, build trust

Once you're in, you'll see business owners asking for help: "Does anyone know a good copywriter?" or "Looking for someone to redesign our website." That's your cue to respond with your portfolio and rates.

Realistic conversion: 5–10% of people who see your recommendation will hire you. In a group of 500 active members, you might land 1–2 clients per month at ₦30,000–₦80,000 each.

6. Referral Networks and Partnerships

Build relationships with other service providers who target the same clients but don't compete with you.

Examples of partnerships:

  • You're a web designer—partner with a WordPress developer and a content writer
  • You're a video editor—partner with videographers and social media managers
  • You're a copywriter—partner with designers and email marketers

Offer a 10–15% referral fee (or reciprocal referrals) and watch your pipeline fill. One partner sending you 1 client per month = ₦50,000–₦100,000/month in extra revenue.

7. Cold Email Campaigns (High-Volume, High-Reward)

Cold email is the most scalable method if done right. The formula:

  1. Get a lead list (either from Freelance LeadsHub or scrape it yourself)
  2. Write a short, benefit-focused email (3–4 sentences max)
  3. Personalize with the business name and a specific insight
  4. Include a clear CTA: "Can I send you a quick example?"
  5. Send via Zaram Web Mailer with built-in follow-ups

Example email for a copywriter:

Hi [Name],
I noticed [Business Name] sells [product] but your homepage doesn't mention the main benefit your customers care about.
I've rewritten homepages for 3 similar brands—average result: 18% more clicks to the product page.
Want me to do a quick 30-second audit of yours?
[Link]
Cheers,
[Your name]

Expected results: 2–5% open rate (with a good subject line), 0.5–2% click-through rate, 10–20% of people who engage will book a call.

If you send 500 emails, expect 2–5 conversations and 0–1 project at ₦50,000–₦150,000. That's why volume matters—you need to send 2,000–5,000 emails per month to build a consistent pipeline.

This is also why understanding why clients ignore cold emails is critical—most Nigerian freelancers send poorly written, generic emails that get deleted immediately.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is cold emailing legal in Nigeria?

A: Yes. Cold emailing (unsolicited business emails) is legal in Nigeria as long as you're not spamming, using false information, or sending fraudulent offers. You must provide a way for recipients to opt-out (a simple "reply and ask to be removed" works). Using Zaram Web Mailer ensures you're following best practices for unsubscribes and not hitting spam filters, which keeps you compliant and effective.

Q: How long does it take to see results from cold email outreach?

A: Typically 1–3 weeks if you're sending consistently (100+ emails/week). First responses come in days 3–7, but most conversations happen in weeks 2–4 as your follow-ups land. If you send 500 emails in week 1, you might get 2–5 replies, then 1–2 conversations that turn into meetings or projects by week 3–4. Patience and volume are both required.
