How to Get Mobile App Development Clients in Nigeria: A Freelancer's Guide

14 August 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

If you're a mobile app developer in Nigeria, you already know the problem: Upwork and Fiverr are crowded. You're competing against developers from Pakistan, India, and Eastern Europe who undercut your rates. Your ₦50,000 quote gets beaten by someone offering the same work for ₦15,000. It's draining.

But here's what most Nigerian app developers miss: you don't have to compete on freelance platforms at all. Instead, you can directly approach Nigerian businesses that need apps but haven't found you yet. That's how you get mobile app development clients in Nigeria — by reaching out to them first, on your own terms, at rates that actually pay your bills.

This guide shows you exactly how.

Why Nigerian Businesses Need App Developers (And Don't Know Where to Find You)

Nigeria's digital economy is booming. According to recent data, the fintech, e-commerce, logistics, and real estate sectors are all expanding rapidly. But most small-to-medium businesses (SMBs) in Nigeria don't browse Upwork. They don't have time. They need a solution, and they're willing to pay for it — if you reach them directly.

Consider this: a logistics company in Lagos needs a driver-tracking app. A real estate firm in Abuja wants a property listing mobile app. An e-commerce startup needs an iOS and Android presence. These aren't people posting jobs on Fiverr for ₦5,000. These are decision-makers with actual budgets.

The catch? They don't advertise those jobs publicly. They expect vendors to come to them.

2. WhatsApp and Phone Outreach (The Follow-Up Path)

Cold email gets attention. WhatsApp gets response. After your initial email, many businesses won't reply in their inbox, but they'll respond on WhatsApp.

Here's the sequence:

  1. Send cold email (days 1–3).
  2. Wait 3 days, then send a WhatsApp follow-up: "Hi [Name], I sent you an email about app development for [their business type]. Are you open to a quick chat?"
  3. If no response after 5 days, call. Yes, actually call. Most Nigerian business owners answer their phones.
  4. During the call, position yourself: "I build custom apps for [their industry]. I worked on a similar [project type] for [another business in their space]. Can we schedule 15 minutes to chat?"

3. Referrals and Networking (The Long-Term Path)

Your first five app clients should come from cold outreach. Your next ten should come from referrals. Why? Because one happy client refers you to five others in their industry.

Build a referral system:

  • Deliver exceptional work. This is non-negotiable.
  • Ask your client for introductions. "Who else in your network could benefit from an app like this?" Most will happily introduce you.
  • Offer a referral bonus. ₦50,000 for a successful referral adds up fast and incentivizes your clients to promote you.
  • Join Nigerian tech communities. Facebook groups, Slack communities, Twitter spaces — where business owners and founders hang out.

What to Charge for App Development in Nigeria

This matters because it sets the tone for your entire career. If you undercharge, you'll always be poor.

Hourly rates are a trap. Don't quote by the hour. Quote by project scope. Here's what Nigerian app developers actually charge in 2026:

  • Simple app (basic e-commerce, service listing, MVP): ₦300,000–₦800,000 ($200–$540 USD).
  • Medium app (custom logistics tracking, real estate marketplace, fintech prototype): ₦1,000,000–₦3,000,000 ($680–$2,000 USD).
  • Complex app (full fintech platform, advanced AI features, multi-platform): ₦5,000,000–₦15,000,000+ ($3,400–$10,000+ USD).

Most app projects fall into the "medium" category. That's ₦1.2M–₦2M per project. If you close one client per month, that's ₦14.4M–₦24M per year. At current exchange rates (₦1,650–₦1,700 per USD), that's roughly $8,500–$15,000 USD per month. Enough to live comfortably in Nigeria, pay your taxes, and reinvest.

How to Avoid Common Mistakes

Mistake 1: Generic Cold Emails

A generic "Hi, I build apps" email gets ignored. Your email should show you know their business:

Bad: "Hi, I'm a mobile app developer. I can build your app. Let me know if interested."

Good: "Hi [Name], I noticed [Company] operates in [sector]. I recently built a similar app for [comparable company], which reduced their [metric] by 40%. Given your expansion plans, a custom app could unlock [specific benefit]. Can we chat briefly?"

Mistake 2: Underestimating Project Scope

A client says, "I want an app like Uber." They think it's a weekend project. It's not. Always scope properly. Break down requirements. Get written approval before coding. Use a contract.

Mistake 3: Not Following Up

Most deals happen on follow-up 4–5, not the first email. Send at least 5 follow-ups over 4 weeks before marking a lead cold.

Mistake 4: Accepting Payment in Naira Only

If you're earning in Naira but paying for tools, hosting, and software in USD, you'll bleed money on exchange rate fluctuations. Use Payoneer, Grey, or Wise to receive payments in USD and convert when the rate is favorable. This alone can save you 5–10% on projects.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How many cold emails do I need to send to get one client?

A: On average, expect a 2–5% conversion rate from initial cold email to a qualified conversation. Of those conversations, 30–50% close into actual projects. So to land one ₦1.5M app project, you might need to send 50–100 cold emails. That's why volume matters — and why tools like Zaram Web Mailer that handle bulk sending without spam issues are essential.

Q: Should I specialize in one type of app or build everything?

A: Specialize. If you say "I build any app," businesses assume you're average at everything. Instead, say "I build fintech apps for Nigerian startups" or "I specialize in e-commerce mobile apps." This positioning makes cold emails more effective because prospects think, "This person understands our industry," and they're more likely to respond. Check our guide on cold email strategy for Nigerian freelancers for more on niche positioning.

Q: How do I handle clients who want to pay only after the app is live?

A: Don't. Use milestone payments. Typically: 30% upfront to start development, 40% at mid-point, 30% on delivery and testing. This protects you and signals to the client that you're professional. If they refuse upfront payment, they're a bad client. Move on to the next lead.

Q: Can I combine cold email with other strategies like freelance platforms?

A: Yes, but prioritize cold email. The marginal effort of also maintaining an Upwork profile is minimal, but the income difference is huge — direct clients pay 3–10x more than platform clients because there's no middleman taking commission. Start with cold outreach, build your client base, and use Upwork/Fiverr only as a secondary source or to fill gaps between projects.
