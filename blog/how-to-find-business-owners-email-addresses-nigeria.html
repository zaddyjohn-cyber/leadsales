But here's the real question: How do you actually find these email addresses? This article walks you through 7 practical methods that work in Nigeria's business landscape—plus the fastest shortcut if you want leads ready to email today.

Why Email Addresses Matter More Than Ever

You've probably heard "cold email doesn't work." That's not true. What doesn't work is cold email without research, without personalization, and without a real value proposition.

Here's what the data shows: A well-researched cold email to a business owner in Nigeria has a 15–25% response rate. That's realistic—not the fantasy "80% response rates" some gurus sell you. But consider this: if you send 100 personalized emails to relevant business owners, you're likely to get 15–25 replies. Of those, maybe 4–6 turn into clients. At ₦150,000–₦300,000 per month per client, that's ₦600,000–₦1,800,000 in new revenue from one batch of emails.

Compare that to bidding on Upwork where you send 20 proposals and get 1 response that wants to negotiate you down by 40%.

The barrier isn't whether cold email works. The barrier is finding the right email addresses. Let's fix that.

Method 1: Direct Website Research (Free, Takes Time)

The slowest but most thorough method is visiting business websites directly and looking for contact pages.

How to do it:

  1. Identify businesses in your niche (e.g., restaurants, digital marketing agencies, e-commerce stores)
  2. Search "best [business type] in Lagos" or "[business type] in Abuja" on Google
  3. Visit each business website's "Contact" page, "About" page, or footer
  4. Look for: info@, contact@, hello@, business owner's direct email (often listed as "CEO", "Founder", "Manager")
  5. If the website doesn't list an email, check their social media bios (LinkedIn, Instagram) for links to email or contact forms

Time investment: 30–45 minutes per 20 businesses.

Reality check: Most small Nigerian businesses have incomplete websites. Many list only phone numbers or WhatsApp. This method works best for medium-sized businesses with professional websites.

Method 2: LinkedIn Advanced Search (Free, Moderate Results)

LinkedIn is the professional network—many Nigerian business owners maintain profiles there, and some list their email publicly.

How to do it:

  1. Go to LinkedIn and use advanced search filters: Location = Nigeria, Industry = (your niche), Title = "Owner", "Founder", "CEO", "Director"
  2. Visit each profile. Many list their email in the contact info section or in the "About" section
  3. If no email is visible, check if they have a company website listed (then use Method 1)
  4. If still no email, you can send a LinkedIn message or use their LinkedIn URL as a basis for guessing their email format (more on that below)

Challenge: LinkedIn's free version limits your searches, and most emails aren't publicly listed. Premium costs $39/month—worth it if you're doing this consistently.

Pro tip: Many Nigerian business owners use standard email formats: firstname@companyname.com, firstname.lastname@companyname.com, or initials@companyname.com. If you find a company website from LinkedIn, you can try these variations.

Method 3: Google Maps & Business Directories (Free)

Nigerian businesses—especially local service providers—often show up on Google Maps with contact details.

How to do it:

  1. Search "[business type] near me" or "[business type] in [city]" on Google Maps
  2. Click each listing and check the "Website" or "Contact" buttons
  3. Many businesses list their email right there on the Maps listing
  4. Expand to Nigerian business directories: Naijafy.com, Naija.com business sections, or industry-specific directories

Best for: Local service businesses (plumbers, electricians, cleaners, event planners, photographers).

Method 4: Email Finding Tools (Paid, Fast, Reliable)

Tools like Hunter.io, RocketReach, and Clearbit are designed to find email addresses from company names and websites. They're not free, but they save huge amounts of time.

Popular options:

  • Hunter.io: ₦8,000–₦15,000/month. Search by company name or domain, get email addresses with confidence scores. Works well for Nigerian businesses with formal websites.
  • RocketReach: Similar pricing, slightly broader data, but often returns less verified results.
  • Clearbit: More expensive (₦20,000+/month) but higher accuracy. Overkill unless you're sending thousands of emails.

Realistic cost-benefit: If you're sending 50 emails and landing 1–2 clients worth ₦200,000 each, paying ₦10,000 for a month of Hunter.io is a 20x return. Do it.

Important: These tools work best if the Nigerian business has a professional website and uses a business domain (not Gmail). Smaller businesses with Gmail addresses won't appear in these databases.

Method 6: Industry-Specific Associations & Regulatory Bodies

Many Nigerian industries have formal associations that publish member lists with contact details.

Examples:

  • Manufacturers: Manufacturers Association of Nigeria (MAN) publishes a directory
  • Accountants: Institute of Chartered Accountants of Nigeria (ICAN)
  • Lawyers: Nigerian Bar Association (NBA)
  • Importers/Exporters: Nigeria Customs Service or Nigerian Export Promotion Council
  • Real Estate: Nigerian Real Estate Professional Body
  • Banks & Finance: Central Bank of Nigeria publishes licensed institution lists

Contact these associations and ask if they sell or provide member contact lists. Some are free, some charge ₦5,000–₦20,000. Many won't respond to an email, so you may need to call or visit in person.

Time investment: High. Bureaucracy is real. But if you're targeting a specific industry (e.g., accountants needing bookkeeping freelancers), this can give you a gold-standard list.

Method 7: Your Existing Network & Referrals (Free, Highest Quality)

Don't sleep on this. Your existing clients, colleagues, and friends know other business owners.

How to do it:

  1. Tell your current network (via WhatsApp, email, or in-person conversation) exactly who you want to reach: "I'm looking to pitch to restaurant owners in Lagos who need social media management"
  2. Ask for introductions or email addresses. Offer a small referral fee if they refer a client who signs a contract (₦5,000–₦10,000)
  3. Referrals have a 50%+ close rate because there's already trust built in

If you land 1 client per month via network referrals, that alone pays ₦150,000–₦300,000. It's slower to scale than cold email, but the quality is unmatched.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Are the email addresses from Freelance LeadsHub actually verified?

A: Yes. Freelance LeadsHub periodically tests email addresses in their database to ensure they're current and bounce-resistant. However, no database is 100% accurate—some businesses change emails, some go out of business. Expect a 90–95% delivery rate, which is industry-standard. Some emails may not respond, but the address itself will be valid. If you're concerned, you can use a tool like Zaram Web Mailer to monitor delivery and bounce rates as you send.

Q: How do I know if the email I found is actually the business owner and not a receptionist or assistant?

A: LinkedIn is your best friend here. Search the email or phone number on LinkedIn to see if a profile matches the business owner. If the person's title is "CEO", "Founder", or "Owner", you've got the right contact. If they're listed as "Customer Service Manager" or similar, they might not be the decision-maker. For important pitches, it's worth checking. Freelance LeadsHub includes job titles in their data, so you can filter for decision-makers directly.

Q: Should I cold email in English or Nigerian Pidgin English?

A: English, always. Business owners in Nigeria expect professional communication in English, even if they speak Pidgin casually. Keep it professional, clear, and direct. However, research your prospect on social media first—if they communicate informally online, you can mirror that tone slightly while keeping the email professional. A/B testing shows English gets 3x better response rates from business owners than informal language.

Q: What's the best time of day to send cold emails in Nigeria?

A: Tuesday–Thursday, 9 AM–12 PM Nigerian time (UTC+1). This is when business owners are typically in the office and checking emails. Avoid Mondays (too busy catching up) and Fridays (focus drops).
