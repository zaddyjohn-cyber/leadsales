Let's be clear: Upwork and Fiverr aren't where Nigerian businesses look for serious work. They're where international bargain hunters go. Nigerian businesses—the ones with real money—are on Google, in WhatsApp groups, and in industry directories. They're not bidding. They're hiring.

Here's what happens when you use business directories instead:

  • Less competition. You're not competing with 50 other freelancers on the same bid. You're one of maybe 3–5 people reaching out.
  • Better rates. A business that found you via directory outreach will pay ₦30,000–₦50,000 for graphic design, not ₦5,000. We're talking 5–10x difference.
  • Recurring work. Businesses need ongoing services. One client becomes 2–3 projects per month. One Upwork client does one ₦8,000 gig and disappears.
  • Faster decisions. Businesses contact you directly because they're serious. No back-and-forth bidding. No waiting 10 days for a response.

Real math: If you land 4 projects per month at ₦200,000 each ($285 USD) via directory outreach, you're earning ₦800,000 monthly (≈$1,140 USD at current rates). On Upwork, most Nigerian freelancers average ₦80,000–₦120,000 per month. That's a 7–10x difference, and the work is less stressful.

There are actual business directories where Nigerian companies list themselves. Your job is to either manually extract leads from them or—much faster—use a tool that does it automatically.

Free & Low-Cost Directories to Search Manually

  1. Google My Business / Google Maps – Search your niche + "Nigeria" (e.g., "digital marketing agency Lagos," "e-commerce business Abuja"). Export names, phone numbers, and email addresses. You can do this manually, or use Google Maps to extract CSV leads more efficiently.
  2. Chamber of Commerce Nigeria – The Nigerian Association of Chambers of Commerce, Industry, Mines and Agriculture (NACCIMA) has member directories. Look for businesses in your vertical.
  3. Naijafy & LocalBiz – These are Nigerian business listing platforms where you can search by industry and state.
  4. LinkedIn Company Search – Filter by "Nigeria," industry, company size (50–500 employees = good target). You'll find contact info for decision-makers.
  5. Yellow Pages Nigeria & Jiji.ng – Old-school but surprisingly useful. Jiji is newer and has recent business listings.

How to Extract & Organize Leads from Business Directories

Whether you're using a directory tool or manual research, you need to organize leads properly before outreach.

Step 1: Define Your Target Business Profile

Don't email every business in Nigeria. That's spam, and your open rates will be 2–3%. Instead, target specific criteria:

  • Industry: What type of business needs your service? (E.g., if you're a copywriter, target e-commerce businesses, SaaS companies, agencies.)
  • Company size: 10–100 employees is ideal. Big enough to have budget. Small enough to make fast decisions.
  • Location: Start with Lagos, Abuja, and Port Harcourt. These have the most businesses with actual spending power.
  • Revenue indicators: Look for businesses with 5+ years in operation, professional websites, active social media.

Step 2: Extract the Right Contact Information

When pulling leads, you need:

  1. Business name
  2. Email address (not info@, try to find the decision-maker's personal email)
  3. Phone number
  4. Website (if available)
  5. LinkedIn profile (optional, for research)

If you're using Freelance LeadsHub, this is all pre-organized in CSV format. If you're extracting manually from Google Maps or LinkedIn, use a spreadsheet and fill in gaps with a quick Google search or phone call.

Step 3: Create a Spreadsheet & Remove Duplicates

Use Google Sheets or Excel. Columns: Business Name | Contact Person | Email | Phone | Website | Industry | Status (Not Contacted / Contacted / Interested / Replied).

Remove duplicate email addresses. Sort by industry so you can customize your pitch.

Cold Email Strategy: Turning Directory Leads into Clients

Having leads is half the battle. Converting them into clients is the other half. Here's the framework that works:

The Three-Message Sequence

Message 1 (Day 1): The soft intro. Short (3–4 sentences). Mention something specific about their business. Ask a simple question. Don't pitch yet.

Example:

Hi [Name], I noticed you launched three new products on your store last month. Congrats. Quick question—are you creating video content for each one, or sticking with just images? I ask because most e-commerce businesses I work with see 40% higher conversion when they add product videos. Let me know if that's something you've thought about. –[Your name]

Message 2 (Day 4): Follow-up if no reply. Add a case study or result. Still no hard pitch.

Example:

Hi [Name], Following up on my last note. I worked with [Company Name] (similar to yours) and helped them reduce product photography costs by 60% using [your method]. Thought it might be useful. Cheers, –[Your name]

Message 3 (Day 7): Final follow-up with a clear call-to-action. After this, move on to the next batch.

Example:

Hi [Name], Last note, then I'll let you be. If you're ever interested in exploring [your service], I'm only taking on 2–3 new clients this quarter. Would be happy to chat for 15 minutes. Calendar link: [calendly link]. Otherwise, no worries. –[Your name]

What to Avoid (Common Mistakes)

  • Generic subject lines. "Quick question" or "Let's work together" gets deleted. Use specific subject lines: "Product video question for [Company Name]" or "[Name], 60% savings idea."
  • Pitching in email 1. Wait until email 2 or 3. Email 1 is just to get a response.
  • Long emails. Busy business owners scan emails in 10 seconds. Keep it to 3–4 sentences per email.
  • Sending from a Gmail account with no website. Use your own domain (even freelanceleadsfreelancer.com looks more professional than gmail).
  • Not personalizing at all. Mention something from their website, recent news, or product launch. Shows you did research.

Critical Tip: Finding Decision-Maker Email Addresses

A lead is only valuable if it reaches the right person. Sending to info@business.com is useless. You need the owner, manager, or decision-maker.

Here's how to find them:

  1. Google search: "[Business Name] founder" or "[Business Name] owner" + email.
  2. LinkedIn: Find the company page. Look at employees listed. The people with "Founder," "Managing Director," or "Operations" in their title.
  3. Website team page: Many businesses list their team with email addresses.
  4. Cold call: Ring the business. Ask: "Hi, who handles [your service] decisions here? Can I have their email?" Takes 30 seconds. They'll usually tell you.
  5. Email pattern guessing: If the company is ABC Company and the founder is John Smith, try: john@abc.com, j.smith@abc.com, john.smith@abc.com. Use Hunter.io to verify which one is real.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How many cold emails should I send per day to avoid being marked as spam?

A: If you're using Zaram Web Mailer or a professional platform, you can safely send 50–100 emails per day without hitting spam filters. The key is warmup—start with 10–15 per day for the first week, then scale up. If you're sending from personal Gmail without a warmup period, stick to 20–30 per day max. The platform matters more than the number.

Q: What's the best way to find leads for a specific service in Nigeria (e.g., video editing)?

A: Use Freelance LeadsHub to filter by "media production companies," "advertising agencies," "e-commerce businesses," and "content creators" in your target state. These are the businesses that hire video editors. Then cross-reference on Google Maps and LinkedIn for verification. You want 300–500 verified leads minimum before starting cold email.

Q: How long before I see responses from cold emails in Nigeria?

A: First responses typically come 2–5 days after the initial email. Nigerian business owners are busy, so patience is key. Run your three-email sequence over 7 days (day 1, 4, 7). By day 10, you'll have your response rate. Don't expect replies on day 1; most come on days 3–5. If you're sending 100+ emails per month, you'll have a constant stream of replies.

Q: Should I cold call businesses before emailing them?

A: Email first, phone second. An email is easier to ignore, but easier to send at scale. Once someone replies to your email or reads it (check with Zaram tracking), then you call. Calling a cold lead who's never heard of you is harder and less effective. Email builds awareness. Phone closes the deal.
