Why Shopify Dropshipping Store Owners Are High-Value Targets

Before we talk about finding them, understand why they're worth your time:

  • They have money: A store doing ₦2,000,000/month in sales typically has ₦400,000–₦600,000 in profit. They can afford ₦100,000–₦250,000/month for freelancers.
  • They're always hiring: Dropshipping store owners scale fast. When they hit ₦1,000,000/month, they immediately need ads managers, copywriters, and customer service support.
  • They understand freelancing: Unlike traditional brick-and-mortar businesses, dropshippers already work with remote teams, use Stripe/PayPal, and pay via Wise or bank transfer.
  • Urgent problems = quick decisions: A store bleeding money on bad ads will hire you in 48 hours if you prove you can fix it.

Reality check: A Shopify dropshipping store owner earning ₦3,000,000/month in gross revenue will happily pay you ₦200,000/month to manage Facebook ads if you increase their ROAS by just 15–20%. That's a ₦450,000 monthly problem solved for ₦200,000. They win. You win.

Method 1: Direct Shopify Store Intelligence (Free but Manual)

The most direct approach is finding Shopify stores, verifying they're dropshipping, and pitching the owner.

Step 1: Use Whatruns to Identify Facebook Ad Campaigns

Install the Whatruns browser extension (whatastatics.com). When you visit a Shopify store, Whatruns shows you:

  • Active Facebook/Instagram ad campaigns
  • The ad images and copy they're running
  • Which platforms they're advertising on
  • Estimated ad spend (rough)

If a store is running active ads, they're serious about scaling. This is your target.

Step 2: Find Their Email and Contact Info

Look for:

  • Footer links: "Contact Us" pages usually list an email or WhatsApp
  • About page: Many store owners list their email or social handles
  • Email finder tools: Use Hunter.io (free tier gives 10/month) or RocketReach to find the owner's email from the domain
  • LinkedIn: Search for the store name + "founder" or "owner"
  • Facebook page: Most Shopify stores have a linked Facebook business page with contact details

Step 3: Qualify the Store (Crucial)

Not all dropshipping stores are worth pitching. Check:

  • Store age: Older than 3 months? Good—they're stable enough to invest in freelancers.
  • Product variety: 20+ products? They're thinking long-term. 3 products? They might fold in a month.
  • Reviews: Any customer reviews? Even 5–10 reviews show real sales.
  • Ad activity: Are they running ads consistently? Or did they spend ₦50,000 once in December?

Time investment: This method takes 2–3 hours per lead and yields ~5 qualified prospects per week if you're disciplined. It works, but it's slow.

Crafting a Pitch That Converts (Real Template)

Finding store owners is half the battle. Your pitch is the other half. Here's what works:

Subject Line (Email)

Avoid generic subject lines. Use specifics:

  • "Quick idea for your fashion store (25% more sales in 30 days)"
  • "Your Instagram ads are leaving ₦400K on the table monthly"
  • "I increased [similar store]'s revenue by ₦800K/month—here's how"

Email Body (3-Part Formula)

Part 1 – Proof you're relevant (2–3 sentences):

Hi [Name], I noticed your [product] store is running ads on Facebook. I help store owners like you reduce ad spend by 30–40% while increasing orders. Last month, I helped a store in [similar niche] go from ₦200K to ₦450K monthly orders.

Part 2 – One specific insight (2–3 sentences):

Looking at your store, I see an opportunity: Your product pages take 3.2 seconds to load (slow for mobile users). That alone costs you 15–20% of clicks. We can fix this + optimize your ad targeting and copy.

Part 3 – CTA (1 sentence):

Open to a 15-minute call next week? I'll share specific insights on how to hit ₦1M/month if you're not there yet.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Pitching service, not results: "I do social media management" fails. "I increased your competitor's engagement by 180% and added 50 orders/month" works.
  • One-off pitches: Send one email, wait for silence. Use a follow-up sequence: initial email → 3 days → follow-up → 3 days → final follow-up. Three touches convert 5–10x better than one.
  • No proof: Show case studies, testimonials, or at minimum your own store's metrics. "I helped X store go from Y to Z" beats "Trust me, I'm good."
  • Ignoring bad fits: A 2-week-old store with zero reviews isn't worth your time. Focus on stores 3+ months old with ₦500K+ monthly revenue.
  • Vague pricing: Don't say "Let's discuss rates." Say "I charge ₦200,000/month for ads management with a 30-day trial period. If we don't see a 15% improvement in ROAS, no charge."

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How do I know if a Shopify store is actually dropshipping and not inventory-based?

A: Look for telltale signs: (1) They sell wildly diverse products across unrelated categories (gadgets + cosmetics + fashion = dropshipping). (2) Their "About Us" mentions sourcing from suppliers rather than manufacturing. (3) They have fulfilment centers listed in the footer (typically China, Dubai, or US). (4) Their delivery times are long (14–28 days = dropshipping; 2–5 days = inventory-based). Dropshippers often state their model openly because it's their selling point—fast setup, low risk.

Q: What if the store owner ignores my first email?

A: Follow up. Most Nigerian freelancers send one email and give up. Send a second email 3 days later with a new angle—maybe a specific metric you found about their store, or a case study of how you helped a similar business. Send a third email after another 3 days. Studies show 40–50% of responses come from the second or third email. Use Zaram Web Mailer to automate this process.

Q: Can I pitch multiple services to the same store owner (ads + copywriting + design)?

A: Yes, but don't do it in your first pitch. Start with your strongest skill. Once they hire you for ads management and see results, propose "I've noticed your product pages could convert 20% better with new copy—want me to handle that too?" This upselling happens naturally after you prove yourself. Pitching everything at once makes you look unfocused.

Q: How much should I charge Nigerian dropshipping store owners?

A: Base it on their monthly revenue and the value you provide. A store doing ₦1,000,000/month should pay ₦100,000–₦200,000 for ads management. A store doing ₦5,000,000/month should pay ₦300,000–₦500,000. Your rate isn't about "hours worked"—it's about the profit increase you generate. If you increase their monthly profit by ₦500,000, charging ₦150,000 is a steal for them. Always emphasize ROI, not your time.
