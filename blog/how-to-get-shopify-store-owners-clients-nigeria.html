How to Get Shopify Store Owners as Clients: A Practical Guide for Nigerian Freelancers

If you're a Nigerian freelancer—whether a designer, copywriter, social media manager, or developer—Shopify store owners are some of the highest-paying clients you can land. A store owner running ₦2–5 million in monthly revenue isn't shopping on Fiverr or Upwork for a ₦5,000 gig. They're looking for professionals who understand their business and can directly impact their bottom line.

The problem? Most freelancers don't know how to find these clients or how to pitch them. They keep competing on saturated platforms instead of going direct. This guide shows you exactly how to get Shopify store owners as clients in Nigeria—with realistic income figures, timelines, and the tools that actually work.

Why Shopify Store Owners Are Your Best Client Bet

Let's start with numbers. A typical Shopify store owner in Nigeria making ₦1–3 million monthly will happily pay ₦150,000–₦500,000 for:

  • Professional product photography and editing
  • Conversion-optimized landing page copywriting
  • Email marketing sequences (to re-engage customers)
  • Facebook and Instagram ad management
  • Website design or Shopify theme customization
  • Monthly content creation and product descriptions
  • Customer service automation setup

In USD terms, that's $100–$300+ per project. If you land 2–3 Shopify clients per month at this rate, you're earning $200–$900 monthly just from them. That's ₦360,000–₦1.6 million naira monthly—far more sustainable than gig-stacking on Upwork.

But here's the real advantage: Shopify owners are motivated. They have cash flow from sales, they understand ROI, and they'll pay for results. They're not the "I'll pay you when I get paid" type you encounter on freelance platforms.

The Core Problem: Finding Shopify Store Owners in Nigeria

You can't just Google "Shopify store owners Nigeria" and get a list. Shopify stores don't come with a publicly searchable database. You need either:

  1. Manual hunting: Browsing Shopify stores manually (slow, exhausting, low ROI)
  2. Paid tools: Shopify explorer tools like Koala Inspector (expensive, most show international stores)
  3. Targeted lead lists: Pre-compiled, verified lists of Shopify store owner emails and phone numbers by country

Most Nigerian freelancers don't realize option 3 exists. They waste weeks trying option 1 and give up.

Key Insight: The fastest way to start getting Shopify clients isn't "outreach strategy"—it's having a verified list of Shopify store owners' emails and phone numbers ready to go. Everything else flows from having real contact data.

Step 2: Craft a Cold Email That Gets Responses

You have the list. Now you need an email that makes a Shopify owner open it, read it, and reply. Most cold emails fail because they're generic. Here's what works:

Structure of a High-Converting Cold Email

  1. Subject line (curiosity + specificity):
    "Quick question about your [product category] sales on Instagram"
    Not: "Let's work together" (boring, deleted instantly)
  2. Personalization hook (first 1–2 lines):
    "Hi [Name], I was browsing your store yesterday—your [specific product] caught my eye. I noticed [observation about their store/marketing]."
    This shows you actually looked at their store, not a bot sending 1,000 emails.
  3. Problem identification (what they're likely struggling with):
    "Most store owners I talk to say their biggest challenge is converting browsers to buyers. A lot of traffic, but sales stay flat."
    This is relatable. They've felt this.
  4. Your solution (brief, one service only):
    "I help e-commerce stores improve their checkout flow and email follow-up sequences. Clients typically see a 15–30% increase in repeat purchases within 60 days."
    Be specific. Give a number. One service, not five.
  5. Social proof (if you have it):
    "I recently helped a Shopify store in Lagos go from 12% to 28% repeat customer rate."
    Even if it's one client, use it. Real numbers > fluff.
  6. Soft CTA (low-pressure ask):
    "Would you be open to a 15-minute call next week to walk through some quick wins for your store?"
    Not "Hire me now." Just a conversation.
  7. Sign-off:
    "—[Your Name] | [Your title] | [Your phone or link]"
    Include a WhatsApp number. Nigerians respond faster on WhatsApp than email.

Step 4: Follow-Up (This Is Where Deals Actually Close)

Most Nigerian freelancers give up after one email. Don't. Your follow-up sequence matters more than your first email.

  1. Day 0: First cold email
  2. Day 7: Follow-up #1 (reference your first email, add a specific case study)

Step 5: Convert Leads Into Paid Projects

A response or meeting doesn't mean a sale. Here's how to close:

During the Call/WhatsApp Chat

  • Ask questions first, pitch second. "What's your biggest frustration with your store right now?" Listen.
  • Diagnose the real problem. They might say "low sales" but the real issue is poor product photography or unclear messaging.
  • Propose one small project first. "Let's start with me rewriting your product descriptions for your top 10 products. It'll take me a week, and I'll charge you ₦40,000. If you see results, we can scale."
  • Get a commitment. "Can you confirm by tomorrow so I can block off time for your project?"

  • Facebook/Instagram ad management (10% of ad spend): varies

Start low to prove yourself. Land your first Shopify client at ₦50,000, deliver excellent work, then upsell to ₦150,000+ monthly retainers once they see ROI.

Common Mistakes Nigerian Freelancers Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Mistake 1: Buying a List But Not Following Up

You download 400 leads, send one email, get 5 replies, then forget about it. The other 395 never hear from you again. Commit to a 4-email follow-up sequence. That's where real conversions happen.

Mistake 2: Pitching Too Many Services at Once

"I can do copywriting, design, social media, ads, video—pick what you need." Confusing. Pitch one thing: "I help Shopify stores increase repeat customer rate." That's it. If they say yes, you upsell later.

Mistake 3: No Social Proof in Your First Pitch

A store owner wants proof you've done this before. Even if you've only worked with one client, use that. "I recently helped a Shopify store in Ikoyi increase their email revenue by 40%." Real number, real result. Builds trust instantly.

Mistake 4: Setting Prices Too Low

Shopify owners have budgets. Charging ₦15,000 for email copywriting signals low quality. Charge ₦100,000+. If they don't bite, they weren't serious anyway. Better to land 1 client at ₦200,000 than 10 at ₦20,000.

Mistake 5: Not Converting Leads to Retainers

First project: one-time ₦100,000 gig. Then what? A month later, you're back to zero. Instead, propose monthly retainers: "I can manage your email campaigns for ₦80,000/month." That's sustainable income.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How do I find Shopify store owners in Nigeria specifically?

A: The easiest way is to use Freelance LeadsHub, which lets you download filtered lists of Nigeria-based e-commerce business owners with their contact details. Alternatively, you can manually search LinkedIn for "Shopify store owner Nigeria," join Nigerian e-commerce Facebook groups, or use Shopify-specific tools like Koala Inspector (though those are more expensive and less Nigeria-focused). The lead list approach saves you weeks of manual research.

Q: What if I don't have previous Shopify clients to show as proof?

A: Start with your first client at a lower rate (₦50,000–80,000) but clearly state in your proposal: "I'm building my portfolio in the e-commerce space and offering this introductory rate in exchange for permission to use your results as a case study." Once you deliver results, you document everything—revenue increase, email open rates, conversion rate improvement—and use that in your next pitch. Your second client will be much easier to close because you now have real numbers to show.

Q: Should I send cold emails or cold calls first?

A: Email first. Cold calls in Nigeria often come across as aggressive and rude, especially to busy store owners. Email is professional and gives them time to read and respond. However, include your WhatsApp or phone number in the email signature. If someone replies via WhatsApp instead of email, that's actually better—Nigerians respond faster on WhatsApp. After 3–4 email exchanges, a phone call becomes appropriate.

Q: How much should I charge Shopify store owners for my services?

A: It depends on the service and the store's size, but here's a baseline: if the store owner is doing ₦1M+ monthly in sales, they can afford ₦100,000–300,000 per project. For monthly retainers, charge ₦100,000–200,000/month for ongoing work. Start low on your first client (₦50,000–80,000) to build proof, then scale rates as you gain case studies. Never undervalue yourself—low prices signal low quality, and Shopify owners respect freelancers who charge professionally.
