How to Pitch Bulk SMS Services to Nigerian Businesses: A Cold Email Strategy That Works

16 August 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

If you're a freelancer in Nigeria looking to escape the Upwork grind and make serious money, bulk SMS services are one of the easiest high-ticket items to sell to local businesses. Here's why: most Nigerian businesses still rely heavily on SMS for customer communication—bank alerts, payment reminders, promotional messages—and they're willing to pay ₦30,000 to ₦150,000+ monthly for a reliable SMS provider.

The problem isn't demand. It's finding the right businesses to pitch to. That's where a cold email strategy with targeted leads comes in. This guide walks you through exactly how to pitch bulk SMS services to Nigerian businesses, from sourcing leads to closing your first deal.

Why Bulk SMS Services Are a Goldmine for Nigerian Freelancers

Before we get into the pitch strategy, let's talk numbers.

A typical bulk SMS reseller in Nigeria can:

  • Charge ₦50,000–₦150,000/month per business client
  • Work with 10–15 clients and earn ₦500,000–₦2,250,000 monthly (₦1,600–₦7,200 USD at current rates)
  • Spend 2–4 hours per month managing each account (sending messages, tracking stats, billing)
  • Scale to 30+ clients and hit ₦1.5M–₦4.5M monthly within 12 months

Even better: SMS services require minimal upfront investment. You source a bulk SMS provider (Termii, Seun SMS, SMSGh, or international APIs), negotiate wholesale rates, and resell at markup. Gross margins are 40–60%.

The bottleneck is always the same: How do I find businesses that actually need SMS services?

That's exactly what makes this different from Upwork. On Upwork, you compete on price with thousands of other freelancers. In cold email, you're directly approaching businesses that fit your ideal customer profile—restaurants, schools, banks, e-commerce stores, logistics companies—and offering them a service that solves a real problem they have today.

Step 2: Craft a Cold Email That Gets Opens and Replies

Your cold email has one job: make the business owner realize they're losing money by NOT using SMS services.

Here's the formula:

Structure of a High-Converting SMS Services Pitch Email

  1. Subject line (curiosity + specificity): Mention their business type or a common problem
  2. Opening (personalization + relevance): Show you know their business
  3. Problem statement (pain point): Remind them of a real cost or missed opportunity
  4. Proof (credibility + numbers): Share a relevant result or case study
  5. Call to action (low friction): Ask for a quick call, not a 2-hour meeting

Example Email Template:

Subject: Quick question—are you using SMS for [business type] notifications?

Hi [FirstName],

I noticed [BusinessName] is in the [niche] space. Most businesses in your industry are losing revenue because their customers don't get timely updates—payment confirmations, booking reminders, order status.

We work with [X] businesses across Lagos, Abuja, and PH sending 50,000+ SMS monthly. The result: customers remember them, show up on time, and spend more.

A typical client sees 15-20% higher repeat bookings within the first month.

Would you be open to a quick 10-minute call to see if this makes sense for [BusinessName]?

Cheers,
[YourName]
[Your Phone]

Why this works:

  • Subject lines with their business type get 30–40% higher open rates
  • Mentioning a specific result (15-20% higher repeat bookings) is more credible than vague benefits
  • Asking for 10 minutes, not a full meeting, gets 3x more "yes" responses
  • Including your phone number signals you're a real person, not an automated bot

Pro tip: Personalize the opening based on what you can find about their business. If they're a hotel, mention "we help hotels reduce no-shows." If they're a school, mention "we help schools improve parent communication." This single detail doubles your reply rate.

From 500 prospects, that's 5–15 qualified conversations and 5–15 new clients at ₦50,000–₦100,000/month each.

Step 4: Handle Objections and Close the Sale

When you get a reply, don't immediately pitch. Ask questions first.

Common objections and how to handle them:

  • "We already use SMS services." Ask: "Which provider? How much are you paying monthly? Are you happy with their pricing and support?" Often, they're overpaying or unhappy.
  • "We don't have budget right now." Reply: "No problem. I can send you a pricing sheet. When would be a better time—end of quarter?" Keep them warm for follow-up.
  • "How is this different from [competitor]?" Your advantage: personalized local support, faster integration, flexible billing. You're not a faceless company.
  • "I need to talk to my team." Say: "Happy to. Let me send you a 2-minute video showing how it works, then you can review with your team."

Closing the deal:

Once they're interested, don't send them a 10-page proposal. Instead:

  1. Schedule a 15-minute call (Zoom or WhatsApp)
  2. During the call, demo a simple SMS message being sent
  3. Ask: "If I could get you set up by [date], would you be ready to start?"
  4. If yes, send an invoice same day. Invoice via Wave (wave.com) or your bank's online billing
  5. Once payment clears to your Wise or Payoneer account, activate their service within 24 hours

Typical contract: ₦50,000–₦100,000 first month, then monthly. Payment upfront via bank transfer or Payoneer.

Avoiding Common Mistakes

  • Pitching before understanding their pain: Don't send a generic email. Know if they're a hotel, school, or e-commerce store, and tailor your message.
  • Asking for too much on the first email: Don't ask for a meeting. Ask for a 10-minute call. Don't explain all features—mention one benefit.
  • Using free Gmail for cold email: Providers throttle bulk sends from @gmail.com. Use a custom domain or business email.
  • Not following up: 80% of deals close after the 3rd or 4th touch. Send follow-up emails on day 3 and day 7 if they don't reply.
  • Underpricing: Don't charge ₦10,000/month. You're not competing on price; you're providing reliability and local support. Charge ₦50,000–₦100,000.
