Freelance Contract Template for Nigeria: Protect Your International Deals

August 3, 2026 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

You just landed a ₦500,000 project from a UK design agency. Excitement kicks in—until you realise you have no contract. No written agreement on payment terms. No clause about what happens if they ghost you mid-project. No protection if they use your work without paying.

This is where most Nigerian freelancers slip up. A freelance contract template for Nigeria and international clients isn't just legal paperwork—it's your financial safety net. In a market where exchange rates fluctuate daily (1 USD = ₦1,500–₦1,800 depending on the day), where payment delays can destroy your monthly targets, and where international disputes are hard to resolve, a solid contract is non-negotiable.

This guide walks you through exactly what you need in a freelance contract, provides you with a usable template, and shows you how to adapt it for different client scenarios.

Why Nigerian Freelancers Need Contracts (The Real Stakes)

Let me be blunt: most Nigerian freelancers work without contracts. They send a proposal on Upwork, get hired, and start work. If things go south—scope creep, unpaid invoices, stolen work—they have zero recourse.

Here's what happens in practice:

  • Payment disputes: A US client owes you $800 USD. They claim "the work wasn't to standard." Without a contract specifying acceptance criteria and payment terms, you're arguing with someone in a different timezone with no legal leverage.
  • Scope creep: You agreed to "3 rounds of revisions." By round 5, you're still working, unpaid. A contract sets hard boundaries.
  • Intellectual property theft: You design a logo. The client uses it but refuses to pay the final invoice. A contract should specify that you retain rights until payment clears.
  • Currency risk: You quoted ₦500,000 in January when the rate was ₦1,500/USD. By March, the naira has weakened to ₦1,800/USD. A contract locks in the payment amount and timeline.
  • Tax liability: If you work with international clients, especially US or UK ones, they may request your tax ID. A contract clarifies who's responsible for tax withholding.

Real example: A Nigerian video editor completed a ₦300,000 project for a Canadian marketing firm. No contract. The client claimed the final video didn't meet specs and refused payment. The editor had no proof of the agreed deliverables. By the time they argued via email for 6 weeks, they'd wasted 15 hours chasing the payment. A one-page contract would have prevented this entirely.

Key Clauses Every Nigerian Freelance Contract Needs

A freelance contract doesn't need to be 20 pages long. In fact, Nigerian clients and international clients both appreciate clarity and brevity. Here are the critical sections:

1. Parties & Scope of Work

State clearly:

  • Your name and business (e.g., "John Okonkwo, trading as JO Digital Services")
  • Client name and company
  • What you're delivering (e.g., "Design 5 social media graphics in Figma format, 1080x1080px each")
  • Deliverables list (don't say "design work"—say exactly what that means)
  • Timeline (e.g., "Final files due September 15, 2026")

2. Payment Terms

This is where Nigerian freelancers often fail. You must specify:

  • Total project fee: ₦500,000 or $3,200 USD (specify which currency will be used for payment)
  • Payment schedule: "50% upfront, 50% on delivery" or "30% deposit, 40% on draft approval, 30% on final delivery"
  • Payment method: Bank transfer via Wise, Payoneer, Grey (which account name should be used)
  • Due date: "Payment due within 5 business days of invoice"
  • Late payment penalty: "Late payments accrue 2% interest per month" (optional but protects you)
  • Currency and rate: If quoting in USD but client pays in NGN, lock in the exchange rate. For example: "USD quoted at ₦1,650 per USD on August 3, 2026. Any rate variation is client's responsibility." This protects you from the naira weakening mid-project.

3. Intellectual Property & Rights

Spell out who owns what:

  • "All deliverables become client's property upon full payment."
  • "Freelancer retains the right to use the work in portfolio until client requests removal."
  • "Client may not resell or relicense the work without written permission."

This is critical. If you design a website, the client owns the final site. But you own the design system or template if they're not paying for full custom work.

4. Revisions & Acceptance

Define limits:

  • "Project includes 2 rounds of revisions. Additional revisions are ₦25,000 each."
  • "Revision requests must be submitted within 5 days of first draft delivery."
  • "Work is considered accepted 7 days after delivery if no revision requests are submitted."

5. Termination & Cancellation

What if the client cancels mid-project?

  • "If client cancels before 50% completion, client pays 50% of project fee."
  • "If client cancels after 50% completion, client pays 80% of project fee."
  • "Cancellation must be submitted in writing via email."

6. Dispute Resolution

For international clients, this matters:

  • "Disputes will first be resolved through good-faith negotiation over 14 days."
  • "If unresolved, disputes are subject to binding arbitration under [your preferred jurisdiction—many Nigerian freelancers use Lagos or London]."
  • "Client and Freelancer agree not to pursue legal action without attempting arbitration first."

7. Confidentiality & NDA

If you're working on sensitive projects (e.g., a startup's new product launch, a client's unreleased course), include:

  • "Freelancer agrees not to disclose any non-public information about the project, client, or deliverables without written consent."
  • "This obligation survives termination for 12 months."

Adapting the Template for Nigerian vs. International Clients

A contract for a Lagos-based business owner looks different from one for a US SaaS company.

For Local Nigerian Clients

  • Payment method: Bank transfer to your Nigerian bank account or Payoneer
  • Currency: Naira (₦)
  • Timeline: Often longer—expect 7–14 day payment delays as standard
  • Dispute resolution: Reference Lagos State courts or arbitration bodies if needed
  • Tone: Slightly more informal; local clients appreciate personal relationships over rigid legalese

For International Clients (US, UK, Canada, EU)

  • Payment method: Wise, Payoneer, or international bank transfer
  • Currency: Often USD or GBP—lock in your exchange rate upfront
  • Timeline: Typically faster (3–7 days payment terms are standard)
  • Dispute resolution: Arbitration clause is critical; reference a neutral jurisdiction
  • Tax: Many US clients will ask for your tax ID (NIN or business registration). You may need to complete a W-8BEN form. State in the contract: "Client is responsible for determining and withholding any required taxes."
  • Tone: Formal, detailed, specific—international clients expect professionalism and clarity

Pro tip: When quoting international clients, always include "This quote is valid for 14 days" and "Price is in USD at an exchange rate of ₦1,650 per USD, locked as of [date]." This prevents the naira from weakening and destroying your margin.

A Simple Freelance Contract Template You Can Use Today

Here's a template you can copy, customise, and use immediately. It's designed for Nigerian freelancers working with international clients:

FREELANCE SERVICE AGREEMENT

This Agreement is entered into as of [DATE] between:

FREELANCER:
Name: [Your Full Name]
Business Name: [If applicable]
Address: [Your Address, Lagos/Abuja/etc.]
Email: [Your Email]
Phone: [Your Phone]
NIN/Tax ID: [If applicable]

CLIENT:
Name/Company: [Client Name]
Address: [Client Address]
Email: [Client Email]
Contact Person: [Name]

1. SCOPE OF WORK
The Freelancer agrees to deliver the following services:
[Detailed description of deliverables]

Timeline: Work will be completed by [DATE].
Deliverables: [List specific files, formats, number of revisions]

2. PAYMENT TERMS
Total Project Fee: [Amount in USD or NGN]
Payment Schedule: 
  - [X]% due upon signing this agreement
  - [X]% due upon delivery of drafts
  - [X]% due upon final delivery

Payment Method: [Wise/Payoneer/Bank Transfer]
Payment Deadline: Within [5–7] business days of invoice date
Currency: All amounts in [USD/NGN]. If quoted in USD, rate is locked at ₦[RATE] per USD as of [DATE].

Late Payment: Overdue invoices accrue 2% interest per month.

3. INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY
Upon receipt of full payment, all deliverables become the property of the Client. The Freelancer retains the right to:
  - Use the completed work in portfolio/case studies (with Client permission)
  - Display the work on personal website or LinkedIn

The Client may not resell, redistribute, or relicense the work without written agreement.

4. REVISIONS
This project includes [NUMBER] rounds of revisions. Additional revision requests are billed at ₦[AMOUNT] each or $[AMOUNT] USD each.

Revision requests must be submitted within [5] days of draft delivery.

5. ACCEPTANCE & DELIVERY
Work is considered "accepted" 7 days after delivery if no revision requests are submitted in writing.

Final files will be delivered in: [Specify formats: PDF, Figma, Photoshop, etc.]

6. TERMINATION
If Client terminates the project:
  - Before 50% completion: Client pays 50% of total fee
  - After 50% completion: Client pays 80% of total fee
  - Termination must be submitted in writing via email

If Freelancer cannot complete due to force majeure (illness, internet outage, etc.), Client retains rights to completed work, or can request a refund.

7. CONFIDENTIALITY
The Freelancer agrees not to disclose any information about this project, Client, or deliverables without written consent. This obligation survives for 12 months after project completion.

8. DISPUTE RESOLUTION
Any disputes will first be resolved through good-faith negotiation within 14 days. If unresolved, disputes will be handled through binding arbitration [in Lagos/London/neutral jurisdiction].

9. PAYMENT METHOD DETAILS
Exchange Rate Lock: If paying in NGN for a USD project, the exchange rate is locked at ₦[RATE] as of [DATE]. Any variation in exchange rate is the Client's responsibility.

Bank Details:
[Your Bank Name]
Account Number: [Your Account Number]
Account Name: [Your Full Name]

Alternative: [Wise/Payoneer Details]

10. SIGNATURES
By signing below, both parties agree to this contract.

Freelancer: __________________ Date: __________

Client: __________________ Date: __________

Currency Risk & Payment Protection for Nigerian Freelancers

Here's a practical reality: the naira is volatile. In the past 2 years, it's moved from ₦410/USD to ₦1,800+/USD. If you quote a project in USD but the client delays payment, you lose money as the exchange rate weakens.

Your contract must protect you here. Include:

  • Exchange rate lock: "Quote valid at ₦1,650/USD as of August 1, 2026. If Client delays payment beyond [DATE], Freelancer may adjust invoice to current market rate."
  • Payment currency:** "Payment in USD via Wise to minimise forex fees." (Wise takes ~1% vs. Nigerian bank transfers which can take 2–5%)
  • Deposit requirement: "50% deposit due upon signing to lock in exchange rate."

What About Liability & Insurance?

For design, copywriting, or video work, you probably don't need liability insurance. But include a clause like:

"Freelancer's total liability under this agreement is limited to the project fee paid. Freelancer is not liable for indirect, consequential, or punitive damages."

This protects you if a client claims your work caused them financial loss. (They can't sue you for more than they paid you.)

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Do I need a lawyer in Nigeria to draw up a contract?

A: Not necessarily. The template provided above is legally valid for freelance work between Nigerian and international clients. However, if you're signing contracts worth over ₦5 million or dealing with complex IP issues, consulting a Lagos-based lawyer is worth the ₦50,000–₦100,000 fee. For routine projects (₦500k–₦2m), the template is sufficient. Many international clients will have their own contract anyway—you can negotiate terms before signing theirs.

Q: What if the client refuses to sign a contract?

A: Walk away. Seriously. If a client won't commit to a written agreement, they're either disorganised or hiding something. Both are red flags. The best clients—those who pay on time and value your work—will always sign. If they say "contracts are too formal," send them a simplified one-page version. If they still refuse, that's a sign they're not a reliable client.

Q: Should I include a non-compete clause?

A: For freelance work, non-competes are usually overkill. You want the freedom to work with other clients. However, if you're working on something truly proprietary (a client's unreleased product, exclusive strategy), you can include: "Freelancer agrees not to do similar work for Client's direct competitors for [3–6] months after project completion." Keep it narrow and time-limited.

Q: How do I handle revisions if the client keeps asking for more rounds?

A: Your contract should say "2 rounds of revisions included; additional revisions billed at ₦25,000 each." When they ask for round 3, send an invoice for ₦25,000 before you start. This stops scope creep immediately. Some clients will push back, but once you bill once, they'll respect the limit. Frame it professionally: "Happy to do more rounds—they're just billed separately so my time is accounted for."
