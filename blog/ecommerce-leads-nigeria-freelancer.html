Why Cold Email Beats Upwork for Nigerian Freelancers

Let's be honest: Upwork and Fiverr are saturated. A ₦20,000 logo design job attracts 80 bids. Clients choose the cheapest option or the person with the best reviews (which you don't have yet). You spend 3 hours writing proposals. You earn nothing.

Cold email is the opposite:

  • Lower competition. Most freelancers don't cold email. The inbox space you're competing for is way smaller.
  • Better rates. When you email a business directly, they've already decided they need the service. They're not shopping for price; they're looking for someone who can deliver. You can charge 2–3x your Upwork rate.
  • Repeat clients. Once you land one ecommerce client through cold email, they often hire you for follow-up work. One client can be ₦150,000–₦500,000 in annual revenue.
  • Your terms. You're not bound by platform rules, escrow delays, or fees. You and the client negotiate and work directly.

The catch? You need to find the right businesses to email. That's where targeted lead lists come in.

Building Your Cold Email Campaign

Step 1: Segment Your Lead List

Don't send the same email to a fashion brand, an electronics seller, and a book retailer. The first mistake most freelancers make is generic outreach.

Before you send anything, segment your leads by:

  • Industry. Fashion, electronics, groceries, beauty, books, etc.
  • Business size. Solo sellers vs. teams with multiple staff (check their about pages and LinkedIn).
  • Platform. Shopify stores, custom sites, Instagram shops, Facebook shops. Each has different pain points.

A 5-minute sort now saves you 50% of wasted emails later.

Step 2: Write Personalized Subject Lines & Opening Lines

Subject line examples that actually work:

  • "Quick idea for [Business Name]'s product photography" (if they have weak product images)
  • "We helped [Competitor Name] get 300+ more monthly orders" (if you know their competitor)

Opening line: Don't say "Hi, I'm a freelancer." Say something specific about their business:

"I noticed your Shopify store sells women's fashion and gets most traffic from Instagram. I help stores like yours set up automated email sequences that bring past customers back. Typically, this adds 20–30% to repeat order revenue."

That's 3 sentences. Specific. Relevant. Shows you've done homework.

Step 3: Pitch Your Service, Not Your Resume

Structure your email:

  1. Problem statement. "Most ecommerce stores lose 70% of visitors without converting them."
  2. Your solution. "I design high-converting product pages and set up email funnels."
  3. Proof. "In the last 6 months, I've helped 3 stores increase their average order value by 15–25%."
  4. Call to action. "I have availability for 2 new projects this month. Want to hop on a 15-min call to see if this fits?"

Keep it under 150 words. Mobile-friendly. No fancy formatting.

Common Mistakes Nigerian Freelancers Make (And How to Avoid Them)

Mistake 1: Sending Too Generic Emails

Wrong: "Hi, I'm a freelancer offering web design services. Contact me for a quote."

Right: "I noticed your store's homepage loads slow and your product pages don't show customer reviews. Both hurt conversion. I can fix both in 2 weeks. Want to see the impact?"

Spend 1 minute per email on personalization. It doubles your response rate.

Mistake 2: Not Following Up

Most people don't reply to the first email. They're busy. Your email was in their inbox along with 50 others. Send a follow-up after 3 days, and a second follow-up after 7 days if they still don't reply.

Zaram Web Mailer handles this automatically. You set it once, it follows up for you.

Mistake 3: Pitching Too Early

Don't ask for money on your first email. Ask for a 15-minute conversation. On the call, you understand their pain, ask questions, and then pitch.

Email = invitation to a conversation, not a sales pitch.

Mistake 4: Not Testing Your Emails

Send your first 50 emails to a small segment. Wait a week. Check open rates and click rates. If open rate is below 20%, your subject line sucks. Rewrite it. Test again with the next 50.

Data beats guesswork.

Mistake 5: Targeting the Wrong Businesses

Don't email a business that's obviously struggling (bad site, no traffic, no social presence). Email businesses that are already successful and can afford to hire you. A store doing ₦2M/month in sales has budget. A store doing ₦100K/month doesn't.

This is why a filtered lead list from Freelance LeadsHub matters. You're not wasting time on unqualified leads.

Building a Portfolio for Cold Email Success

If you're brand new, you don't have case studies yet. Here's how to fix that fast:

  1. Do 2–3 paid projects at a discount (₦40,000–₦60,000 instead of ₦150,000). Ask for permission to use them as portfolio pieces and testimonials.
  2. Build 2–3 spec projects. Design a fictional ecommerce site, an email funnel, or a product page. It doesn't have to be live, just visually impressive.
  3. Collect testimonials from friends/previous clients. "Working with [your name] increased my sales by 18%" is gold.
  4. Put everything on a simple portfolio site. Don't need fancy—just clean and professional. Carrd.co works great.

With even one real testimonial + 2 spec pieces, you can start cold emailing. You'll be surprised how many people hire you based on that.

Staying Consistent: The Real Challenge

Cold email isn't hard. It's boring. You'll send 500 emails and get 2 responses. You'll wonder if it's working. You'll think about going back to Upwork.

Don't.

The people who succeed with cold email are the ones who:

  • Send at least 100 emails per week (2 hours of work)
  • Stick with it for 8–12 weeks before judging results
  • Follow up religiously (70% of deals come from follow-ups, not first emails)
  • Track metrics and improve their template based on data

If you do this, by week 8, you should have 2–3 active clients. By week 12, you're doing ₦300,000+/month.

If you send 10 emails and quit because you got no response, you'll never know what could have happened.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is cold email legal in Nigeria? Will I get in trouble?

A: Yes, cold email is completely legal in Nigeria. You're simply sending a professional business inquiry. As long as you're not spamming (sending to irrelevant people), using misleading subject lines, or being abusive, there's no legal issue. Cold email is how professional B2B outreach works globally. That said, if someone tells you to stop emailing them, respect that and remove them from your list.

Q: How long before I make my first ₦100,000 from cold email?

A: If you start today and send consistent, high-quality emails, expect your first client within 4–6 weeks. That client might pay ₦100,000–₦250,000 for the first project. So realistically, 4–8 weeks. The key is consistency. Two people sending 100 emails each per week will see results. One person sending 50 emails per week might take 10–12 weeks because they're hitting fewer leads.

Q: Do I need a professional website to cold email?

A: Not required, but strongly recommended. A simple 3-page portfolio site on Carrd or Wix takes 2 hours and costs ₦0–₦3,000. When someone clicks the link in your email and sees your work, you're 10x more likely to close. If you don't have a website, at least have a professional Gmail address and a Behance or LinkedIn profile they can visit.

Q: What if I get rejected or no response? How do I stay motivated?

A: Rejection is part of cold email. A 2–4% response rate is actually good. That means 96–98 people aren't interested. That's okay. They're not your market. Focus on the 2–4 who are. Track your numbers: "I sent 200 emails, got 6 replies, closed 1 deal for ₦200,000." That's a win. Celebrate it. Next batch, aim for 250 emails and 1.5 deals. The compounding effect is real.
