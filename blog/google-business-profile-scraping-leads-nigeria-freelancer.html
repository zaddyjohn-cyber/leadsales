What Is Google Business Profile Scraping?

Google Business Profiles are the listings that show up when someone searches "plumber near me" or "hair salon Lagos." Every business on Google Maps has a profile with:

  • Business name
  • Phone number
  • Email address (if listed)
  • Website URL
  • Address and hours
  • Customer reviews and ratings

Scraping means extracting this data automatically using tools or scripts. Instead of manually clicking through 500 businesses on Google Maps to copy their contact info, you use a tool to pull the data in bulk.

For a Nigerian freelancer, this is gold. Why? Because these are real businesses with revenue, decision-makers, and actual problems you can solve. A restaurant owner who's active on Google Maps has a business. They likely need help with their website, social media, or online visibility. They're not on Upwork competing with 10,000 other freelancers.

How Google Business Profile Scraping Works (Step-by-Step)

Method 1: Manual Scraping (Free, but Time-Intensive)

This is where most beginners start:

  1. Go to Google Maps
  2. Search for your target industry in a specific location (e.g., "web design services Lagos," "digital marketing agencies Abuja")
  3. Click each business listing
  4. Copy the name, phone, email (if visible), and website
  5. Paste into a Google Sheet or Excel file
  6. Repeat 100+ times

Reality check: This takes 4–6 hours per 100 leads. If you're targeting 500 businesses, you're looking at 20–30 hours of clicking and copying. That's two or three working days just gathering contact info.

Manual scraping makes sense if you only need 50 leads and you have time. Beyond that, it's inefficient.

Method 2: Using Scraping Tools (Semi-Automated)

Tools like Bright Data, ScrapingBee, or local alternatives can automate this. You:

  1. Set parameters (industry, location, rating threshold)
  2. Run the scraper
  3. Get a CSV file with 500–5,000 leads in 10 minutes

Cost: ₦15,000–₦80,000 per month depending on volume and tool.

Problem: Google actively blocks scrapers. Your IP might get flagged. Ethical concerns exist around scraping Google's data without permission.

Converting Google Business Profile Leads into Clients

Having leads is one thing. Converting them is another. Here's the practical process:

Step 1: Segment Your Leads

Not all leads are equal. Before you pitch, categorize them:

  • By rating: Businesses with 4+ stars often have cash flow and care about their image. Pitch these first.
  • By industry: If you're a web designer, focus on e-commerce stores, agencies, and service businesses (not individuals).
  • By location: Start with your city or state. Easier logistics, better timezone alignment.
  • By profile completeness: Businesses with detailed profiles, multiple photos, and recent posts are actively managed. More likely to respond.

Step 2: Craft Your Cold Email

This is critical. Your email is competing for 3 seconds of attention in a busy inbox.

Formula that works in Nigeria:

  1. Personalized opener (1 line): Reference something specific about their business. "I noticed your new product line on Instagram" or "Your reviews mention slow website load times."
  2. Problem statement (2 lines): Name the pain point. "Most e-commerce stores in Lagos lose 40% of sales due to slow checkout."
  3. Your solution (1–2 lines): What you offer. "I help stores like yours cut load time in half and boost conversions."
  4. Proof (1 line): A case study, metric, or credential. "I redesigned [business name]'s site and they saw a 60% increase in orders."
  5. Call-to-action (1 line): Keep it small. "Would a 15-minute call this week work?"

Total: 30–40 words. That's it.

Once you have your lead list and your email template, use Zaram Web Mailer to send bulk cold emails. It handles personalization, follow-ups, and avoids spam filters—critical for Nigeria where email deliverability can be tricky. You can track opens and replies in one dashboard.

Step 3: Follow-Up Sequences

Most people don't reply to the first email. That's normal. Your follow-up matters more than your initial pitch.

Recommended sequence:

  1. Email 1 (Day 0): Your main pitch
  2. Email 2 (Day 3): "Just following up—did you see my previous message?"
  3. Email 3 (Day 7): New angle: "One thing I didn't mention..."
  4. Email 4 (Day 14): Social proof angle: "We just helped [similar business]..."
  5. Stop after 4. If they haven't responded by day 14, they're not interested.

Zaram Web Mailer automates this. You set it up once, and it sends the sequence automatically while you focus on closing deals with people who do reply.

Step 4: Response Management

When someone replies—and they will—be ready:

  • Reply within 2 hours. Speed kills. Reply faster than they expect.
  • Schedule a call. Don't negotiate price over email. A 15-minute call converts better.
  • Ask qualifying questions: "What's your current website costing you in lost sales?" or "How much are you spending on ads per month?"
  • Quote based on value, not hours. If they'll make ₦500,000 extra per month with your help, charging ₦50,000 is reasonable. A flat rate per project beats hourly in Nigeria—clients expect it, and you earn more.

Common Questions Nigerian Freelancers Ask

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is scraping Google Business Profiles legal in Nigeria?

A: Scraping Google's data directly violates their Terms of Service. However, buying pre-built lead lists from legitimate sources like Freelance LeadsHub is perfectly legal—those companies have already extracted and verified the data. Many Nigerian businesses operate this way. If you're extracting data yourself via scripts, you're in a gray area. To stay safe and efficient, use verified lead sources instead. You save time, avoid legal headaches, and get better data.

Q: How do I know if leads from a list are still active/valid?

A: Good lead sources (like Freelance LeadsHub) verify data regularly. However, phone numbers and emails do change. Your first email acts as a validation. If 10% of emails bounce, that's normal. If 50% bounce, the source is bad. When you get responses, you know you have good leads. Also, check Google Maps directly for high-rated, recently reviewed businesses—they're almost always active.

Q: What if businesses report my emails as spam?

A: This is why your email copy matters. Personalized, relevant emails rarely get marked as spam. Spammy emails ("You need SEO!" sent to 10,000 people) get flagged. Use Zaram Web Mailer which includes spam-check tools and warming features—it gradually ramps up volume so email providers don't flag you. Also: never buy lists of "emails" from sketchy vendors. Stick to real businesses extracted from real sources.

Q: Should I do this full-time or alongside Upwork?

A: Start alongside Upwork. Spend 5 hours/week on outreach, keep your Upwork going. By month 2, you'll have 1–2 clients. By month 3–4, you'll have 3–4. At that point, reduce Upwork to just retain existing clients, and focus fully on outreach. This is the low-risk path. Don't quit your job on day 1 of cold email. Give it 90 days while running both channels.
