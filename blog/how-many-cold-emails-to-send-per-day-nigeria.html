How Many Cold Emails Should a Freelancer Send Per Day in Nigeria? A Data-Driven Guide

2026-08-07 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

If you're a freelancer in Nigeria, you've probably asked yourself this question: How many cold emails should I send per day to actually land paying clients?

The answer isn't just a number. It depends on your niche, your email quality, your follow-up strategy, and whether you're sending 10 generic pitches or 10 highly targeted, personalized emails to decision-makers who actually need your services.

Here's what most Nigerian freelancers get wrong: they treat cold email volume like Upwork proposals. They blast 50 emails a day, get no responses, burn out, and go back to competing on platforms where clients haggle you down to ₦2,000 per project.

This guide gives you the real numbers—based on actual response rates, conversion data, and what works specifically for Nigerian freelancers sending cold emails to local and international businesses.

The Short Answer: 15-30 Cold Emails Per Day (If Done Right)

If you're sending highly targeted, personalized cold emails to businesses that actually fit your service, you should aim for 15-30 emails per day maximum.

Why not 100? Because:

  • Quality beats volume. A 5% response rate from 20 personalized emails = 1 qualified lead. A 0.5% response rate from 100 generic emails = also 1 lead, but you wasted 80 emails and your sender reputation.
  • Email providers flag spam. Gmail, Outlook, and Nigerian ISPs (especially if you're using local bank email domains) have stricter filters now. Sending 100+ emails daily trains algorithms to trash your future emails.
  • Your time is worth money. At ₦3,000–₦10,000 per hour (realistic for Nigerian freelancers), spending 6 hours personalizing and sending 100 emails costs more than it generates. 20 focused emails take 2-3 hours and convert better.

The Real Metric That Matters: Not emails sent, but qualified responses per week. If you send 100 cold emails weekly and get 2-3 genuine inquiries, that's a 2-3% response rate. That's healthy. If you send 200 and get 2 responses, you're wasting time. Aim for 2-4 qualified conversations per week from cold email.

Breaking Down the Numbers: Response Rates by Email Quality

Generic Cold Email (Blast and Pray)

This is the "Hi [FirstName], I'm a designer, check out my portfolio" email sent to 100 random businesses.

  • Expected response rate: 0.2–0.5%
  • Conversion to paid work: 5–10% of responses
  • Real math: Send 100 emails → 0-1 responses → 0 projects
  • Time invested: 4-5 hours (if you're just copying and pasting)
  • Income per week: ₦0–₦5,000

Moderately Personalized Cold Email (Research the Prospect)

This includes the company name, a specific detail about their business or website, and a one-sentence reason why you're reaching out.

  • Expected response rate: 2–4%
  • Conversion to paid work: 15–25% of responses
  • Real math: Send 20 emails → 0.4-0.8 responses → 1-2 leads per week
  • Time invested: 2-3 hours (spend 5-10 minutes per prospect researching)
  • Average project value (Nigeria): $150–$500 USD (₦247,500–₦825,000 at 1,650 rate) for design, copywriting, web dev, or VA work
  • Income per week: ₦100,000–₦400,000+ (if one-third convert)

Highly Personalized Cold Email (Warm Angle + Specific Value Prop)

You mention their recent blog post, a problem you noticed on their website, or a mutual connection. You propose a specific outcome, not a service.

  • Expected response rate: 5–8%
  • Conversion to paid work: 30–50% of responses
  • Real math: Send 15 emails → 0.75-1.2 responses → 0.25-0.6 projects per week
  • Time invested: 3-4 hours (spend 10-15 minutes deeply personalizing each email)
  • Average project value: $300–$1,000 USD (₦495,000–₦1,650,000) because better prospects = bigger budgets
  • Income per week: ₦150,000–₦800,000+

Notice the pattern? Fewer emails, better research, higher income. That's the real game.

Critical Factors That Change Your Ideal Email Volume

1. Your Niche

If you're a web developer targeting e-commerce stores in Lagos, you should send 20-25 highly personalized emails weekly. If you're a content writer sending the same pitch to 500 blogs, volume matters less—but personalization still wins.

2. Your Email Reputation & Warm-Up

New email accounts get flagged faster. If you're using a fresh domain or Gmail, start with 10-15 emails/day for the first 2-3 weeks to build reputation. After a month, scale to 25-30/day. This is called "email warm-up," and it's critical if you're serious about cold email.

Read our guide on how to warm up your email for cold outreach in Nigeria to avoid getting blacklisted.

3. Your Response Tracking & Follow-Up Cadence

Here's a fact most freelancers ignore: 40-50% of responses come from follow-ups, not the first email. If you send 30 emails and don't follow up, you're throwing away half your potential income.

Follow-up schedule:

  • First email: Day 0
  • First follow-up: Day 3 (short, different angle)
  • Second follow-up: Day 7 (mention a case study or social proof)
  • Final follow-up: Day 12 (softer tone, low-pressure)

This is why Zaram Web Mailer is essential—it automates follow-ups so you're not manually sending 4 emails to 30 prospects.

4. Your Service Price Point

If you're charging ₦10,000 for a logo design, you need higher volume (40-50 emails/week) because conversion rate is low and margins are tight.

If you're charging ₦200,000+ for a full web project, you need fewer emails (15-20/week) because even 1-2 clients per month hits your income target.

Common Mistakes That Kill Cold Email Response Rates

Mistake #1: Too Many Emails, Too Little Research

Sending 100 generic emails = 0-1 response. Sending 20 personalized emails = 1-2 responses. The second path pays more and takes less time.

Mistake #2: Weak Subject Lines

Subject lines like "Freelance Designer Here" or "Let's Work Together" get deleted. Test subject lines like:

  • "I noticed [specific thing] on your site"
  • "Quick idea for [company name]"
  • Personalization: "[CEO name]—[niche] question"

A/B test 2-3 subject lines per week. Measure opens. Scale what works.

Mistake #3: Sending From a Gmail or Outlook Account

Businesses distrust generic email providers. Get a branded domain email (cheapest: Namecheap + Zoho Mail at ₦2,000/month). It doubles response rates.

Mistake #4: Not Following Up

Send 30 emails, get 2-3 responses, assume the rest are uninterested. Wrong. Most are just busy. Your follow-up gets the open.

Mistake #5: Blasting Leads Without Filtering

If you target construction companies and you're a graphic designer, you're wasting emails. Use Freelance LeadsHub to download leads already filtered by niche, so you're only emailing relevant prospects.

The Data You Should Track Weekly

Don't send blind. Track these metrics:

  • Emails sent: Total per week
  • Opens: What % of recipients opened (should be 25-45% for cold email)
  • Replies: First-time responses (track positive vs. "not interested" vs. auto-replies)
  • Response rate: Replies ÷ emails sent (target: 2-5%)
  • Qualified leads: Replies with actual budget/interest (target: 50%+ of replies)
  • Projects closed: Revenue from cold email weekly
  • Email-to-revenue ratio: Total revenue ÷ emails sent = profit per email

If your response rate drops below 1% for 2 weeks, your subject lines or pitch is weak—test new angles. If responses aren't converting, your follow-up or pricing is off.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is 50 cold emails per day too much for a Nigerian freelancer?

A: Yes, if they're generic or poorly researched. Your email domain and sending pattern will get flagged by ISPs, your sender reputation tanks, and you'll end up in spam folders. If you're sending 50 highly personalized, targeted emails daily to prospects that actually need your service and you're warming up your email domain properly, it's possible—but most freelancers can't maintain that quality level. Stick to 20-30 daily emails done right instead of 50 done wrong. Quality converts; volume doesn't.

Q: How long does it take to see results from cold email in Nigeria?

A: First responses typically arrive 2-3 days after your initial email. Serious conversations and proposals come within 1-2 weeks. Actual signed contracts and first payments take 2-4 weeks depending on your prospect's decision timeline. This is why consistency matters—start cold email on Week 1 and you won't see real income until Week 4. Most freelancers quit by Week 2 because they're impatient. The ones who stick it out make ₦300,000+ monthly by Month 3.

Q: Should I send cold emails from my personal phone number or email only?

A: Always start with email—it's less intrusive and gives prospects time to warm up to your pitch. Include a phone number in your email signature (optional, don't lead with it). After 2-3 email touchpoints with no response, sending a WhatsApp or calling is acceptable, but only for high-priority prospects. Most Nigerian businesses won't mind a WhatsApp follow-up if you've already emailed them twice—just keep it professional. That said, don't cold-call or WhatsApp without an email first. It feels spammy and tanks your reputation.

Q: What's the best way to get email addresses for Nigerian businesses if I don't have a lead list?

A: Manual methods: (1) Google "[company name] contact" or check their website's Contact page, (2) Use Hunter.io or RocketReach to verify emails (both have free tiers), (3) Check LinkedIn and see if employees list company email formats, (4) Call their office and ask the receptionist politely.
