Cold Email: The Numbers You Need to Know

Average Conversion Rates

  • Open rate: 15–25% (depends on subject line quality)
  • Reply rate: 2–5% (this is actual engagement, not opens)
  • Conversion to client: 0.5–1.5% (depends on your follow-up and offer)

What does this mean in practice? If you send 100 cold emails:

  • 15–25 people open it
  • 2–5 reply to your email
  • 1–2 actually become paying clients

So to land one client via cold email, you need to send approximately 50–100 emails (assuming your subject lines and copy are decent).

Key insight: Cold email's biggest advantage is it's scalable and passive. You send it once, it sits in their inbox, and they reply when they're ready. You're not chasing them daily like you would on WhatsApp.

WhatsApp Outreach: The Reality Check

Average Conversion Rates

  • Message delivery: 95–99% (WhatsApp almost always delivers)
  • Read rate: 70–90% (people check WhatsApp constantly)
  • Reply rate: 1–3% (much lower than email, surprisingly)
  • Conversion to client: 0.2–0.8%

The counterintuitive truth: WhatsApp has higher delivery and read rates, but lower conversion rates. Why? Because messages feel personal, and most people don't expect business pitches in their DMs. They see it as spam immediately, even if it's well-written.

Time Investment

This is where WhatsApp costs you. If someone replies, you need to engage with them in real-time (or they'll think you're ignoring them). WhatsApp conversations require back-and-forth banter, answers to their objections, and constant presence. Many Nigerian freelancers spend 3–5 hours daily just managing WhatsApp conversations with prospects.

That sounds productive. It's not. You're trading your time for low conversion rates.

Cost

If you're sending WhatsApp messages manually, it's free. But if you're scaling to 500+ contacts, manual sending is impossible. WhatsApp Business API tools cost ₦10,000–₦30,000/month, and you still need leads. You're looking at the same cost as cold email, but with higher time investment and lower ROI.

The Nigeria-Specific Problem

Here's something Nigerians understand but most guides don't mention: data costs. Sending 100 WhatsApp messages with images/videos uses data. If you're on MTN or Airtel (₦3,000/GB), that adds up. Cold email uses negligible data because it's just text.

Also, WhatsApp business conversations work better with local Nigerian businesses (hair salons, event planners, printing shops), but they work poorly with international clients or larger companies. If your clients are mostly overseas (which is likely if you're freelancing), WhatsApp is a terrible channel.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Cold Email Mistakes

  • Generic subject lines. "Freelancer here" or "Hey" gets deleted. Use specific subject lines like "Quick question about your [Company] blog" or "I noticed you don't have [specific thing]."
  • Selling too hard in the first email. Don't pitch your full service list. Ask a question or offer a tiny insight first.
  • Forgetting follow-ups. Most conversions happen on the 2nd or 3rd email. If you only send one, you'll miss 60% of potential clients.
  • Not personalizing. At least mention the company name or something you noticed about them. "I saw you recently launched [product]" takes 10 seconds and doubles reply rates.

WhatsApp Mistakes

  • Sending a wall of text. WhatsApp is for quick, punchy messages. If your pitch is longer than 3 lines, use email instead.
  • Not respecting boundaries. One message is fine. If they don't reply in 3 days, sending 5 more messages makes you look desperate and guarantees they block you.
  • Interrupting at bad times. Sending WhatsApp messages at 11 PM looks unprofessional. Space them out and time them during business hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I combine cold email and WhatsApp for better results?

A: Yes, but do it strategically. Email first, wait 5–7 days for a reply. If no reply, you can send a WhatsApp as a follow-up (if you have their number) with something like: "Hi [Name], sent you an email last week about [topic]. Happy to jump on a quick call if you're interested." This "omnichannel" approach works best for high-ticket services (web development, branding) where a single client is worth ₦500,000+. For lower-ticket work, stick to one channel to save time.

Q: What's the best email subject line for Nigerian freelancers to use?

A: Avoid anything that sounds like it's from a bot. Don't use all caps, don't overuse emojis, and don't make false urgency claims. Good subject lines look like: "Quick question re: [Company name]," "I think [specific pain point] might be affecting your [metric]," or "One idea for your [service/product]." Test 3–5 subject lines on small batches to see what your audience responds to. Most Nigerian business owners respond better to straightforward, professional subject lines than gimmicky ones.
