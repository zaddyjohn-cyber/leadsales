How to Get Copywriting Clients in Nigeria: A Direct Path Beyond Upwork

2026-07-28 · 9 min read · By Freelance LeadsHub

If you're a copywriter in Nigeria, you already know the problem: Upwork is flooded. You're bidding against 200 other writers for a ₦5,000 job. Your proposals disappear into the void. You're spending hours chasing clients who don't pay.

There's a better way. How to get copywriting clients in Nigeria doesn't mean fighting for scraps on marketplaces. It means going direct to businesses that actually need your skills—and can afford to pay proper rates.

This guide shows you exactly how to build a sustainable copywriting business in Nigeria with realistic income targets (₦150,000–₦500,000/month per client), the tools that work, and the systems successful copywriters use right now.

Why Direct Outreach Beats Marketplace Grinding

Let's be honest: Upwork and Fiverr are income traps for Nigerian copywriters. Here's why:

  • Race to the bottom: A 2,000-word sales page that takes 6 hours to write gets bids from ₦3,000 to ₦10,000. You can't compete.
  • Buyer power: Clients control the terms. They can dispute, demand revisions indefinitely, or ghost you after getting the work.
  • Platform cuts: Upwork takes 5–20%, Fiverr takes 20%. Direct clients? Zero middleman.
  • No leverage: You're one of thousands. Businesses that contact you directly? You're their solution.

Compare that to direct outreach: You contact a real business (an e-commerce store, agency, SaaS startup) with a personalized pitch. They need copy written. You quote ₦80,000–₦150,000 for a sales page. They pay. No bidding wars. No dispute madness.

Realistic timeline: 2–3 months of consistent cold outreach before you land your first direct client. Then 1–2 months to build a pipeline of 3–4 retainer clients at ₦40,000–₦100,000/month each. That's ₦120,000–₦400,000/month, paid directly to your Wise, Payoneer, or Grey account—no platform fighting you.

Step 1: Build Your Copywriting Offer (Specific to Your Niche)

Before you reach out to anyone, you need clarity. "General copywriting" doesn't work. Businesses want specialists.

Pick one or two niches:

  • E-commerce: Product descriptions, sales pages, email sequences for Shopify/WooCommerce stores
  • SaaS/Tech: Landing pages, case studies, onboarding emails for software tools
  • Services: Homepage copy, sales sequences, and email funnels for agencies, consulting, fitness coaching
  • Info products: Sales pages and email launches for digital courses, ebooks, coaching programs
  • Local B2B: Website copy, proposal templates, email outreach templates for local businesses (real estate, hospitality, healthcare)

Here's why this matters: A business owner in Lagos runs a digital marketing agency. When you pitch "I write copy," they think "another writer." When you pitch "I write email sequences that increase SaaS trial signups by 25%," they listen.

Document 2–3 case studies (even if they're past clients, friends' businesses, or your own projects). Include:

  • The problem the client faced
  • The copy you wrote (project type)
  • Results (if available): "Increased email open rate from 18% to 31%" or "Generated 15 qualified leads in first month"

Step 3: Write Cold Emails That Actually Convert

Now you have leads. You need emails that get replies.

Most cold emails fail because they're generic, salesy, or self-centered ("Here's what I do"). Winners are personalized, problem-focused, and short.

Formula that works:

  1. Hook (1 line): Reference something specific about their business—their product, recent LinkedIn post, website copy, or industry trend.
  2. Problem/Insight (2–3 lines): Mention a specific copywriting problem relevant to their business. Don't say "bad copy"—be specific: "Most e-commerce sites lose 60% of checkout visitors because product descriptions don't address objections."
  3. Your angle (2 lines): One sentence about what you do. One sentence about a client result (if you have it).
  4. Soft ask (1 line): "Worth a quick call?" or "Curious if this applies to your business?"

Example email for an e-commerce business:

Hi [Name],

Saw your new summer collection on [store name]—strong product line. Most online stores in Nigeria leave 50–70% of money on the table with weak product pages and checkout emails.

I write sales copy and email sequences that convert browsers into buyers. Helped [client] increase average order value by 35% with better product descriptions and post-purchase emails.

Worth a 15-minute call to see if this applies to your store?

[Your name]

That's it. Short, specific, and focused on their problem—not your credentials.

Step 5: Qualifying Leads and Booking Calls

Not every reply is a real opportunity. Some people will reply "not interested," some will say "send your rates," and some will actually want to talk.

Quick qualification questions to ask:

  • "Are you looking to improve your copy right now, or exploring options for later?"
  • "What's your main goal—more sales, more signups, or better customer retention?"
  • "What's your budget range for this project?"

If they're interested and have budget, book a 20-minute call. Not a sales call—a discovery call. Ask:

  • What copy do they need? (Sales page, emails, website, product descriptions)
  • What's the deadline?
  • Who's the decision maker?
  • What's worked or failed in the past?

Close with: "Here's what I'd suggest..." (offer a simple next step: paid project, trial project, or retainer arrangement).

Pricing in Nigeria: Expect to charge ₦50,000–₦200,000 per project depending on scope and client size. Retainers (₦40,000–₦100,000/month for ongoing emails, landing page tweaks, etc.) are most profitable long-term.

Step 6: Systemize to Build Recurring Income

One-off projects are feast or famine. You want retainers.

How to position retainers:

After the first project, pitch: "Most businesses see a 40% drop in results 3 months after launch because the copy gets stale and doesn't adapt to market changes. I offer a monthly retainer where I maintain and optimize your copy—write new email sequences, test subject lines, refresh landing pages, and track what's working."

Price: ₦40,000–₦100,000/month depending on scope.

With 3–4 retainer clients, you're at ₦120,000–₦400,000/month—consistent, predictable income. You also get data to show future prospects ("I maintain ongoing copy for 4 e-commerce businesses averaging ₦3.2M/month in revenue").

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How many cold emails should I send per day to get copywriting clients in Nigeria?

A: Start with 20–30 per day using a cold email tool like Zaram Web Mailer. This avoids spam filters (the main killer for Gmail-to-Gmail outreach) and lets the tool handle follow-ups automatically. If you have a dedicated email domain (not Gmail), you can push to 40–50 daily. Consistency matters more than volume—20 per day for 60 days beats 500 in one week and getting blacklisted.

Q: Should I specialize in one type of copy or offer general copywriting?

A: Specialize. "I write copy for e-commerce businesses" converts 5–10x better than "I'm a copywriter." Pick one niche (e-commerce, SaaS, service businesses, or info products), build a case study or two in that space, and pitch only companies in that niche. Once you have 3–4 retainer clients, you can add a second niche if you want. Specialization = higher rates + easier selling.

Q: How much should I charge for copywriting in Nigeria?

A: Depends on scope. Sales pages: ₦80,000–₦150,000. Email sequences (5–10 emails): ₦50,000–₦100,000. Website copy refresh: ₦60,000–₦120,000. Retainers: ₦40,000–₦100,000/month. Never quote less than ₦40,000—it trains clients to undervalue your work. If a prospect says "too expensive," they're not your client. Your ideal client pays because they know better copy = more revenue.

Q: How do I get past the "no experience" problem if I'm just starting?

A: Write a case study for a friend's business, your own project, or a past employer (with permission). Document the copy you wrote and any results—even estimated results are fine ("estimated 20 additional orders per month based on industry benchmarks"). Include it in your cold email: "Just completed a sales page for a Lagos-based e-commerce store that generated 12 orders on day one." One solid case study destroys the "you're a beginner" objection.
